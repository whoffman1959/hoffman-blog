**DAY 24 – HOPE AND VICTORY**

**Scripture**

**1 Peter 3:17–22 CSB**

For it is better to suffer for doing good, if that should be God's will, than for doing evil. [18] For Christ also suffered for sins once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring you to God. He was put to death in the flesh but made alive by the Spirit, [19] in which he also went and made proclamation to the spirits in prison [20] who in the past were disobedient, when God patiently waited in the days of Noah while the ark was being prepared. In it a few-that is, eight people-were saved through water. [21] Baptism, which corresponds to this, now saves you (not as the removal of dirt from the body, but the pledge of a good conscience toward God) through the resurrection of Jesus Christ, [22] who has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God with angels, authorities, and powers subject to him.

**John 10:10–11 CSB**

A thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy. I have come so that they may have life and have it in abundance. [11] "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."

**Look to the Heavenly**

Life can feel overwhelming and bleak when we neglect the many blessings God has given us. During times of depression, simply pausing to write down those gifts often brings immediate relief and restores our perspective.

Discouragement creeps in most easily at our lowest moments. We feel the sharp sting of betrayal by a close friend, watch a marriage slowly unravel as two people drift apart, or endure rejection from a rebellious child. It hurts to be mocked or shunned for our faith, and it is deeply painful to witness once-vibrant things fall into decay. In this broken world, earthly things return to dust: people die, dreams fade, and friendships dissolve. If we dwell solely on these losses, such thoughts take root and drag our spirits into a dark spiral that impacts everyone around us.

That is precisely when it is time to turn our hearts toward gratitude and make our list of blessings. A job is only temporary, and I have the opportunity now to become who I have always longed to be in Christ. Though my health may be poor, I am blessed to rise each morning, breathe deeply, and enjoy life's simple pleasures. I can meet new people and form fresh friendships. If I am ridiculed for my faith, it simply shows I am living it. I am grateful, too, for a loyal dog whose unconditional love brightens every day. We can keep adding to this list, choosing by God's grace to focus on His gifts rather than our trials.

As Christians, we possess a hope that overcomes every dark thought: we have a Lord who loves us and came so that we might have life—both now and for eternity. Jesus suffered once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, so that we might have enduring hope and be brought safely to God. He lives, watches over us, understands our suffering, and intercedes on our behalf. We rejoice that He has removed the scales of worldly blindness from our eyes, granting us a heavenly perspective.

We approached the Lord humbly, obeying His command to be baptized—not merely as an outward washing of dirt from the body, but as a pledge of a good conscience toward God. In obedience, we look to the heavenly. Because Jesus rose from the dead, we have the sure hope and assurance of our own resurrection. He has gone into heaven and sits exalted at the right hand of the Father—our Good Shepherd—to whom all angels, authorities, and powers are subject. He loved us enough to lay down His life for the sheep; how much more will He guide us through this life and into an eternity of His goodness?

Jesus has given us the Holy Spirit to dwell in our hearts—the life-giving, healing Spirit who guides and comforts us during our time on earth. In Him, we are never alone or orphaned.

What, then, should we fear? Is the temporary loss of a job truly so devastating in light of eternity? Is a betraying friend worth carrying lifelong anger, or is it an opportunity to forgive and reflect God's love? Am I grateful for the time shared with a loved one who departed before me, knowing our ultimate hope is in the Lord? It is a simple and profound truth: grounded in the victory of Jesus Christ, we can choose to walk in His joy every day. If we do have trials, let them be for doing good—good that we can find joy in.

**Reflection Questions**

1. When life feels heavy or discouraging, how can taking time to list God’s daily blessings help shift your focus from earthly trials to heavenly hope?
2. What does it mean to you personally that Jesus is your Good Shepherd who has authority over all "angels, authorities, and powers" (1 Peter 3:22)?
3. How does viewing baptism as a "pledge of a good conscience toward God" strengthen your commitment to follow Christ through every season of life?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for the ultimate victory we have in Your Son, Jesus Christ. Lord, when dark thoughts or heavy trials threaten to overwhelm our hearts, remind us of Your many blessings and fix our eyes on what is eternal. Thank You for Jesus, our Good Shepherd, who laid down His life for us and now reigns at Your right hand in power. Fill us with Your Holy Spirit, grant us the grace to choose joy, and help us to walk in the fullness of the abundant life You have promised. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and keep our faith firm until the day we see You face to face. In the holy and victorious name of Jesus, we pray.

Amen.
