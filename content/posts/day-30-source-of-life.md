**DAY 29 – SOURCE OF LIFE**

**Scripture**

**2 Peter 1:1-4 CSB**

Simeon Peter, a servant and an apostle of Jesus Christ: To those who have received a faith equal to ours through the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ. [2] May grace and peace be multiplied to you through the knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord. [3] His divine power has given us everything required for life and godliness through the knowledge of him who called us by his own glory and goodness. [4] By these he has given us very great and precious promises, so that through them you may share in the divine nature, escaping the corruption that is in the world because of evil desire.

**Jeremiah 2:13 CSB**

For my people have committed a double evil: They have abandoned me, the fountain of living water, and dug cisterns for themselves- cracked cisterns that cannot hold water.

**Thankful Worship**

When we start the book of 2 Peter, we see a great promise. We have been given wonderful gifts. Through God's power, we have everything we need for life and godliness. Through the knowledge of Christ, we have been given precious promises and are called to live differently—to escape the corruption of the world and grow in the life God has called us to.

Peter tells us that through God's promises we may “share in the divine nature.” This does not mean that we become God. Rather, through Christ we are given a new life and are being changed to become more like Him, escaping the sinful corruption of the world.

We are to keep our focus on our Father in Heaven, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. But it is easy to start worshiping the gifts, lose sight of the Giver, and slowly drift from the life God has called us to. We forget our first love.

In Jeremiah, God's people had turned away from Him. God had delivered Israel from bondage in Egypt, provided for them in the wilderness, and brought them into the land He had promised them—a land flowing with milk and honey. Yet they abandoned the Lord, the fountain of living water, and turned to other sources for what only God could provide.

This can also happen in the life of a new believer. We come to the Lord broken and in need of help. God graciously receives us, forgives us, and begins restoring our lives. We may experience many blessings along the way. It might be being able to hold a job again, or meeting someone special to share our life with. It could be acquiring the education we always wanted, or finally getting the dream home.

We may rightly see these things as undeserved gifts from our loving Father in Heaven.

The temptation over time—as it was for the Israelites—is to wrap our lives around the gift instead of the Giver.

The Israelites abandoned God, the fountain of living water, and dug cisterns for themselves. They looked elsewhere for the security, satisfaction, and life that only God could ultimately provide. Their cisterns were broken and could not hold water.

We can judge them and say we would never do such a thing, but it can happen to us just as easily. When the job, the spouse, the home, financial security, success, or anything else takes our full attention, we can slowly lose sight of God in our lives. Something that was given to us as a blessing can become the thing we live for.

We have replaced the worship of God with the worship of something He has created.

The blessings of God can become idols when they begin to rule and direct our lives. We can replace the eternal hope we have in God with the temporary things of this world. This is so subtle that even someone who is doing ministry for the Lord can begin to treat the ministry itself as more important than our loving Creator.

How can we avoid such a thing?

Joining together with other believers in worship is one way to remember what the Lord has done in our lives. Another is to spend time in God's Word and prayer, giving thanks for what we have received. We can also share a portion of our blessings with others.

When we give, we look at our gifts with gratitude, remembering that everything we have ultimately comes from God. We can then share those gifts unselfishly with others rather than allowing them to become things we selfishly guard and live for.

In Proverbs 11:24-25, we are given this promise:

One person gives freely, yet gains more; another withholds what is right, only to become poor. A generous person will be enriched, and the one who gives a drink of water will receive water.

By giving a portion of what we have received to others, we acknowledge that our blessings are gifts from God. We did not create everything we have through our own efforts. God is the ultimate giver of every good thing, and generosity helps us remember that we are stewards of His gifts rather than owners who must cling to them.

The things of this world are temporary. As believers, our citizenship is in Heaven, and our ultimate hope is not found in anything this world can give us. We can enjoy the good gifts God gives us in this life while remembering that the greatest gift—eternal life with Him—is still before us.

The gift should always point us back to the Giver.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Is there something I have been blessed with that I am slowly making into a god in my life, instead of living for the One who gave the gift?
2. What are examples of “broken cisterns” in my life—things I have put my faith in for security, happiness, or fulfillment, only to find that they cannot truly satisfy?
3. How can I give a portion of my blessings to others?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for the gifts You have given us, especially the promised gift of Your Holy Spirit. Help us always keep our eyes toward You and Heaven, worshiping You with thankful hearts. Guard our hearts from worshiping the gift instead of the Giver. Forgive us when we have done this in the past. Help us turn our thoughts toward how we can pass the blessings You have given us on to others. Make us a people who see You as our only God and the true source of life. Fill our lives with Christian fellowship and worship, humble prayer, and the reading of Your Word, so that we will remember You, the Giver of all good things. Help us enjoy the blessings You give us without allowing those blessings to take Your place in our lives. Keep our hearts focused on You and on the eternal life You have promised through Jesus Christ. I pray these things in the name of Jesus.

Amen.
