**DAY 47 – CHILDREN OF RIGHTOUSNESS**

**Scripture**

**1 John 3:7-10 CSB**

Little children, let no one deceive you. The one who does what is right is righteous, just as he is righteous. [8] The one who commits sin is of the devil, for the devil has sinned from the beginning. The Son of God was revealed for this purpose: to destroy the devil's works. [9] Everyone who has been born of God does not sin, because his seed remains in him; he is not able to sin, because he has been born of God. [10] This is how God's children and the devil's children become obvious. Whoever does not do what is right is not of God, especially the one who does not love his brother or sister.

**John 8:12 CSB**

Jesus spoke to them again: "I am the light of the world. Anyone who follows me will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life."

**Following the Lord**

Doing good things does not make us right with God. We are made right with Him because Jesus took our sins upon Himself and paid for them through His death on the cross. Our standing with God rests on what Jesus has done for us, not on how many good things we do. He gives us new life and begins to change the way we live. Even the most selfish among us know how to give good gifts to our own children and protect them. There is good and bad in everyone, but doing good things is not the same as being made right with God. Jesus is the light of the world, and He has given us His light to guide the way we live. Through Him, we are forgiven and built up to become more like Him. As He works in our lives, the things we do here on earth should reflect the character of God’s Son.

The evil one has been sinning from the beginning, and Jesus calls him the father of lies. In John’s day, there were deceivers who claimed to have special spiritual knowledge, but their lives told a different story. How can someone claim to be guided by the Spirit of God while pursuing greed, sexual immorality, and power, speaking half-truths, and manipulating others behind the scenes? Unfortunately, we still see these things in and around the church community today. That is why we must look beyond what people claim and pay attention to how they live.

We are told that those who are born of God do not sin. God’s Spirit remains in them, and their lives should show that they are walking by the Spirit. Yes, there will be sins that need to be repented of, but there is a difference between willingly chasing after the things of the world with wild abandon and a Christian who falls short, humbly asks for forgiveness, and seeks God’s strength to overcome the sin that is in their life.

The Holy Spirit does not leave us powerless against sin. He works in us to change our desires and gives us strength to turn away from what is wrong.

A person can be beautiful to look at, have the verbal ability to sway others, and have charisma. They can even understand the Bible through years of university work and study. But knowledge and appearance are not the same as righteousness. If their lives do not reflect what is right, if they are not led by the Spirit or showing the light of Jesus in how they live, we should not be deceived by their outward appearance or knowledge.

John ends this passage by telling us that God’s children and the devil’s children become evident by what they do, especially by whether they love their brothers and sisters. We should examine how we treat our fellow believers. Are we truly concerned about their well-being, or do we simply say that we love them? Our love should not depend on a person’s appearance, position, or ability to impress us or the time they disappoint us. As God’s children, we are called to show the love and compassion that Christ has shown us, not just in words but in deeds.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Do I truly love my brothers and sisters in Christ? Does that love show in how I treat them and care for their needs?

2. Can I recognize the difference between a Christian who falls short and turns back to God in repentance and someone who willingly continues in sin while claiming to have special spiritual knowledge?

3. Am I trusting in Jesus Christ rather than my own good works to make me right with God? Is the Holy Spirit changing the way I live?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

It is our prayer that all might come to know Your Son, Jesus—both those who are considered good and those who are considered bad—because our righteousness comes from You, not from anything we have done. May we turn to Jesus as Lord of our lives and be built up by Your Holy Spirit to become more like Your Son in this world. Keep us in Your strength and guide us in what is right. Forgive us for our wrongs, and give us the strength to turn away from sin. Let the thoughts of our hearts be focused on You so that Your love is shown in the way we live and in how we care for our brothers and sisters in Christ during this short life You have given us. In Your Son’s name we pray.

Amen.
