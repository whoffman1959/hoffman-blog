**DAY 17 – FOR HIS GLORY**

**Scripture**

**1 Corinthians 10:31 (CSB)**

So, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do everything for the glory of God.

**2 Corinthians 5:15 (CSB)**

And he died for all so that those who live should no longer live for themselves, but for the one who died for them and was raised.

**Godly Focus**

As my wife and I talk about giving, we often come back to the needs right in front of us. We’ve seen towns here in Illinois — places that were once beautiful and full of life — fade into broken homes, closed businesses, addiction, and young people fleeing for something better. When you see that with your own eyes, it changes how you think about giving and how you think about your own heart.

It’s so easy, especially when we’re young, to build our lives around the same small list of desires:

I need to get my degree so I can make more money. I’m not satisfied with my car. I just need to find that soul mate to make me happy. I want that promotion. If only people would listen to me.

Around and around we go — planning, controlling, grasping — trying to hold our world together with our own hands. “I need to supply for my own needs,” we tell ourselves.

Breathe.

It’s not about me. Go back to the basics. It’s about the Father and His Son, Jesus. It’s about being guided by the Holy Spirit. The real question beneath everything is this, what does the Father want me to do?

We crave normalcy, comfort, and predictability — but that isn’t the guiding purpose of our lives. This is the Father’s world, not ours. Everything exists for His glory. We were created to know Him, to reflect His love, to live in fellowship with Him, and to reach out and bless others in His name.

When we forget this, the Father has a gentle way of slowing us down. Sometimes He lets us see the limits of our own strength. Sometimes He lets us feel the emptiness of chasing the next accomplishment. He sent His Son Jesus to redeem us from those blind spots — the places where our desires crowd out His will and our care for others.

Maybe the car that still runs is already a blessing meant to be used — like giving a neighbor a ride to church. Maybe being single offers space to serve God with a whole heart. Maybe insisting on getting my own way is the very thing keeping me from hearing His voice. Maybe the extra resources I cling to were meant to bless the widow, the orphan, the lonely, or the struggling believer.

James reminds us of this with a painfully clear example: “Go in peace, stay warm, and be well fed,” means nothing if we don’t actually help the one in need. Good intentions never replaced faithful action.

We all need to turn — daily.

Read the Word. Pray and ask for forgiveness. Stay connected to believers who strengthen your faith. Give your life away — not just your money, but your time, your kindness, your presence, your compassion.

When we shift the center of our lives from self to Savior, everything else finally finds its right place.

**Reflection Questions**

1. In what areas of my life am I most tempted to focus on myself rather than God’s glory?

2. How might God be inviting me to use what I already have — time, gifts, or resources — to serve others?

3. What would it look like this week to do even simple things “for the glory of God”?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for reminding me that life is not about my success or satisfaction, but about Your glory. Help me to turn my eyes from myself and fix them on You. Teach me to see every moment — every job, every relationship, every task — as an opportunity to honor You. May my life reflect Your goodness, and may my heart rest in the joy of serving You above all.

Amen.
