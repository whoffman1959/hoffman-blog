**DAY 74 – CITY IN CONFUSION**

**Scripture**

**Esther 3:7-15 CSB**

In the first month, the month of Nisan, in King Ahasuerus's twelfth year, the pur-that is, the lot-was cast before Haman for each day in each month, and it fell on the twelfth month, the month Adar. [8] Then Haman informed King Ahasuerus, "There is one ethnic group, scattered throughout the peoples in every province of your kingdom, keeping themselves separate. Their laws are different from everyone else's and they do not obey the king's laws. It is not in the king's best interest to tolerate them. [9] If the king approves, let an order be drawn up authorizing their destruction, and I will pay 375 tons of silver to the officials for deposit in the royal treasury." [10] The king removed his signet ring from his hand and gave it to Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews. [11] Then the king told Haman, "The money and people are given to you to do with as you see fit." [12] The royal scribes were summoned on the thirteenth day of the first month, and the order was written exactly as Haman commanded. It was intended for the royal satraps, the governors of each of the provinces, and the officials of each ethnic group and written for each province in its own script and to each ethnic group in its own language. It was written in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed with the royal signet ring. [13] Letters were sent by couriers to each of the royal provinces telling the officials to destroy, kill, and annihilate all the Jewish people-young and old, women and children-and plunder their possessions on a single day, the thirteenth day of Adar, the twelfth month. [14] A copy of the text, issued as law throughout every province, was distributed to all the peoples so that they might get ready for that day. [15] The couriers left, spurred on by royal command, and the law was issued in the fortress of Susa. The king and Haman sat down to drink, while the city of Susa was in confusion.

**Matthew 7:24-25 CSB**

"Therefore, everyone who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on the rock. [25] The rain fell, the rivers rose, and the winds blew and pounded that house. Yet it didn't collapse, because its foundation was on the rock.

**A Law Distributed**

As we look at the events taking place in the world around us, we see a world filled with confusion. Competing news outlets often paint different pictures of what is happening, and even families find themselves divided over political and social issues. Some stand on the right, others on the left, and disagreements can become so heated that relationships suffer.

Wars and conflicts threaten the peace of nations, while political unrest and violence leave people wondering what tomorrow will bring. Rising living expenses place financial pressure on families, and some who once lived comfortably now struggle to meet their basic needs. New technologies, including artificial intelligence, are advancing rapidly, bringing opportunities but also concerns about the future of employment.

With so much uncertainty, it is easy for people to become fearful of what lies ahead.

Yet Jesus gives us a promise. In Matthew chapter 7, He tells us that those who hear His words and put them into practice are like a wise man who built his house upon a solid foundation. The rain fell, the rivers rose, and the winds beat against that house, but it did not collapse because it was built upon the rock.

Jesus did not say the storms would never come. He taught us that the foundation upon which we build our lives determines whether we will stand when those storms arrive.

We know that life in this world will bring trials. There will be wars, famine, disease, natural disasters, and suffering. Jesus also spoke of such troubles as the beginning of birth pains in Matthew chapter 24, reminding His followers not to be alarmed when they hear of wars and rumors of wars. These events remind us that the world is troubled, but they do not give us permission to assume that we know the exact time of His return.

In Philippians chapter 1, Paul encourages believers to live in a manner worthy of the gospel of Jesus Christ. He tells them to stand firm in one spirit, united in their faith, and not to be frightened by those who oppose them. Later, in Philippians chapter 3, he reminds us that our citizenship is in heaven.

What a comfort this is for Christians when everything around us seems to be in turmoil. We belong to a kingdom that cannot be shaken, and our hope rests in the One who has overcome the world.

This does not mean that we will never experience hardship or suffering. Christians throughout history have faced persecution, loss, and even death because of their faith. But through Christ, we have the assurance that the troubles of this world cannot separate us from the love of God.

Our faith can also become a witness to those around us. When others see that we have hope in difficult circumstances, they may begin to wonder where that peace comes from. We have an opportunity to point them toward Jesus Christ, the One who offers salvation and eternal life.

We now return to the Persian Empire, where Haman has used his position and influence with King Ahasuerus to set a terrible plan in motion.

Haman was determined to destroy not only Mordecai but all the Jewish people throughout the king's provinces. He had lots cast to determine the day on which his plan would be carried out, and the date fell in the twelfth month, the month of Adar.

Haman approached the king with accusations against a people he did not identify by name. He described them as scattered throughout the kingdom, keeping themselves separate, following different laws, and failing to obey the king's commands. He argued that allowing them to remain would not be in the king's best interest.

We can see how Haman used the differences between the Jewish people and the surrounding culture to justify his hatred. His accusations were designed to make an entire people appear dangerous and disloyal.

God had called Israel to be a people set apart for Him. Their laws and customs distinguished them from the nations around them. Yet being different did not make them deserving of persecution or destruction.

We are also called as Christians to live differently from the world. Jesus tells us to be the light of the world, allowing our good works to bring glory to our Father in heaven. We are not called to withdraw from everyone around us, but to live among others in a way that reflects God's love, righteousness, and truth.

Haman's complaint may have been influenced by Mordecai's refusal to bow before him. What began as anger toward one man had now grown into a plan to annihilate an entire people.

King Ahasuerus placed extraordinary trust in Haman. He gave him his royal signet ring, granting him the authority to issue a decree in the king's name. The order was written and distributed throughout the empire in the languages of the various provinces.

The decree commanded that all Jewish people, young and old, women and children, were to be killed on a single day. Their possessions were also to be taken as plunder.

Imagine the fear and uncertainty that such a decree would bring. Families who had been living and working throughout the kingdom would now face the threat of destruction simply because of their Jewish identity.

The royal couriers carried the order throughout the provinces, and the decree was issued in the fortress of Susa.

Then we come to one of the most striking scenes in this chapter.

While the city of Susa was thrown into confusion, King Ahasuerus and Haman sat down to drink.

What a contrast! Two powerful men sat comfortably together while an entire city struggled to understand the consequences of their decision.

The king and Haman may have believed that they controlled the future of the Jewish people, but they did not understand how events would unfold.

And here we see the importance of the foundation upon which we build our lives.

The Jewish people were facing a storm unlike anything many of them had experienced. Their future appeared uncertain, and the power of the Persian Empire stood against them. Yet as we continue through Esther, we will discover how God worked through circumstances and people to bring deliverance.

We still see hatred, prejudice, and opposition toward Jewish people today, and Christians also face hostility and persecution in parts of the world. The circumstances may differ, but the need to remain faithful to God has not changed.

We cannot always control the storms that surround us. We cannot stop every war, settle every political disagreement, or prevent every hardship that may come into our lives.

But we can choose the foundation upon which we stand.

Jesus calls us not only to hear His words but to put them into practice. When our lives are built upon Him, we have a hope that reaches beyond the uncertainty of this world.

And as the storms rage around us, we have an opportunity to become a light to others, sharing the peace and hope that can be found through faith in Jesus Christ.

The world may be thrown into confusion, but our hearts can remain anchored to the Rock upon which our faith is built.

**Reflection Questions**

1. When you look at the troubles and uncertainty in the world today, do you find yourself becoming fearful or anxious? How can you place those concerns into God's hands?

2. Jesus tells us that those who hear His words and put them into practice are like a wise man who built his house upon the rock. Is your life built upon the solid foundation of Jesus Christ? Are there areas where you need to strengthen that foundation through obedience to His Word?

3. Is there someone in your life who is struggling with fear, uncertainty, or difficult circumstances? How can you be a light to that person today and share the hope that is found in Jesus?
