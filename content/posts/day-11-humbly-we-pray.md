**DAY 11 – HUMBLY WE PRAY**

**Scripture**

**Hebrews 5:7 (CSB)**

"During his earthly life, he offered prayers and appeals with loud cries and tears to the one who was able to save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverence."

**Psalm 54:1–2 (CSB)**

"God, save me by your name, and vindicate me by your might! [2] God, hear my prayer; listen to the words from my mouth."

**Father, My God, I Pray**

Throughout His ministry, Jesus often withdrew to quiet places to pray. The Son of God—the One through whom all things were made—chose stillness and solitude to speak with His Father. His prayers were never meant to impress anyone; they flowed from deep communion and a heart perfectly aligned with the Father’s will.

I have always struggled with knowing how to pray, especially when I hear others in church who pray with such eloquence. Their words flow beautifully, filled with Scripture and poetic phrasing. Meanwhile, my prayers feel simple—sometimes stumbling, sometimes quiet, often just a conversation from the heart. For a long time I felt inadequate, as if my prayers were somehow “less than.”

But over the years, I’ve learned something precious: the Father isn’t listening for eloquence. He’s listening for me.

I’ve found that beginning with a few memorized prayers—ones that match the intent of my heart—helps settle my mind and guide me into those quieter, more personal moments. Once my heart is still, I can speak openly to Him, sharing my hopes, fears, gratitude, and struggles. Those quiet, private moments—where it’s just me and the Father—are where I feel closest to Him.

The Pharisees loved praying where everyone could hear them, seeking admiration for their words. But Jesus prayed differently. His prayers were shaped by relationship, not performance. Even when He prayed in public, it was to bless, to teach, or to reveal the Father—not to draw attention to Himself.

In the Psalms, David shows us this same intimacy. He poured out his heart honestly—fear, anger, praise, confusion, gratitude—all laid bare before God. And God called David a man after His own heart, not because of perfect words but because of honest ones.

Real prayer is not a show. It isn’t measured by volume or poetry. Real prayer is a child speaking to their Father—sometimes whispering, sometimes weeping, sometimes rejoicing, always welcomed.

The Father delights when we come to Him—not just with needs, but simply because we want to be near Him. Prayer is not about saying the right words. It’s about being in the right place:

At the feet of our Father, where love listens and grace meets us every time.

**Reflection Questions**

1. When was the last time I truly prayed from the heart, without worrying about the words?

2. What helps me find stillness and focus when I want to talk with God?

3. How can I make prayer more of a daily conversation rather than an occasional ritual?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for the gift of prayer—an open door into Your presence. Teach me to come before You with honesty and humility, not to impress, but to connect. Forgive me when I rush through words or let distractions steal the quiet moments You long to share with me. Help me to pray as Jesus prayed—with reverence, faith, and love. Let my prayers rise to You not only in need but in gratitude and trust. Be near, Lord, and help me to listen as much as I speak.

Amen.
