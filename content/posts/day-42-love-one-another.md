**DAY 42 – LOVE ONE ANOTHER**

**Scripture**

**1 John 2:7-11 CSB**

Dear friends, I am not writing you a new command but an old command that you have had from the beginning. The old command is the word you have heard. [8] Yet I am writing you a new command, which is true in him and in you, because the darkness is passing away and the true light is already shining. [9] The one who says he is in the light but hates his brother or sister is in the darkness until now. [10] The one who loves his brother or sister remains in the light, and there is no cause for stumbling in him. [11] But the one who hates his brother or sister is in the darkness, walks in the darkness, and doesn't know where he's going, because the darkness has blinded his eyes.

**False Teachers**

John reminds us of something that has been part of our faith from the beginning: we are called to love one another. Jesus taught love for others throughout His ministry. At the Last Supper, He stressed this teaching when He told His disciples, “Just as I have loved you, you are also to love one another.” Jesus did more than teach us about love; He showed us what that love looks like through the way He lived and ultimately through His sacrifice on the cross.

In one sense, this command was old. God's people had long been commanded to love their neighbor, and those who followed Jesus had heard this message from the beginning of their faith. Yet John also calls it a new command. Jesus gave us a living example of what this love should look like. We are to love one another as He has loved us. As we follow Him, His light should become more visible in the way we live and in the way we treat one another.

This is where John's words become personal for each of us. It is easy to say that we walk in the light, but what happens when someone hurts us, disappoints us, or simply gets on our nerves? We can allow bitterness and anger to grow. Sometimes we may even feel justified in holding on to those feelings. Yet John reminds us that hatred belongs to darkness, not to the light in which Christ has called us to walk.

None of us loves perfectly. We have probably all said something harsh, held a grudge longer than we should have, or failed to show love when someone needed it. There may even be times when another Christian is difficult for us to love. But John's words cause us to look at our own hearts. Are we allowing resentment to grow, or are we asking the Lord to help us forgive and love as He has loved us?

This same truth can help us when we listen to those who teach and lead within the church. God's Word should always be our standard for what is taught, but love should also be visible among God's people. Those of us who teach, lead, encourage, or simply share our faith with others need to remember that our words and actions affect people. We are all part of the same body, and we should seek to build one another up rather than tear one another down.

John is also helping us recognize those who would lead believers away from the truth. False teachers were present in John's day, and false teaching is still present within the church today. John gives us several things to consider. What does a teacher say about Jesus? Does the teaching agree with God's Word? Does the person's life show obedience to Christ? Here John adds another question: is the love of Christ visible in the way that person treats others? 

We should not judge someone by a single failure, because we all fall short, but over time both the teaching and the fruit of a person's life will become visible. If someone continually claims to know Christ while showing hatred toward their brothers and sisters in Christ, we have to ask: how can the love of God truly be at work in them?

Later, in his third letter, John tells us about Diotrephes, a man who loved to have first place. He refused to acknowledge John's authority, spoke maliciously about John and his companions, refused to welcome fellow believers, and even put out of the church those who wanted to receive them. His example reminds us what can happen when pride and selfish ambition begin to replace love. We may never behave exactly as Diotrephes did, but we can still ask ourselves whether pride, resentment, or the desire to have our own way sometimes affects how we treat others.

Walking in the light is not simply knowing the right things about Jesus. His light should begin to change the way we live. We will never love as perfectly as He loves us, but we can grow. We can forgive when we have been hurt, encourage someone who is struggling, help someone in need, and show patience when patience is difficult. Each day gives us another opportunity to love our brothers and sisters a little more like Jesus has loved us.

**Reflection Questions**

1.	As I examine my own walk with the Lord, is there anyone toward whom I am holding bitterness or resentment?

2.	Does the way I treat others show the love that Jesus has shown me? What is one way I can show that love today?

3.	Are there times when pride or the desire to have my own way keeps me from loving others as I should?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for the love You have shown us through Your Son. Teach us to love one another as Jesus has loved us. Forgive us for the times that bitterness, anger, pride, or selfishness have affected the way we treat others. Help us to walk in Your light and to see others through the love You have shown us. Give us wisdom to recognize what is true and keep us grounded in Your Word. Help us to forgive, encourage, and care for one another so that the love of Christ can be seen in our lives. In the name of Jesus we pray.

Amen.
