**DAY 81 – COME TO ME**

**Scripture**

**Matthew 11:25-30 CSB**

At that time Jesus said, "I praise you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and intelligent and revealed them to infants. [26] Yes, Father, because this was your good pleasure. [27] All things have been entrusted to me by my Father. No one knows the Son except the Father, and no one knows the Father except the Son and anyone to whom the Son desires to reveal him. [28] "Come to me, all of you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. [29] Take up my yoke and learn from me, because I am lowly and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. [30] For my yoke is easy and my burden is light."

**Rest for your Souls**

In the verses leading up to our reading today, Jesus spoke of three cities in Galilee: Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum. These were places where He had performed many miracles, yet the people had not responded with repentance. They had witnessed remarkable demonstrations of God's power and mercy, but their hearts remained unchanged. Jesus warned that judgment would be more bearable for the ancient cities of Tyre and Sidon than for those who had seen His mighty works and still refused to turn to God.

What a contrast we find in the words that follow. Jesus praises His Heavenly Father because the things of God, hidden from those who consider themselves wise and intelligent, have been revealed to little children. He was not saying that knowledge or intelligence is wrong, but that pride and self-reliance can keep us from recognizing the truth standing before us.

Think of a small child reaching for a parent's hand. The child does not need to understand everything about the road ahead. There is comfort in knowing that someone stronger and wiser is holding that hand, guiding each step and watching for dangers the child may not see. This is the kind of humble trust we should have in our Heavenly Father.

Jesus tells us that the Father has entrusted all things to Him. In John 10:28–29, we also find His wonderful promise that no one can snatch His sheep from His hand or from the Father's hand. That does not mean we will never experience hardship, disappointment, or suffering. It means that through every trial, we belong to the One who loves us and will never abandon us.

Then Jesus gives one of the most comforting invitations in all of Scripture:

“Come to me, all of you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. Take up my yoke and learn from me, because I am lowly and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy and my burden is light.”

How many people spend their lives searching for happiness in the things of this world? We work long hours trying to make a name for ourselves. We strive for the right position, the impressive home, or the automobile that tells others we have succeeded. Some devote themselves to appearances, recognition, or acquiring what someone else possesses. Yet after reaching one goal, another takes its place, and the satisfaction we expected often fades.

There is nothing wrong with working hard, caring for our health, or enjoying the good things God has provided. The problem begins when those things become the measure of our worth and the source of our happiness. We can spend a lifetime trying to impress others and still find ourselves weary, wondering why everything we have accomplished has not brought lasting peace.

Jesus offers us a different way to live.

He does not invite us to compete for His attention or prove that we are worthy of His love. He calls us to come to Him, bringing the burdens we have carried and the sins we cannot erase. Through His sacrifice, we can receive forgiveness and begin a new life. As Jesus explained to Nicodemus in John chapter 3, we must be born again, receiving a new life, and this life is given by God.

Consider the words Jesus uses when He describes Himself: lowly and humble in heart. The Son of God, to whom the Father has entrusted all things, does not call us to follow Him through pride or displays of worldly greatness. He invites us to learn from His humility, His love, and His obedience to the Father.

In biblical times, a yoke was placed upon animals to guide them in their work. Jesus uses this familiar picture to describe a life of following Him. He does not promise that we will have no responsibilities or difficulties, but He offers a way of living that is different from carrying the crushing burden of trying to earn our own righteousness or satisfy the endless demands of the world.

As we grow in our relationship with Christ, our priorities begin to change. We learn to appreciate what God has given us rather than continually comparing our lives with those of others. We discover that our value is not determined by our possessions, accomplishments, or standing in society. Our identity is found in Jesus Christ, and through Him we become part of God's family.

There is a happiness in this that the world cannot provide. It is not the passing excitement of receiving something new or the temporary satisfaction of being praised by others. It is the quiet assurance that we are loved by our Heavenly Father, that our sins can be forgiven, and that our future rests securely in His hands.

Jesus once asked what it would benefit a person to gain the whole world and yet lose his soul (Mark 8:36). What good are all the treasures and honors of this life if we neglect the very One who offers us eternal life?

Perhaps today you are carrying burdens that others cannot see. You may be tired from responsibilities, troubled by the past, or uncertain about what tomorrow will bring. Jesus does not ask you to solve every problem before coming to Him. His invitation is for those who are already weary and burdened.

Come to Him. Learn from Him. Trust Him with your life.

In Jesus Christ, we find a safe and secure harbor for our souls, a place of rest that no amount of worldly success could ever purchase.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Are there things in your life that you have pursued because you hoped they would bring happiness or recognition? Have they given you the lasting peace you expected?

2. Jesus invites those who are weary and burdened to come to Him. What burdens are you carrying today that you need to place in His hands through prayer?

3. What does it mean to you to approach God with the humble trust of a child? How might your life change if you relied more fully on His guidance rather than your own strength?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for loving us and inviting us to come to You through Your Son, Jesus Christ. Forgive us when we seek happiness in the things of this world instead of finding our peace in You. We grow tired and carry burdens that sometimes seem too heavy, but through your Son we have rest for our souls. Help us to trust You with the faith of a child, to learn from the humble heart of Your Son, and to be thankful for the blessings You have given us. Walk with us each day, and remind us that our true worth is found in belonging to You. In Jesus' name we pray.

Amen.
