**DAY 59 – FOREIGN LANDS**

**Scripture**

**Ruth 1:19-22 CSB**

The two of them traveled until they came to Bethlehem. When they entered Bethlehem, the whole town was excited about their arrival and the local women exclaimed, "Can this be Naomi?" [20] "Don't call me Naomi. Call me Mara," she answered, "for the Almighty has made me very bitter. [21] I went away full, but the LORD has brought me back empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the LORD has opposed me, and the Almighty has afflicted me?" [22] So Naomi came back from the territory of Moab with her daughter-in-law Ruth the Moabitess. They arrived in Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.

**Old Friends**

When Naomi's husband took the family to Moab during the famine, the situation surrounding them was one of want. But as Naomi returns home, the whole town is stirred by her arrival. These were people who remembered her and must have wondered what had happened during all those years she had lived in a foreign land. She had left Bethlehem with a husband and two sons, but now she returned with only her daughter-in-law Ruth.

Naomi looked back at what she had when she left Bethlehem. Yes, there had been a famine, but she had her husband and her two sons. Now they were gone. She told the women not to call her Naomi, a name associated with pleasantness, but Mara, which means bitter. In her eyes, she had left full and returned empty.

Many make a comparison here to those who try to work around the problems of this life in their own power instead of going to God in prayer and seeking His direction. We do not know whether Elimelech's decision to leave Bethlehem for Moab during the famine was against the will of God, nor are we told whether he sought God's direction before leaving. We do know, however, of the troubled relationship between Israel and Moab. In Deuteronomy chapter 23, God even placed a restriction on Moabites entering the LORD's assembly because of the way Moab had treated Israel after the Israelites came out of Egypt.

Naomi clearly believed that the Lord had brought affliction into her life. She answered:

"Don't call me Naomi. Call me Mara," she answered, "for the Almighty has made me very bitter. I went away full, but the LORD has brought me back empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the LORD has opposed me, and the Almighty has afflicted me?"

Naomi could see everything she had lost, but she could not yet see what God was going to do. Sometimes we suffer in this life and wonder whether it is because of something we have done. God does correct His children, but we also suffer simply because we live in a fallen world. We should be careful about assuming that every hardship is God's punishment. Whatever the reason for our suffering, God is there with us and can put the broken pieces of our lives back together.

How can we doubt God's concern for us when He sent His Son to die for us? Naomi believed that the Lord had opposed and afflicted her. Perhaps she also wondered about the decisions her family had made in leaving Bethlehem for a foreign land surrounded by foreign gods. Scripture does not tell us that their move to Moab caused the deaths of her husband and sons, so we should not say more than God has revealed. But Naomi knew that she had left Bethlehem full and had now returned grieving and broken.

There is a similarity here to the prodigal son. He left his father's house with his inheritance and sought something better among the promises of the world. Eventually, after losing everything, he returned home humbled. Instead of rejecting him, his father ran to meet him and welcomed him home.

Naomi's story is different, and we do not know whether leaving Bethlehem was an act of disobedience. But she too had finally returned home. The years in the foreign land had been hard on her and her family, but now she was back in Bethlehem.

And Naomi had not really returned empty. Ruth was standing beside her.
Ruth was a Moabite, from a people who had a troubled history with Israel and upon whom God had placed restrictions in His law. Yet Ruth had already made an extraordinary declaration:

"For wherever you go, I will go, and wherever you live, I will live; your people will be my people, and your God will be my God."

Ruth was leaving behind her people and their gods and placing her faith in the God of Israel. Naomi could see only what she had lost, but she could not yet see what God would do through the daughter who had returned with her.

And there was another small sign of hope. Naomi had left Bethlehem during a famine, but she returned at the beginning of the barley harvest. The foreign land was behind her. She was home, Ruth was beside her, and the harvest was beginning.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Has there been a time in your life when something bad happened and you interpreted it as punishment from God? How did you react?

2. How do you think the people of Naomi's hometown felt when they saw her return with Ruth?

3. Have you ever looked at your situation in life and wanted a change? Did you go to God in prayer and ask for His guidance, or did you try to change things on your own?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for being a God who loves us and cares for us. Help us to be content in the situation in life where You have placed us, and teach us to come to You in prayer for guidance when we desire to make changes in our lives. Help us to be more concerned about the people around us than the things we can gain from this world. Give us the joy of knowing that our worth is not measured by what other people have, but by who we are in You. When we suffer loss and cannot understand what You are doing, keep us from becoming bitter. Help us trust You even when all we can see is what we have lost. In Jesus' name I pray.
