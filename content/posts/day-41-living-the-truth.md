**DAY 41 – LIVING THE TRUTH**

**Scripture**

**1 John 2:1-6 CSB**

My little children, I am writing you these things so that you may not sin. But if anyone does sin, we have an advocate with the Father-Jesus Christ the righteous one. [2] He himself is the atoning sacrifice for our sins, and not only for ours, but also for those of the whole world. [3] This is how we know that we know him: if we keep his commands. [4] The one who says, "I have come to know him," and yet doesn't keep his commands, is a liar, and the truth is not in him. [5] But whoever keeps his word, truly in him the love of God is made complete. This is how we know we are in him: [6] The one who says he remains in him should walk just as he walked.

**John 15:9-10 CSB**

"As the Father has loved me, I have also loved you. Remain in my love. [10] If you keep my commands you will remain in my love, just as I have kept my Father's commands and remain in his love.

**Walking the Way**

Today’s devotion should move us to bow in worship, for Jesus tells us that the very love that the Father pours out for the Son is the same love that Jesus pours out on believers—a vast, unmeasurable love beyond our full comprehension. Jesus also tells us to remain in His love, and He connects remaining in His love with keeping His commands. John now echoes that same truth: those who know Christ will desire to walk as He walked.

John freely calls all Christians “little children,” reminding us that we belong to God’s family. Yet in this fallen world, we will never achieve perfect Christlikeness. We still sin, sometimes even without realizing that we have fallen short of God’s will for our lives. But Jesus paid the price for all our sins. When we fall short, Jesus is our advocate with the Father. He speaks to the Father in our defense. Through Jesus’ own sacrifice on the cross, He has already paid for our sins and won our forgiveness.

Does that mean we may willfully continue in sin because we know that God forgives us? Of course not. God’s grace is never permission to live in rebellion against Him. John tells us that those who truly know Christ will desire to keep His commands.

John goes on to describe the marks of true Christians, the first being obedience. We do what Jesus commands, driven by a loving desire to do His will. Jesus summed up the Law and the Prophets by telling us to love God with all our heart, soul, and mind and to love our neighbor as ourselves. The Christian life should reflect that love. We demonstrate our love for the Lord by paying attention to His words and allowing His love to shape how we treat others.

John’s words would certainly apply to the false teachers who were leading believers astray at the time he wrote this letter, but his warning reaches further than them. Anyone can claim to know Christ. John tells us that a life continually opposed to Jesus’ commands contradicts that claim. Our obedience does not earn our salvation, but it gives evidence that Jesus truly is Lord of our lives. Because Jesus loves us, we desire to remain in His love and obey His commands. A sign of a true Christian is a desire to please the Lord.

Even the best of us must admit that our love falls far short of the great love the Lord has shown us. Selfish desires often crowd out our devotion to Him and our commitment to others. Sometimes these are not mere oversights but choices—moments that later cause us to humbly bow before the Lord and ask for forgiveness. Yet when we sin, we have an advocate with the Father: Jesus Christ the righteous one. We can seek His strength to become more like Him and move beyond our shortcomings. May we follow Jesus out of love, rest in His forgiveness, and grow more into the likeness of God’s Son every day.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Do I really love Jesus and want to follow His will and the things that He taught? When I fall short, do I humbly ask for forgiveness and the strength to become a better follower?

2. Do I truly love others as myself? What is one thing I can do today to demonstrate this love to someone else?

3. How does knowing that Jesus loves us as the Father loves Him change how I respond to what Jesus would have me become?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

May I always follow after You, being led by Your Spirit of peace and love. Give me strength to overcome the temptations of this world. Surround me with believers to pray for me, comfort me, and remind me that my peace and strength are only in You. Take away the sin in my life so that I might better serve You and that my life might be a testimony for You. Remove jealousy, bitterness, and selfishness from my life, and let me follow after the things of the Spirit. In Jesus’ name.
