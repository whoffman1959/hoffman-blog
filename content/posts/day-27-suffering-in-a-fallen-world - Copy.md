**DAY 27 – SUFFERING IN A FALLEN WORLD**

**Scripture**

**1 Peter 4:12–19 CSB**

Dear friends, don't be surprised when the fiery ordeal comes among you to test you, as if something unusual were happening to you. [13] Instead, rejoice as you share in the sufferings of Christ, so that you may also rejoice with great joy when his glory is revealed. [14] If you are ridiculed for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you. [15] Let none of you suffer as a murderer, a thief, an evildoer, or a meddler. [16] But if anyone suffers as a Christian, let him not be ashamed but let him glorify God in having that name. [17] For the time has come for judgment to begin with God's household, and if it begins with us, what will the outcome be for those who disobey the gospel of God? [18] And if a righteous person is saved with difficulty, what will become of the ungodly and the sinner? [19] So then, let those who suffer according to God's will entrust themselves to a faithful Creator while doing what is good.

**Judgement and Testing**

We live in a fallen world. Not all people are Christians, and we often find ourselves in conflict with others—left hurting by the sting of loss and pain. Sometimes this pain goes deep into our inmost parts, where we cry out, “Where is the justice, God?” This is the point when we need to humbly remember that Jesus also experienced deep suffering, yet He entrusted Himself to the Father and willingly took upon Himself the punishment we deserved for the dark, and sometimes not so dark, hidden things that we have done in our lives against God's will and against one another.

It is foolish to shout at the top of our lungs for justice when our Heavenly Father already sees. Is not the One who created all things, who knows when a sparrow is in distress, able to see what is happening to us?

Psalm 94 voices the concern of those who are hurt plainly:

LORD, how long will the wicked—how long will the wicked celebrate?

Later in the Psalm, we have reassurance that the LORD will not leave His people. He does judge in righteousness. He stands for His people against the wicked, keeps our feet from slipping, and supports us. The comfort of the Lord brings us joy. God is not restricted by our timetable for justice, but His justice is tempered with grace. He desires that people turn to Him and be saved.

By turning our hate to love, being good to those who harm us as Jesus taught, and forgiving those who wrong us, those who hurt us might see a glimpse of the love of the Father in us and turn to Him.

We know that we have a Father in Heaven who corrects us as His children, allows testing to develop the character of our faith, and allows us to face the challenges of standing firm for His Word when those outside the faith oppose us. Just as an earthly parent would correct a child or allow them to face challenges that build them up, our Heavenly Father does it for us out of love. We prove our faith is real by what we endure.

It hurt my mother and father to correct me as a child and to see me struggle as I became a better person. The schoolwork, the first rejection by someone I wanted to be with, not making the team at school—the list goes on. They let me know when I got older that they hurt with me and felt it most with the correction as it was given to me. They did it for my good. They did it so that I might become a better person and be able to survive in this world.

Our God also corrects us, but He also allows us to face trials that strengthen our faith, develop perseverance, and teach us to stand firm in Him.

If we, as His children, undergo correction, how much more serious is the judgment that awaits those who reject Him? Peter reminds us that judgment begins with God's household. If God's people are not exempt from suffering and judgment, what will become of those who disobey the gospel of God?

We know that Scripture warns us of the judgment that awaits those who reject God. Scripture tells us that the wicked fall into the traps they have made, and their sins will bring judgment upon themselves. This is nothing to wish for. It is a terrible thing to fall under the judgment of God. May they also find our Father in Heaven, come to believe in His Son, and be saved. May we not run with them, seeking the life they momentarily have that will soon pass away. May we walk in love, resisting the temptation of evil.

**Reflection Questions**

1. How might I be tempted to do the earthly things that others do to have what they have—to deny Jesus for a short time so that I won't be hurt and can receive earthly glory?
2. Can I pray that God would give me the grace to forgive those who have hurt me in the past, understanding that I myself have been forgiven? If not, can I pray that God would heal the hurt and help me learn to forgive?
3. Do I understand that my Father in Heaven is loving, but also a God of justice? Do I believe that God watches over me and that true justice is in His hands?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Help us in those times when we have been hurt, abandoned, and betrayed, as Your Son was. Give us the heart of Jesus so that we might forgive others as Jesus did. Let us take the burden of the hurts caused by others and return love instead, praying that they also might come to You and be saved. Allow us to have the faith that You always watch over Your children and that You know what is happening in our lives. You care even for the sparrow, and we know that we are much more valuable to You. You care for all of Your creation. Let us rest in Your justice and not our own. In your Son’s name we pray.

Amen.
