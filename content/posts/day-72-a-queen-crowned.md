**DAY 72 – A QUEEN CROWNED**

**Scripture**

**Esther 2:15-23 CSB**

Esther was the daughter of Abihail, the uncle of Mordecai who had adopted her as his own daughter. When her turn came to go to the king, she did not ask for anything except what Hegai, the king's eunuch, keeper of the women, suggested. Esther gained favor in the eyes of everyone who saw her. [16] She was taken to King Ahasuerus in the palace in the tenth month, the month Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign. [17] The king loved Esther more than all the other women. She won more favor and approval from him than did any of the other virgins. He placed the royal crown on her head and made her queen in place of Vashti. [18] The king held a great banquet for all his officials and staff. It was Esther's banquet. He freed his provinces from tax payments and gave gifts worthy of the king's bounty. [19] When the virgins were gathered a second time, Mordecai was sitting at the King's Gate. [20] Esther still did not reveal her family background or her ethnicity, as Mordecai had directed. She obeyed Mordecai's orders, as she always had while he raised her. [21] During those days while Mordecai was sitting at the King's Gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the entrance, became infuriated and planned to assassinate King Ahasuerus. [22] When Mordecai learned of the plot, he reported it to Queen Esther, and she told the king on Mordecai's behalf. [23] When the report was investigated and verified, both men were hanged on the gallows. This event was recorded in the Historical Record in the king's presence.

**Following Advice**

Instead of making lavish requests for outward garments and adornments that Esther thought might impress the king, she followed the advice of Hegai, the keeper of the king's harem. We do not know what he suggested, but being responsible for the women in the king's household, he probably understood the king's preferences better than most. Perhaps he suggested that Esther depend upon her natural beauty, something many people hold in high esteem.

We are told that Esther went before King Ahasuerus and that he loved her more than any of the other women. She gained his favor, and he placed the royal crown upon her head, making her queen in place of Vashti. The king celebrated with a great banquet, gave gifts, and granted relief from taxes throughout his provinces.

But what if Esther had not followed Hegai's advice? What if she had decided that she was strong and independent enough to make every decision on her own, without listening to someone who understood the circumstances better than she did?

This is a problem many of us encounter in our relationship with God. We sometimes treat God's Word as advice rather than instruction from the great King, the Creator of the universe. We begin thinking that our circumstances are somehow different or that we know what is best for our lives.

We may even act as though God does not understand our situation or what we are going through. This is pride rather than the humble spirit that God cherishes. God knows all things, including those things we cannot see or understand.

When we read Matthew chapter 6, Jesus reminds us that our heavenly Father knows what we need before we ask Him. We cannot add even a moment to our lives through worry. Jesus teaches us to seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, trusting our Father to provide for our needs.

Yet how easy it is to say, I am the captain of my own ship. I can direct my own scenes in the play of life.

We make plans, set goals, and attempt to arrange everything according to our own desires. There is nothing wrong with planning or accepting responsibility for our lives, but when we begin believing that everything depends upon our own strength and understanding, worry and fear can quickly take hold.

Our very existence depends upon God. The breath we take, the opportunities before us, and the days we are given are all gifts from Him. We may not know what tomorrow will bring, but we can trust the One who does.

We see this humble and trusting spirit reflected in Esther's willingness to listen to Mordecai, the man who had raised her as his own daughter. Even after becoming queen, she continued to follow his instruction not to reveal her family background or Jewish ethnicity. What may have seemed like a small matter at the time would become important as the story unfolded.

But there was another life through which God would work to protect the Jewish people.

Mordecai was sitting at the King's Gate when the young women were gathered a second time. Scripture does not explain the purpose of this second gathering, but it places Mordecai in a position where he learned of a dangerous plot.

Two of the king's eunuchs, Bigthan and Teresh, who guarded the entrance, became angry with King Ahasuerus and planned to assassinate him.

When Mordecai learned of their plans, he informed Esther, who reported the matter to the king on Mordecai's behalf. The accusation was investigated and found to be true. The two men were executed, and the incident was recorded in the official chronicles of the kingdom.

At this point, Mordecai received no reward that Scripture mentions. Yet the record of what he had done would become important later.

We can see how events were beginning to come together. Esther had become queen, Mordecai remained near the royal court, and his loyalty to the king had been recorded. Neither of them could have known how these circumstances would eventually affect the future of the Jewish people.

This is one of the beautiful lessons we find in Esther. God's name is not mentioned in the book, yet we can recognize His providence through the events that unfold. What may appear to be ordinary decisions and circumstances can become part of something far greater than we understand.

Many of us go through life worrying about so much, trying to balance every circumstance in our favor. We want to know what tomorrow will bring and how every problem will be resolved.

But one of the greatest gifts we can receive is the peace of mind that comes from trusting God.

We can bring our doubts, fears, and concerns before our Father in heaven, who loves us. This does not mean that every difficulty will disappear or that life will always unfold according to our desires. It means that we do not have to carry our burdens alone.

Jesus reminds us that each day has enough trouble of its own. We can make our plans, seek wise counsel, and fulfill our responsibilities while trusting God with the things beyond our control.

We may not always understand what He is doing, but we can trust His wisdom, His love, and His faithfulness.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Do you try to control every aspect of your life, or do you bring your concerns to God in prayer, trusting Him to guide your decisions?

2. Jesus reminds us that God feeds the birds and clothes the flowers of the field. What blessings has God provided in your life that remind you of His love and care?

3. What is the value of having a humble and teachable spirit? Are you willing to listen to wise advice from others, especially when it challenges your own understanding?
