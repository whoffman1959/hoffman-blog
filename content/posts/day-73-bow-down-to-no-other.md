**DAY 73 – BOW DOWN TO NO OTHER**

**Scripture**

**Esther 3:1-6 CSB**

After all this took place, King Ahasuerus honored Haman, son of Hammedatha the Agagite. He promoted him in rank and gave him a higher position than all the other officials. [2] The entire royal staff at the King's Gate bowed down and paid homage to Haman, because the king had commanded this to be done for him. But Mordecai would not bow down or pay homage. [3] The members of the royal staff at the King's Gate asked Mordecai, "Why are you disobeying the king's command?" [4] When they had warned him day after day and he still would not listen to them, they told Haman in order to see if Mordecai's actions would be tolerated, since he had told them he was a Jew. [5] When Haman saw that Mordecai was not bowing down or paying him homage, he was filled with rage. [6] And when he learned of Mordecai's ethnic identity, it seemed repugnant to Haman to do away with Mordecai alone. He planned to destroy all of Mordecai's people, the Jews, throughout Ahasuerus's kingdom.

**Matthew 4:8-10 CSB**

Again, the devil took him to a very high mountain and showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their splendor. [9] And he said to him, "I will give you all these things if you will fall down and worship me." [10] Then Jesus told him, "Go away, Satan! For it is written: Worship the Lord your God, and serve only him."

**No Other Gods**

The opening words of Esther chapter 3, “After all this took place,” indicate that some time had passed since the events of the previous chapter. It is estimated that approximately five years had passed between Esther becoming queen and the events now unfolding. As in many stories, an antagonist enters the picture, and this time it is a man named Haman.

We are not told why King Ahasuerus promoted Haman above the other officials of his court. Yet as we continue reading, we will see a man whose pride and hatred would become a threat to God's people. Haman was called an Agagite, a name often associated with Agag, king of the Amalekites. The Amalekites had a long history of hostility toward Israel. In Exodus 17:8–16, we read how the Amalekites attacked Israel and how Moses stood on the hill with the staff of God in his hand. When his hands grew tired, Aaron and Hur supported them as Joshua led Israel to victory.

The King's Gate was an important entrance to the royal palace complex at Susa, where officials and others conducted the business of the kingdom. As Haman came and went, the royal staff at the gate bowed down and paid homage to him, as King Ahasuerus had commanded. Everyone obeyed, except one man: Mordecai.

When does honoring someone in authority become an act of worship? Perhaps this was something Mordecai considered as he refused to bow before Haman. As a Jew, Mordecai would have known the commandments given to Israel, including those found in Exodus 20:3–5, which forbid placing other gods before the Lord or bowing down in worship to idols.

Scripture does not tell us precisely why Mordecai refused, but it does connect his refusal with his Jewish identity. We are called to respect those in authority, but our highest loyalty must always remain with God. There is a difference between showing respect to another person and giving that person the devotion that belongs to the Lord alone.

Mordecai's fellow workers at the King's Gate questioned him repeatedly about his refusal to obey the king's command. Day after day, they warned him, but he would not change his mind. Eventually, they reported his actions to Haman, wanting to see whether Mordecai's refusal would be tolerated. Mordecai had told them that he was a Jew.

We see a similar question of worship and loyalty in the temptation of Jesus, recorded in Matthew chapter 4. After Jesus had fasted for forty days and forty nights, Satan tempted Him with the kingdoms of the world and their splendor. Satan offered Him these things if He would bow down and worship him.
But Jesus refused. He reminded Satan that worship and service belong to the Lord God alone.

Satan may not come to us with the direct words, Bow down and worship me, as he did with Jesus, but temptation can still come through the things of this world.
It may begin with a thought: Did God really say that I should not do this? Surely God does not expect me to give up everything that brings me pleasure. Doesn't He want me to enjoy life?

And God certainly does want us to experience the blessings of life. His commandments are not given to rob us of joy, but to guide us toward a life that honors Him and protects us from the destructive power of sin.

Yet sin is deceitful. What begins as something attractive or seemingly harmless can gradually take hold of our hearts. The consequences may not appear immediately, but when we continue placing our desires above God's will, those desires can begin to control us.

We can make gods out of many things: wealth, possessions, power, success, pleasure, or even our own importance. When something becomes more important to us than following God's will, we are allowing it to occupy a place in our hearts that belongs to Him.

This does not mean that working hard, enjoying life, or seeking success is wrong. But we must be careful that these things do not become the masters of our lives.

Haman was filled with rage when he discovered that Mordecai would not bow before him. His pride had been challenged, and rather than dealing with Mordecai alone, he determined to destroy all the Jewish people throughout the kingdom.

What a terrible example of how pride and anger can grow into hatred. One man's refusal to bow became, in Haman's mind, a reason to destroy an entire people.

We can also recognize the spiritual struggle taking place. Throughout history, God's people have faced opposition from those who seek power and demand loyalty that belongs to God. Yet the Lord has never abandoned His people.
And here we begin to see how God was already preparing the way.

Esther, a Jewish woman whose identity remained unknown to the king, was now queen of Persia. Mordecai, who had refused to bow before Haman, had already saved the king's life by reporting an assassination plot. His actions had been recorded in the royal chronicles.

Neither Esther nor Mordecai could have known how these circumstances would eventually come together. But as the story unfolds, we will see God's providence at work, even through the pride and evil intentions of others.

The world may offer us wealth, recognition, and power, but none of these things can take the place of God. Like Jesus in the wilderness, we must learn to recognize temptation and remain faithful to the One who gives us life.

We may bow our heads in respect before earthly authorities, but our hearts must bow in worship before God alone.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Have you ever allowed something in your life to become more important than following God's will? Could your work, possessions, ambitions, or personal desires be taking time and devotion that should belong to the Lord?

2. Why do you think Haman became so angry when Mordecai refused to bow before him? Have there been times when your own pride caused you to react in anger toward someone else?

3. Jesus refused the riches and kingdoms of the world rather than bow down to Satan. Are you willing to place obedience to God above worldly success, recognition, or personal gain?
