**DAY 85 – GOD'S KINDNESS**

**Scripture**

**Matthew 13:18-23 CSB**

"So listen to the parable of the sower: [19] When anyone hears the word about the kingdom and doesn't understand it, the evil one comes and snatches away what was sown in his heart. This is the one sown along the path. [20] And the one sown on rocky ground-this is one who hears the word and immediately receives it with joy. [21] But he has no root and is short-lived. When distress or persecution comes because of the word, immediately he falls away. [22] Now the one sown among the thorns-this is one who hears the word, but the worries of this age and the deceitfulness of wealth choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful. [23] But the one sown on the good ground-this is one who hears and understands the word, who does produce fruit and yields: some a hundred, some sixty, some thirty times what was sown."

**Romans 2:4 CSB**

Or do you despise the riches of his kindness, restraint, and patience, not recognizing that God's kindness is intended to lead you to repentance?

**Sowing Seed**

There is another way we might express the question Paul asks in Romans 2:4: Do we truly appreciate the riches God offers us through His kindness, patience, and restraint? It is God's kindness that calls us to repentance, offering forgiveness through His Son, Jesus Christ. Rather than continuing in our old ways, He invites us to turn from sin and begin a new life, seeking to live in a way that honors Him.

God's invitation reaches out to the world. Scripture tells us in 2 Peter 3:9 that the Lord is patient, not wanting any to perish but all to come to repentance. Yet Jesus also reminds us in Matthew 22:14 that many are invited, but few are chosen. We are offered a wonderful gift, but not everyone responds to God's calling with a willing heart.

Jesus explains something about this unwillingness in John 3:19–20. Although the light has come into the world, people often love darkness rather than light because they do not want their deeds exposed. This is a sobering thought, especially when we examine our own hearts. Even today, when I resist God's will, I must ask myself whether I am holding on to some desire that conflicts with His Word. Sometimes we want to remain in control of our own lives rather than surrendering ourselves completely to Him.

In today's passage from Matthew, Jesus explains the different ways people respond when they hear the Word of God. He uses the familiar picture of a farmer scattering seed, but the condition of the soil determines whether that seed will grow and bear fruit.

Some hear the Word of God, but their hearts have become hardened. They may listen to the message, yet they do not understand or accept what God is telling them. The evil one comes and snatches away what was sown in their hearts. They may listen instead to the lies and temptations of Satan, allowing those voices to lead them away from the truth. God's Word never has an opportunity to take root in their lives.

Others hear the message of Jesus and receive it with great joy. They may even be excited about their newfound faith, but like seed falling on rocky ground, their roots never grow deep. When life becomes difficult, when friends turn against them, or when following Christ requires personal sacrifice, their enthusiasm begins to fade. Rather than holding firmly to the Lord, they fall away when the cost of following Him becomes greater than they expected.

Still others hear and receive the Word, but the pressures of everyday life begin to crowd it out. There are bills to pay, families to care for, jobs demanding our attention, and the bright lights and attractions of the world promising happiness and success. Before long, we may find ourselves with little time to open God's Word, spend time in prayer, enjoy fellowship with Him, or tell others about Jesus. The worries of life and the pursuit of worldly things become like thorns, choking the growth of God's Word until little fruit is produced.

Then there are those whose hearts are like good soil. They hear God's Word, understand it, and allow it to take root in their lives. The worries and attractions of this world may still surround them, but these things do not become more important than their relationship with Christ. Jesus becomes more precious to than anything the world can offer. They seek His guidance, spend time in His Word, and find joy in doing the things that please Him. Their lives begin to reflect His love, and through their kindness, compassion, and willingness to share the gospel, others may be drawn to Christ. The fruit they produce becomes a living testimony of God's work in their hearts.

This brings us back to the kindness and patience of our Heavenly Father. God continues to call us through His Word, giving us opportunities to examine our lives and turn away from the things that separate us from Him. His patience should never be mistaken for indifference toward sin. Rather, it reveals His mercy and His desire that we come to repentance.

When we find that our lives are not producing the fruit they should, we need to examine the condition of our hearts. Have we allowed the enemy to harden us against God's Word? Is our commitment to Christ shallow, fading whenever following Him becomes difficult? Have the worries and attractions of this world crowded out the time we once spent in prayer and fellowship with Him?

Or are we allowing His Word to grow within us, shaping our thoughts, guiding our decisions, and teaching us to love others as Christ has loved us?

Our Heavenly Father is patient and kind, but He also calls us to respond. Each day presents another opportunity to draw closer to Him, turn away from what displeases Him, and allow His Word to bear fruit in our lives. What a precious gift it is to know that the God who calls us to repentance also offers the grace and strength we need to follow Him.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Romans 2:4 reminds us that God's kindness and patience are intended to lead us to repentance. Are there areas of your life where God has been patiently calling you to turn away from something that displeases Him?

2. In the parable of the sower, Jesus describes how the worries of life and the attractions of this world can prevent His Word from bearing fruit. Are there things in your life that are distracting you from spending time with God and following His will?

3. When you examine your relationship with Jesus Christ, can you see the fruit of His Word growing in your life? How might you become more fruitful in showing His love and sharing His message with others?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for Your wonderful kindness, patience, and mercy toward us. You continue to call us to repentance, even when we allow the cares and attractions of this world to draw our hearts away from You. Forgive us when we resist Your guidance or place our own desires above Your will. Help us to receive Your Word with open hearts, allowing it to take root and grow in our lives. Remove the distractions that keep us from drawing closer to You, and give us the strength to remain faithful when trials and difficulties come. May our lives produce fruit that honors You, and may others come to know the love and salvation found in Your Son, Jesus Christ. In His precious name we pray.

Amen.
