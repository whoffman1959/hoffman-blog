**DAY 13 – THE GIFT OF FORGIVENESS**

**Scripture**

**Matthew 6:14–15 (CSB)**

"For if you forgive others their offenses, your heavenly Father will forgive you as well. [15] But if you don't forgive others, your Father will not forgive your offenses."

**Forgive Thy Neighbor**

Everyone in life has been hurt, and we have all—whether knowingly or not—hurt others in return. Sometimes it's a misunderstanding, a moment of pride, or a reaction born out of fear. Other times the wounds run deeper: betrayal, abandonment, loss, injustice, or seasons where we feel God is distant. But much of the world’s pain comes simply from living in a fallen world.

In my own life, some of the deepest wounds have come through abandonment and divorce. Yet with time, I came to see my own part in those years—I truly did not understand how to be the husband I should have been. At work, there were seasons when people talked behind my back or created difficult situations, and those memories stung deeply. But when I look back honestly, I see moments in my younger years when I gossiped or acted without understanding the weight of words.

Even in times of illness, when I felt alone, I later realized how often I had failed to show up for others who were suffering. I prayed for them, yes—but I didn’t always go, or call, or help the way I should have.

Seeing the flaws in others often reveals our own brokenness. And yet—this is where the beauty of grace shines brightest. The Father called me even in my brokenness. He forgave me through His Son long before I was lovable, long before I knew how deeply I needed His mercy.

What a loving Father we have.

Now I am learning to respond with that same love—to forgive as I have been forgiven. I cannot do this on my own. There are still days when I come to the Father with failures in my hands, asking for strength to love those who have been unloving, just as He loved me when I was far from Him.

Forgiveness is one of the most powerful gifts God gives. It doesn’t erase the pain or pretend the hurt didn’t matter. Instead, it frees our hearts. Many people discover, after holding on to bitterness for years, that forgiveness finally brings peace. It stops the endless replay of old wounds and removes the power those moments once had over us.

When we forgive, we release the weight we were never meant to carry. Jesus teaches that as we forgive, we open our hearts to receive the Father’s forgiveness more deeply—not because God holds back His grace, but because forgiveness shapes our hearts to resemble His.

Forgiveness is not weakness. It is strength. It is faith. It is the quiet confidence that God will bring justice, healing, and restoration in ways we never could.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Is there someone in my life I need to forgive—someone whose actions still hold power over my peace?

2. How has God’s forgiveness toward me helped me extend grace to others?

3. What might change in my heart and my relationship with God if I chose to forgive completely today?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for the gift of forgiveness—both the mercy You’ve shown to me and the grace You call me to give others. You know the hurts I’ve carried and the pain that has lingered. Help me to release bitterness and anger into Your hands, trusting that You are just and good. Teach me to forgive as You forgive—to let go, to love, and to live in freedom. May Your peace fill the places where pain once lived, and may my heart reflect Your mercy each day.

Amen.
