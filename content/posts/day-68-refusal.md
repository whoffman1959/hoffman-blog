**DAY 68 – REFUSAL**

**Scripture**

**Esther 1:10-15 CSB**

On the seventh day, when the king was feeling good from the wine, Ahasuerus commanded Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar, and Carkas-the seven eunuchs who personally served him- [11] to bring Queen Vashti before him with her royal crown. He wanted to show off her beauty to the people and the officials, because she was very beautiful. [12] But Queen Vashti refused to come at the king's command that was delivered by his eunuchs. The king became furious and his anger burned within him. [13] The king consulted the wise men who understood the times, for it was his normal procedure to confer with experts in law and justice. [14] The most trusted ones were Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena, and Memucan. They were the seven officials of Persia and Media who had personal access to the king and occupied the highest positions in the kingdom. [15] The king asked, "According to the law, what should be done with Queen Vashti, since she refused to obey King Ahasuerus's command that was delivered by the eunuchs?"

**Matthew 5:3-5 CSB**

"Blessed are the poor in spirit, for the kingdom of heaven is theirs. [4] Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted. [5] Blessed are the humble, for they will inherit the earth.

**Consequences of Pride**

When we read the Beatitudes in Matthew chapter 5 and the teachings that follow, we begin to understand how Christians are called to live as citizens of heaven. These are not expectations reserved for some future time, but principles we are called to live by today.

When we consider how we should act before God and treat others, we recognize how often we fall short. To obey Christ is to become more like Him. This is a continuing work of God's grace, as He molds us into the image of His Son through the Holy Spirit. Our experiences in life often become the means through which God teaches us and helps us grow spiritually.

God gives us the strength to follow His will. Yet when we stumble along the way, we can humbly come before Him, confess our shortcomings, and seek forgiveness through His Son.

We see pride, the struggle to maintain authority, and a sense of self-importance in our Scripture reading today.

The king's celebration had continued for days throughout the royal palace. We can imagine the sounds of conversation and laughter filling the courtyard as servants moved among the guests, pouring wine into golden goblets. The colorful linen hangings stirred in the evening breeze, while the light from the lamps reflected off the marble columns and the silver and gold furnishings.

Surrounded by such wealth and splendor, Ahasuerus was enjoying the festivities, his judgment perhaps clouded by the wine.

Meanwhile, Queen Vashti was holding her own banquet for the women of the royal palace, something consistent with the customs of the ancient Persian court. Perhaps she was surrounded by other important women of the kingdom, including the wives of counselors and military leaders, enjoying their own conversations and festivities away from the king's banquet.

But the festivities were about to take an unexpected turn.

The king sent seven of his personal servants to bring Queen Vashti before the assembled guests, intending to display her beauty. His pride appears to have led him to regard his wife as another possession to show off alongside the riches of his kingdom.

When the command was delivered, Vashti refused to come. Scripture does not tell us why. Perhaps she believed the request was improper, or perhaps she wished to protect her dignity.

Whatever her reason, her refusal stirred the king's anger. A man accustomed to having his commands obeyed had now been openly defied, and his authority appeared to have been challenged before those gathered at his banquet.

Jesus tells us there are blessings in things that often run contrary to the thinking of worldly society. We certainly do not see this humility demonstrated by King Ahasuerus, and perhaps many of those caught up in the excitement and splendor of the celebration had also lost sight of it. The poor in spirit are those who acknowledge their own spiritual helplessness and rely upon God's strength. These are the ones Jesus says will be blessed.

The kingdom of heaven is not about self-sufficiency and self-exaltation. What becomes a virtue in the eyes of God is a humble heart.

And in this life, we will experience mourning. There will be times of loss, disappointment, and suffering. We may mourn over our own failures and the times we have fallen short of God's will. We may also suffer at the hands of those who believe they have power and importance over others.

There will be people who mistake the humility of a believer for weakness and use it against us. To be humble is not always without cost. There will be times when others manipulate circumstances to our detriment. But Christ calls us to love and forgive, even when doing so is difficult. This, in itself, is part of making Jesus Lord of our lives.

Jesus says that the humble are blessed, and this is the mantle we take upon ourselves as Christians.

We might have been vengeful and quick to fight for our own causes before being called by the Lord, but now we have a different spirit. We may be strong as a lion when standing up for God and the needs of others, but humble when it comes to our own desires and importance.

We know that when we hunger and thirst for righteousness, we will be filled. When we are merciful, we will be shown mercy. Those with a pure heart will see God. Peacemakers will be called children of God, and those who are persecuted for righteousness because of Christ belong to His kingdom.

These are not always the qualities that the world admires. The world often celebrates those who are powerful, wealthy, and able to command the obedience of others. But Jesus teaches us that greatness in the kingdom of heaven is found in humility, mercy, and a heart that seeks after God.

Returning to Ahasuerus, we can imagine how his pride must have been stung when Queen Vashti refused his command before those gathered at the banquet. What should be done about this insult? What punishment should follow her refusal?

The king turned to his counselors, men who understood the laws and customs of Persia and Media. Rather than allowing his anger to subside, he sought their advice about what should be done with Queen Vashti.

Perhaps forgiveness and a quiet conversation after the celebration had ended could have resolved the issue. But pride often stands in the way of reconciliation. We become more concerned with being right, protecting our reputation, or having the final word than with understanding the feelings of others.

Actions have consequences, and as we continue through Esther, we will discover that the powerful men surrounding the king feared the implications of this one act of refusal by Queen Vashti.

Yet even in the weakness of human pride, God was working. Events were beginning to unfold that would eventually place Esther in a position to help protect the Jewish people.

We can become so concerned with our own importance that we forget the importance of others. But Christ calls us to something different. He calls us to walk humbly, to forgive, and to place our trust in Him rather than in the things of this world.

Pride can blind us to our own shortcomings, but humility allows us to see our need for God's grace.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Are there things in this life that I am putting before God? Do I believe that self-sufficiency and how others view me are more important than relying upon Him?

2. What are some examples of times when I have allowed pride to rule my life? What were the consequences?

3. If I have a loved one in my life, do I treat that person as a possession, or do I show them the love and respect they deserve?

**Prayer**

Let us walk in forgiveness when we are wronged, showing the light of Jesus and extending the same forgiveness that You have shown us. Help us recognize when pride begins to rule our hearts. Give us the wisdom to consider the feelings of others and the humility to acknowledge our own shortcomings. Allow us always to remember that our sufficiency lies in You. You have created us, and it is by You alone that we live. Guide us in this life to do Your will. In Jesus' name we pray.
