**DAY 64 – PROMISE MADE**

**Scripture**

**Ruth 3:10-18 CSB**

Then he said, "May the LORD bless you, my daughter. You have shown more kindness now than before, because you have not pursued younger men, whether rich or poor. [11] Now don't be afraid, my daughter. I will do for you whatever you say, since all the people in my town know that you are a woman of noble character. [12] Yes, it is true that I am a family redeemer, but there is a redeemer closer than I am. [13] Stay here tonight, and in the morning, if he wants to redeem you, that's good. Let him redeem you. But if he doesn't want to redeem you, as the LORD lives, I will. Now lie down until morning." [14] So she lay down at his feet until morning but got up while it was still dark. Then Boaz said, "Don't let it be known that a woman came to the threshing floor." [15] And he told Ruth, "Bring the shawl you're wearing and hold it out." When she held it out, he shoveled six measures of barley into her shawl, and she went into the town. [16] She went to her mother-in-law, Naomi, who asked her, "What happened, my daughter?" Then Ruth told her everything the man had done for her. [17] She said, "He gave me these six measures of barley, because he said, 'Don't go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed.'" [18] Naomi said, "My daughter, wait until you find out how things go, for he won't rest unless he resolves this today."

**Protection Given**

Under the heavy blanket of stars on the threshing floor, Boaz showed his character not in words alone, but in action. He woke to find Ruth at his feet, and instead of scorn, he offered honor, calling her a "woman of noble character." He allowed her to remain until just before dawn, protecting her from the dangers of the night and the judgment of others. As morning began to brighten the sky, he sent her away with her shawl filled with six measures of barley, a generous gift that would provide food for both Ruth and Naomi. But perhaps the grain served another purpose as well. If someone saw Ruth leaving the threshing floor carrying barley, they might simply assume she had come to collect grain, never questioning why she had been there during the night. In this simple act of kindness, Boaz was providing for Ruth's needs while also protecting her reputation and dignity.

Ruth, too, was known throughout Bethlehem for her quiet dignity and devotion to Naomi. She had left Moab, its gods, and the familiarity of her own people to follow an aging widow into a foreign land. Each day she gleaned in the hot sun, gathering what she could to provide food for them both. Her loyalty was not simply expressed in words, but through her daily actions.

We see in their story a model of faith in action: putting others first, serving without expectation, and humbly trusting in God's providence. Ruth, though young enough to attract a younger suitor, followed the uncertain path Naomi had set before her. She approached Boaz in the night, asking him to take her under his protection as a family redeemer, a request that carried the hope of marriage. Her heart must have been pounding as she waited for his response, not knowing whether he would accept or reject her.

But Boaz reassured her. He promised to act as her redeemer if he was able. There was one complication: another relative had a closer claim and the first opportunity to redeem the family property. Boaz would have to approach this man before he could fulfill his promise. Still, his words gave Ruth hope that he would not abandon her.

Here is a love story built not on passion alone, but on loyalty, faithfulness, and mutual respect. When Ruth returned home and told Naomi what had happened, Naomi was confident that Boaz would not rest until he had settled the matter honorably. There was now hope that Ruth would have a home and that their family's name and inheritance might be preserved.

We also have a Redeemer in Jesus Christ. He loves us so much that He gave His life for us. Through His sacrifice, we have been redeemed from sin and brought into the family of God. Jesus tells us that He has gone to prepare a place for us, and Scripture reminds us that He intercedes for us before the Father. His promises are not empty words, but assurances grounded in His love and faithfulness.

Marriage and its sacred bonds point us toward the relationship Christ has with His Church. Just as Boaz gave Ruth a generous measure of barley as a sign of his care, Jesus has given us something far greater: the Holy Spirit, our Comforter and Helper. The Holy Spirit is also described in Scripture as the guarantee of our inheritance, assuring us that God's promises will be fulfilled.

Boaz made a promise to Ruth, and Naomi trusted that he would keep it. Jesus has made promises to us as well. He has promised never to leave or forsake us, to prepare a place for us, and to give eternal life to those who believe in Him. We may not know what tomorrow will bring, but we can rest in the assurance that our Redeemer is faithful. One day, we will see Him face to face.

**Reflection Questions**

1. In what ways can we demonstrate the loyalty, kindness, and integrity that Boaz and Ruth displayed toward one another?

2. What does Boaz's gift of six measures of barley teach us about generosity, compassion, and keeping our promises?

3. How does our covenant relationship with God reflect the sacred bonds of marriage, and how can we find comfort in the promises of Jesus Christ?
