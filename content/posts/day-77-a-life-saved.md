**DAY 77 – A LIFE SAVED**

**Scripture**

**Esther 5:1-10 CSB**

On the third day, Esther dressed in her royal clothing and stood in the inner courtyard of the palace facing it. The king was sitting on his royal throne in the royal courtroom, facing its entrance. [2] As soon as the king saw Queen Esther standing in the courtyard, she gained favor with him. The king extended the gold scepter in his hand toward Esther, and she approached and touched the tip of the scepter. [3] "What is it, Queen Esther?" the king asked her. "Whatever you want, even to half the kingdom, will be given to you." [4] "If it pleases the king," Esther replied, "may the king and Haman come today to the banquet I have prepared for them." [5] The king said, "Hurry, and get Haman so we can do as Esther has requested." So the king and Haman went to the banquet Esther had prepared. [6] While drinking the wine, the king asked Esther, "Whatever you ask will be given to you. Whatever you want, even to half the kingdom, will be done." [7] Esther answered, "This is my petition and my request: [8] If I have found favor in the eyes of the king, and if it pleases the king to grant my petition and perform my request, may the king and Haman come to the banquet I will prepare for them. Tomorrow I will do what the king has asked." [9] That day Haman left full of joy and in good spirits. But when Haman saw Mordecai at the King's Gate, and Mordecai didn't rise or tremble in fear at his presence, Haman was filled with rage toward Mordecai. [10] Yet Haman controlled himself and went home. He sent for his friends and his wife Zeresh to join him.

**Request to be Asked**

Sometimes when we read the Bible, the words play out in our minds like a movie, with the author giving us a glimpse into the past. We can almost picture the people, their surroundings, and the emotions they may have experienced. Occasionally, it is good to take a moment and imagine the scene, remembering that some of the details are our own interpretation of what Scripture describes.

The king was holding court, his profile austere against the ivory inlay of his throne. Then he turned and saw Esther standing in the courtyard. Something in his posture seemed to ease, and without a word, he extended the golden scepter toward her.

Esther stepped forward across the polished marble floor, the cool stone beneath her slippers. She approached the throne and gently touched the tip of the scepter, knowing that this simple gesture meant her life had been spared.

"What is it, Queen Esther?" the king asked. "Whatever you want, even to half the kingdom, will be given to you."

Esther lowered her eyes respectfully. "If it pleases the king, may the king and Haman come today to the banquet I have prepared for them."

The king immediately ordered that Haman be brought so they could honor Esther's request.

Later, at the banquet, wine was poured into polished cups, and the aroma of carefully prepared food filled the room. As they drank, the king again asked Esther what she desired, promising to grant her request, even to half his kingdom.

Esther paused before answering. She asked that the king and Haman return the following day for another banquet. Then, she promised, she would reveal her petition.

We are not told exactly why Esther waited until the second banquet to make her request. Perhaps she was carefully choosing the right moment, or perhaps she simply needed more time. Whatever her reason, she demonstrated patience and restraint rather than rushing into a decision that carried such serious consequences.

Meanwhile, Haman left the banquet in exceptionally good spirits. Not only had he been invited to dine with the king, but the queen herself had requested his presence. He had already enjoyed the king's confidence, and now he may have believed that he held a special place in the queen's favor as well.

There is a warning in Proverbs 16:18 that pride comes before destruction, and we can see that danger growing in Haman's heart. He was a powerful man who had received extraordinary authority from the king. Now, being invited to a private banquet with the royal couple must have seemed like another confirmation of his importance.

But his happiness did not last long.

As Haman passed through the King's Gate, he saw Mordecai. The man who had refused to bow before him still would not rise or tremble in his presence. In an instant, Haman's joy turned to rage.

How dare this man refuse to give me the honor I deserve?

We are not told Haman's exact thoughts, but his reaction reveals how deeply his pride had taken hold of him. Despite all his wealth, authority, and recognition, he could not bear the thought of one man refusing to honor him.

Yet Haman controlled his anger for the moment and returned home, where he gathered his friends and his wife, Zeresh.

This is one of the dangers of pride. We can become so concerned with our own importance that we allow resentment and jealousy to overshadow the blessings already in our lives. Haman had nearly everything a man in his position could desire, but the actions of one person were enough to rob him of his happiness.

We must guard against this same attitude in our own lives. There may be times when someone receives a promotion we hoped for, gains recognition for something we worked hard to achieve, or is given an opportunity we wanted for ourselves. How do we respond? Do we become jealous and resentful, or can we congratulate that person and offer our support?

Sometimes the situation is more painful. We may experience unfair treatment, dishonesty, or the loss of something precious to us. Anger and disappointment are natural responses, but we must be careful not to allow those feelings to grow into bitterness and a desire for revenge.

Jesus teaches us to love our enemies and pray for those who persecute us. He also tells us to turn the other cheek. These teachings are not easy to follow, especially when we believe we have been wronged. Yet if we call Jesus our Lord, we should strive to follow His example.

Forgiveness does not mean pretending that wrongdoing never happened, nor does it require us to allow someone to continue harming us. It means releasing our desire for revenge and trusting God to judge with righteousness. We can seek justice, establish necessary boundaries, and still refuse to let hatred control our hearts.

When we allow bitterness to remain, it can gradually consume our thoughts and steal the peace that God desires for us. Haman's anger toward Mordecai had already grown into a plan to destroy an entire people. His pride had become so great that he could no longer see beyond his own wounded sense of importance.

As Christians, we have a much better reason to rejoice. We are children of our heavenly Father, loved and redeemed through Jesus Christ. Our worth does not come from a position, a title, or the approval of others. It comes from belonging to Him.

And when we remember how much God has forgiven us, we should also remember how much we need to forgive others. If we look honestly at our own lives, we can probably recall times when our words or actions have hurt someone, even when that was not our intention.

We all need forgiveness, and we all need the humility to extend it.

Esther entered the king's presence with a humble request, seeking an opportunity to save her people. Haman left the same banquet thinking of his own importance, only to become consumed with anger when one man refused to honor him.

What a contrast between humility and pride.

One seeks the good of others, while the other seeks only to satisfy himself. As followers of Jesus Christ, we are called to walk in humility, serve others with love, and remember that everything we have is a gift from God.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Have you ever felt jealous when someone received an opportunity or recognition you wanted? How can you respond in a way that honors Jesus Christ?

2. Has there been a time when something wonderful happened in your life and you forgot to give God the glory? How can you remain humble when you experience success?

3. Is there someone with whom you are having difficulties today? How can you respond with forgiveness and love while still maintaining healthy boundaries?

**Prayer**

When someone receives a blessing or opportunity we wanted, give us a heart that rejoices with them instead of becoming jealous.
