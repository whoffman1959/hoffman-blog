**DAY 52 – SELF MADE**

**Rely on God**

When we get up in the morning and survey what we have gained in this world, it can be tempting to think that our wealth and successes were achieved solely by our own hands or our own righteousness. The Jewish people received a warning from Moses in Deuteronomy 8 and Deuteronomy 9 about this false belief of self-sufficiency.

Moses repeated the words of God and called the nation that was about to take possession of the promised land a stiff-necked people. He reminded them that they were not receiving the land because of their own righteousness. No, they had failed in many ways. The inhabitants of the land were being driven out because of their own wickedness. Moses then gave a warning that should remain in our hearts today:

You may say to yourself, 'My power and my own ability have gained this wealth for me,' but remember that the LORD your God gives you the power to gain wealth, in order to confirm his covenant he swore to your ancestors, as it is today.

Like the Jewish nation, we are dependent upon God. We are saved by grace; salvation is not something we have accomplished by our own works. Even our ability to come to Christ begins with God. The Father grants that we may come to the Son, and it is the Spirit who gives life. We cannot give ourselves spiritual life or save ourselves.

The Jewish people were warned that once they entered the land, they were not to become proud because of the things they had obtained. These things were not achieved by their own power and strength. They were to remember God.

Some today come to God broken and almost at the brink of destruction. God calls them to Himself, and out of His love He begins restoring their lives. Yet there can be a temptation for those who once relied completely upon God to eventually say, “Look what I have done by my own power.”

The god of self will always try to deceive us. We should never forget the One who gave us life, abilities, opportunities, and the strength to use them. Even when we work hard for what we have, we should recognize the One who gave us the ability to work in the first place.

A humble person appreciates the love of God, recognizing how completely we depend upon Him. Our very salvation and our place in the Kingdom of Heaven are not earned through our own efforts but are given through Jesus, who gave Himself for us.

Our righteousness comes through the Son. We love because He first loved us. His perfect love drives out fear. We are in Christ, and as His Spirit works within us, the love we have received from God should be seen in the way we love others.

Those who remain in God's love do not need to live in fear of judgment. They are forgiven through His beloved Son. And the same love that God has given us should flow outward in our love for our brothers and sisters in Christ. I am forgiven, so I do not need to be scared or live under the fear and doubt that come from trying to earn my own salvation.

When this life is over and we stand before the Lord, we can stand with confidence rather than fear, knowing that our sins are forgiven through Christ. God's perfect love has driven out the fear of punishment.

**Reflection Questions**

1. How is God's love made complete in our lives?

2. How can I show my love for a fellow believer who is in need or hurting? Do I truly love my brothers and sisters in Christ?

3. Do I claim that the things I have are due solely to my own efforts and strength, or do I recognize what God has provided and give Him the glory?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Please fill me with love for my brothers and sisters in Christ. Give me assurance of my salvation—not a salvation that comes from my own efforts, but one that comes through Your Son, Jesus. Thank You for the things I have been given in this life. I thank You for all things: the food on the table, the place where I lay my head at night, and the successes I have experienced in life. Allow me always to remember that behind these things are the provisions, opportunities, abilities, and talents You have given me. Let me walk in love—love for my fellow Christians and love for those who need to hear the gospel. Guard me from the pride of believing that everything I have accomplished has come from my own strength and effort. Keep me humble and dependent upon You, remembering that You loved me first. In Jesus' name.

Amen.
