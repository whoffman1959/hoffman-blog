**DAY 9 – THE GOOD SHEPARD**

**Scripture**

**John 10:10–11 (CSB)**

"A thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy. I have come so that they may have life and have it in abundance. [11] I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."

**A Friend in Jesus**

Life is a journey filled with mountaintops and valleys—seasons of laughter and seasons of tears. In those hard moments, it’s easy to wonder: Does God see me? Does He care?

Even faithful hearts sometimes wrestle with doubt. Yet Scripture tells us that Jesus came not only to save us but to give us life in abundance—a life anchored in His love, even when our circumstances shake.

Before I became a Christian—truly became a Christian, believing in my heart that Jesus is Lord and that He cares for me—I did not live the life I should have lived. Losing everything I cared about because of my own shortcomings caused me to spiral and hurt others even more. But in His grace, the Father stepped in, stopped my downfall, and showed me a better way.

Sometimes, when I look back on those dark moments, I wonder how the Father could still love me—how the Son’s sacrifice could cover my sins. Doubt creeps in, and at the root of that doubt is pride: the belief that my sin is somehow too great for Jesus’ blood to cover. But then the discouragement lifts, just as quickly as it came, and I am left with gratitude. Gratitude for a Father who loves even those who have fallen into ruin. Gratitude for a Savior whose love reaches deeper than the darkest part of my past.

When discouragement rises, it helps to pause and remember how God has carried us before. The moment we believed in Him. The times He provided, guided, or protected when we didn’t even realize it. Like the Israelites who forgot God’s miracles soon after the Exodus, we too can lose sight of His faithfulness. But the same Lord who parted the sea still parts the storms in our hearts.

The Father not only forgives our past—He renews us daily, forming us into the image of His dear Son.

Jesus calls Himself the Good Shepherd—not a distant ruler, but a friend who knows our names and walks beside us. He laid down His life for us, not out of obligation, but out of love. No matter what the day brings, we can rest knowing our Shepherd watches over us with compassion and tenderness. And the Father, who began a good work in us, will carry it to completion (Philippians 1:6).

**Reflection Questions**

1. When have I seen God’s hand at work in my life, even if I didn’t recognize it at first?

2. What “gratitude moments” can I recall today that remind me of Jesus’ care and friendship?

3. How can I learn to trust God’s presence more deeply in both joy and hardship?

**Prayer**

Loving Shepherd,

Thank You for being my friend and protector. When life feels heavy or uncertain, remind me that You are near. Help me to remember the times You have provided, healed, and guided me, even when I could not see it. Teach me to walk with gratitude and trust, knowing that You came to give life—real, abundant, everlasting life. May my heart rest in Your care today and always.

Amen.
