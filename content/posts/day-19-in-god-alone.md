**DAY 19 – IN GOD ALONE**

**Scripture**

**Psalm 62:5-6 (CSB)**

Rest in God alone, my soul, for my hope comes from him. [6] He alone is my rock and my salvation, my stronghold; I will not be shaken.

**Matthew 6:19-20 (CSB)**

"Don't store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal. [20] But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust destroys, and where thieves don't break in and steal."

**Not About Me**

When I look back over my life, I can clearly see all the opportunities for financial security that I ignored — the moments I could have saved, the reckless spending, the credit cards used for things I only thought I needed. It is so easy to chase after money, possessions, and comfort, believing they will bring happiness or give us a sense of safety.

But then I look at our Lord.

Jesus — the Son of God — did not even have a place to lay His head. He lived entirely dependent on His Father’s will and on the generosity of those faithful women who supported His ministry. His life reminds me that my faith is not measured by what I own, but by what I give… not by the comfort I accumulate, but by the love I offer… not by how secure I feel, but by whether I am doing the Lord’s work — sharing the gospel, serving others, and investing in eternal things instead of spending my days chasing self.

Jesus once told a parable about a rich farmer whose land produced abundantly. His barns overflowed, so he built bigger ones, saying to himself, “Now you can take life easy.” But that very night, his life ended — and everything he worked for slipped from his hands. “Then who will get what you have prepared for yourself?” Jesus asks.

It’s a question that echoes through every generation.

Because like that farmer, we often live with our eyes fixed on the moment instead of eternity.
“I just need to pass that test.”
“I can’t wait for that steak.”
“I’ll work a few more hours — I’m on the fast track.”

Then suddenly — with one phone call, one illness, one accident — everything shifts. The things we called important turn to mist. The plans fade. The possessions mean little. And the things we spent so much time clinging to lose their shine.

Jesus calls us to build a different kind of wealth — one that cannot be stolen, rusted, or burned away.

Eternal treasure is found in trust.
In giving.
In serving.
In loving people who cannot repay us.
In spending our lives for the glory of God.

When we depend on the Father instead of ourselves, we discover that true rest does not come from full barns or padded accounts — it comes from knowing that our lives are held in His hands. No loss can take away what He gives. Life on earth is a twinkling — a breath between two eternities. The wise learn to loosen their grip on what fades and strengthen their hold on what lasts.

To live with open hands.
To lift our eyes to eternity.
To anchor our hearts in the Father’s faithfulness.
This is the treasure that cannot be lost.

**Reflection Questions**

1. What treasures am I storing — are they temporary or eternal?

2. How can I shift my focus from worldly gain to serving and loving others for God’s glory?

3. What does “resting in God alone” look like in my daily choices and priorities?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Teach me to rest in You alone. Free my heart from the need to prove myself through success or possessions. Help me to store treasures in heaven — acts of kindness, faithfulness, and love that reflect Your heart. When I am tempted to chase after temporary things, remind me that You are my Rock, my Salvation, and my Stronghold. May my life point others toward eternity and the peace found only in You.

Amen.
