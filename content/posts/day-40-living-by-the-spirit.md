**Walking in Light**

Many of us know the old cartoon: a person with an angel on one shoulder and a devil on the other, each whispering advice. The picture is too simple, but it gets something right. Temptation does not come only at us from outside; it also rises from within, from the desires already in our heart. Our heart can turn toward the things of this world or toward God. It can chase power, revenge, riches gained at another’s expense, and all the other worldly weaknesses that breed misery—or it can seek the things of the Spirit, which bring light, love, and peace.

John states plainly that anyone who claims to belong to Christ but chases after the selfish desires of the world is not living as a child of God. Yet when we do stray, choosing our own will over His, we find forgiveness through Christ if we come to Him with a humble and contrite heart, confessing our sins.

Some justify their pursuit of worldly gain at the cost of others, claiming there is no wrong in it. They preach a doctrine of survival, where the strong thrive and the weak are cast aside. They ask, what is the harm in pleasure—in revelry, in unbridled desire, in the assertion of power? They call it natural. But we must remember: this world is fallen. Its nature is not as it was first made. Christ came to restore what was broken. As Isaiah wrote about Jesus:

Isaiah 61:1 CSB

The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on me, because the LORD has anointed me to bring good news to the poor. He has sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives and freedom to the prisoners;

Christ came to set us free from the world and all that belongs to it. He brought the good news that our sins can be forgiven and that sin’s power over us can be broken. We were prisoners—held captive by the world and by the darkness that presses in on every side, by the sin that lives in every human heart. He came to open the prison doors.

Someone wrestling with sin might say: I am too weak. I have no strength. I try not to do the wrong things, and still I do them. Most often the trouble is this: that person is fighting alone against what they hate in their life. We cannot overcome sin by our own power. It takes God living in us to win that battle.

It takes the power of the Holy Spirit, and that Spirit is given only to those who are in Christ. Many hear the truth and turn away from it. But those who humble themselves and listen—who admit their faults and confess their sins—find forgiveness from a righteous and loving Father in Heaven, forgiveness bought by His Son. And those who heed the Spirit’s voice find victory over the things that once held them captive. Yet how can anyone hear that voice who has never bowed the knee and called Jesus Lord of their life?

**Reflection Questions**

1. Is there sin in my life that I need to confess to God? Do I believe in God’s word that I am forgiven through His Son?

2. Do I fight the things of the world that have power over me through my own strength, or do I turn to the power of God? Do I go to God in prayer, asking for forgiveness and the strength to overcome?

3. Do I have a support group of other Christians around me so that I do not try to fight the things of the world on my own? Do I have others to pray for me, comfort me, and remind me that my strength is in the Lord?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

May I always follow after You, being led by Your Spirit of peace and love. Give me strength to overcome the temptations of this world. Surround me with believers to pray for me, comfort me, and remind me that my peace and strength are only in You. Take away the sin in my life so that I might better serve You and that my life might be a testimony for You. Remove jealousy, bitterness, and selfishness from my life, and let me follow after the things of the Spirit. In Jesus’ name.

Amen.
