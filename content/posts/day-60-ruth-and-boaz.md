**DAY 60 – RUTH AND BOAZ**

**Scripture**

**Ruth 2:1-12 CSB**

Now Naomi had a relative on her husband's side. He was a prominent man of noble character from Elimelech's family. His name was Boaz. [2] Ruth the Moabitess asked Naomi, "Will you let me go into the fields and gather fallen grain behind someone with whom I find favor?" Naomi answered her, "Go ahead, my daughter." [3] So Ruth left and entered the field to gather grain behind the harvesters. She happened to be in the portion of the field belonging to Boaz, who was from Elimelech's family. [4] Later, when Boaz arrived from Bethlehem, he said to the harvesters, "The LORD be with you." "The LORD bless you," they replied. [5] Boaz asked his servant who was in charge of the harvesters, "Whose young woman is this?" [6] The servant answered, "She is the young Moabite woman who returned with Naomi from the territory of Moab. [7] She asked, 'Will you let me gather fallen grain among the bundles behind the harvesters?' She came and has been on her feet since early morning, except that she rested a little in the shelter." [8] Then Boaz said to Ruth, "Listen, my daughter. Don't go and gather grain in another field, and don't leave this one, but stay here close to my female servants. [9] See which field they are harvesting, and follow them. Haven't I ordered the young men not to touch you? When you are thirsty, go and drink from the jars the young men have filled." [10] She fell facedown, bowed to the ground, and said to him, "Why have I found favor with you, so that you notice me, although I am a foreigner?" [11] Boaz answered her, "Everything you have done for your mother-in-law since your husband's death has been fully reported to me: how you left your father and mother and your native land, and how you came to a people you didn't previously know. [12] May the LORD reward you for what you have done, and may you receive a full reward from the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings you have come for refuge."

**Under His Wings**

123As we read the reflection today, we get a snapshot of who Ruth is. She left her father and mother and her native land and followed Naomi to a land that was strange to her and to a people she did not know. She devoted herself to caring for Naomi. Ruth asked permission to go into the fields and gather the grain left behind by the harvesters so that she and Naomi would have something to eat.

Then we meet Boaz, a man who believed in the Lord. He came from Bethlehem to his field, apparently to oversee the work that was being done, and greeted his workers with a blessing: “The LORD be with you.” The workers replied in kind: “The LORD bless you.”

There is a blessing that pastors often give in church that makes the heart sing:

“May the Lord bless you and protect you;
may the Lord make his face shine on you
and be gracious to you;
may the Lord look with favor on you
and give you peace.”

We are beginning to see something like this happening in the lives of Naomi and Ruth. Boaz was a relative of Naomi's deceased husband, Elimelech. He was a prominent man and was described as having noble character. When he saw Ruth gathering grain in his field, he asked his servant who she was.

He was told that she was the Moabite woman who had returned with Naomi. Boaz had already heard about Ruth and how she had left her father, mother, and homeland to remain with her mother-in-law. He was also told that Ruth had been on her feet since early that morning, resting only briefly in the shelter. Ruth was working hard to gather enough grain for herself and Naomi.

In life, the good that we do often seems to go unnoticed, while our failures can quickly become the things people remember and talk about. People love rumors, especially the bad ones. But the report that had reached Boaz about Ruth was a good one. Her faithfulness to Naomi and the sacrifices she had made were known by others.

Boaz then spoke directly to Ruth. He was concerned about her welfare and provided what protection he could. He told her to remain close to his female servants and ordered the young men not to touch her. He also gave her permission to drink from the water his workers had drawn. Ruth had come into the field looking for enough grain to survive, but she found food, water, and protection.

Ruth knew that she was a foreigner and wondered why Boaz would show her such kindness. Boaz told her that he knew what she had done for Naomi. Then he gave Ruth a beautiful blessing:

“May the LORD reward you for what you have done, and may you receive a full reward from the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings you have come for refuge.”

Ruth had left her homeland and the gods of Moab behind. She had told Naomi, “Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God.” Now Boaz recognized what Ruth had done. She had come to the God of Israel for refuge.

Yesterday, Naomi returned to Bethlehem believing that she was empty. But God was already beginning to provide. Ruth went into a field looking for leftover grain and found herself in the field of a relative of Elimelech who was a man of noble character. Ruth did not know where that simple decision to gather grain would eventually lead.

In life, we are given opportunities to lift people up rather than tear them down. There will be foreigners, widows, orphans, and others around us who need care. Both the Old and New Testaments speak plainly about God's concern for the vulnerable, and His people should share that concern. Like Boaz, we can offer more than kind words when God has given us the ability to help. We can notice people whom others may overlook and provide what help we are able to give.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Do you know a foreigner, widow, orphan, or someone else in your community whom you could bless, both through your words and by helping with a need they may have?

2. How do you think the workers in Boaz's field viewed Ruth as she gathered grain behind them?

3. Why did Boaz look favorably upon Ruth, and what does his treatment of her teach us about how we should treat others?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

May we care for widows and orphans and also for the foreigners in our land. Give us wisdom to look at others with compassionate hearts and to see the good in people rather than always looking for their faults. Thank You for the gifts You have given us in this life so that we might use them to help others. Be with those who are caring for the people they love, sometimes without having enough themselves. Give us eyes to see the needs of others more clearly and the wisdom to know how we can be a blessing in their lives. Just as Ruth came under Your wings for refuge, remind us that our refuge is also found in You. In Jesus' name we lift up this prayer.

Amen.
