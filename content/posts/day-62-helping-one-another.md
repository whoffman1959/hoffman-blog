**DAY 62 – HELPING ONE ANOTHER**

**Scripture**

**Ruth 2:19-23 CSB**

Her mother-in-law said to her, "Where did you gather barley today, and where did you work? May the LORD bless the man who noticed you." Ruth told her mother-in-law whom she had worked with and said, "The name of the man I worked with today is Boaz." [20] Then Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, "May the LORD bless him because he has not abandoned his kindness to the living or the dead." Naomi continued, "The man is a close relative. He is one of our family redeemers." [21] Ruth the Moabitess said, "He also told me, 'Stay with my young men until they have finished all of my harvest.'" [22] So Naomi said to her daughter-in-law Ruth, "My daughter, it is good for you to work with his female servants, so that nothing will happen to you in another field." [23] Ruth stayed close to Boaz's female servants and gathered grain until the barley and the wheat harvests were finished. And she lived with her mother-in-law.

**Family Redeemer**

A family redeemer was a close male relative who could step in and protect the interests of family members who had fallen into poverty, lost property, or suffered other hardships.

There was a time when people grew up in a town surrounded by family and stayed in that town for the rest of their lives. Generation after generation experienced life together in the same community. Families knew one another and were there to help when someone was in need. It was a close community, something that has become less common with our ability to travel around the world and move from one city to another for work.

Naomi had left her home and the support of her community with her husband. She had been gone for more than ten years, but when she returned, the people still remembered her.

Some of us long for our childhood homes and the security they brought. Sometimes, when we return, we find that home is no longer the same. The people we knew have moved away or passed on, and the familiar places have changed. That is when a feeling of emptiness can set in. We have been gone too long, and the home we remember is no longer there.

But as Christians, we still have a home. We have an eternal home in Heaven waiting for us, and even now, through Jesus Christ, we belong to the family of God. We are never truly without a home.

God was with Naomi as she traveled back to her ancestral home. When Ruth returned after a hard day's work carrying food for them to eat, Naomi must have been surprised and delighted. She asked, "Where did you gather barley today?"
Not everyone in a community is kind, and Naomi realized that someone had shown Ruth extraordinary kindness. We also see that Naomi's faith was still present, even after the terrible loss of her husband and sons. She said, "May the LORD bless the man who noticed you." Despite all she had suffered, Naomi still called upon the name of the LORD.

When Ruth told her that the man's name was Boaz, Naomi blessed him again. She recognized that he was a close relative and one of their family redeemers. Boaz had already shown kindness and protection to Ruth, and Naomi now understood that he was someone who might be able to help their family in an even greater way.

I have had people who were like family redeemers in my own life when my parents died. What a blessing it is to have people gather around us and help carry the pain when we suffer a loss. Their love and support can give us strength when we feel we have none left.

Some of us do not have an earthly family to turn to, but as Christians, we have a heavenly family. When we gather with other believers, we find fathers and mothers, brothers and sisters, and younger members of the family of God. This fellowship can become a source of comfort, encouragement, and strength.
Jesus spoke about this in Luke 18:29–30:

So he said to them, "Truly I tell you, there is no one who has left a house, wife or brothers or sisters, parents or children because of the kingdom of God, who will not receive many times more at this time, and eternal life in the age to come."

Naomi knew that Ruth would be safer working in the fields of Boaz, so she encouraged her to remain there with his female servants. Ruth continued gathering grain through both the barley and wheat harvests, under the protection and kindness of a man who was part of Naomi's family.

Just as Boaz cared for Ruth and Naomi, we have opportunities to care for the people God places in our lives. We may not be family redeemers under the Law of Moses, but we can still help those who are struggling, comfort those who are grieving, and provide a sense of belonging to those who feel alone.

And we have a Redeemer today. Our Redeemer as Christians is Jesus Christ. He paid the price for our sins through His sacrifice so that we might have eternal life. Through Him, we have become part of God's family, with a home that will never be taken away.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Is there someone in our family, a close neighbor, or a Christian brother or sister who needs our help today?

2. Where is our home today? Do we spend our time looking back at what we lost when we were younger, or are we looking forward to our heavenly home?

3. How can I serve others in the community where I live?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for surrounding me with family and friends when I was younger. Thank You for the many blessings You have given me throughout this life. Protect my family members and watch over them, not only my relatives but also my family in Jesus Christ. Let there be a bond of love without division, so that we may faithfully support and care for one another. If there is someone who has lost that feeling of home in their life, or who never grew up surrounded by love, let them find that love and support among Your people. Help us to open our hearts to those who are lonely, grieving, or in need, so that they may experience the love of Christ through us. Remind us that our earthly homes may change, but our eternal home with You will never fade away. Lord Jesus, be with us always. It is in Your name we pray.

Amen.
