**DAY 79 – CANNOT SLEEP**

**Scripture**

**Esther 6:1-14 CSB**

That night sleep escaped the king, so he ordered the book recording daily events to be brought and read to the king. [2] They found the written report of how Mordecai had informed on Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the entrance, when they planned to assassinate King Ahasuerus. [3] The king inquired, "What honor and special recognition have been given to Mordecai for this act?" The king's personal attendants replied, "Nothing has been done for him." [4] The king asked, "Who is in the court?" Now Haman was just entering the outer court of the palace to ask the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows he had prepared for him. [5] The king's attendants answered him, "Haman is there, standing in the court." "Have him enter," the king ordered. [6] Haman entered, and the king asked him, "What should be done for the man the king wants to honor?" Haman thought to himself, "Who is it the king would want to honor more than me?" [7] Haman told the king, "For the man the king wants to honor: [8] Have them bring a royal garment that the king himself has worn and a horse the king himself has ridden, which has a royal crown on its head. [9] Put the garment and the horse under the charge of one of the king's most noble officials. Have them clothe the man the king wants to honor, parade him on the horse through the city square, and call out before him, 'This is what is done for the man the king wants to honor.'" [10] The king told Haman, "Hurry, and do just as you proposed. Take a garment and a horse for Mordecai the Jew, who is sitting at the King's Gate. Do not leave out anything you have suggested." [11] So Haman took the garment and the horse. He clothed Mordecai and paraded him through the city square, calling out before him, "This is what is done for the man the king wants to honor." [12] Then Mordecai returned to the King's Gate, but Haman hurried off for home, mournful and with his head covered. [13] Haman told his wife Zeresh and all his friends everything that had happened. His advisers and his wife Zeresh said to him, "Since Mordecai is Jewish, and you have begun to fall before him, you won't overcome him, because your downfall is certain." [14] While they were still speaking with him, the king's eunuchs arrived and rushed Haman to the banquet Esther had prepared.

**Quiet Miracle**

When we read Jesus' words in Matthew chapter 10, we learn that two sparrows can be purchased for very little money, yet not one falls to the ground apart from our Heavenly Father's knowledge and care. We are also so well known to God that even the hairs on our heads are numbered.

In Zephaniah 3:17, we read:

“The LORD your God is among you, a warrior who saves. He will rejoice over you with gladness. He will be quiet in his love. He will delight in you with singing.”

Many people expect miracles to be grand events, something extraordinary that everyone can see. Yet when we look back over our lives, we may recognize times when God was working quietly through ordinary circumstances. Something that seemed insignificant at the time may have made a tremendous difference later.

King Ahasuerus could not sleep. Our reading today does not tell us that God prevented him from sleeping, but considering what was about to happen to Mordecai, I believe we can see God's hand at work.

The king did something familiar to many of us when sleep escapes us. He had something brought to him to read, or in his case, to have read aloud. Perhaps listening to the records of his kingdom would help pass the time until he could sleep.

And what was read to him? The account of Mordecai exposing a plot by two royal officials to assassinate the king. Mordecai had reported the conspiracy through Queen Esther, and the king's life had been saved.

The king asked what honor or recognition Mordecai had received for his actions. His attendants answered that nothing had been done for him.

Consider the timing. A king unable to sleep, a record of past events being read, and the discovery that a man who had saved the king's life had never been rewarded. Meanwhile, Haman had constructed a gallows and was preparing to ask permission to execute that very man.

What appeared to be an ordinary sleepless night was about to change everything.

Haman arrived early at the palace, eager to receive permission to hang Mordecai. But before he could present his request, the king summoned him and asked a simple question: What should be done for the man the king wishes to honor?

Haman, filled with pride, immediately assumed the king was speaking about him. After all, who else could the king possibly want to honor more?

He imagined a magnificent public display. The honored man should wear a royal garment that the king himself had worn and ride a horse belonging to the king, with a royal crown on its head. One of the king's most noble officials should lead the horse through the city square, proclaiming to everyone that this was how the king honored a man.

What an opportunity for Haman to receive even greater recognition!

Then came the words that shattered his expectations. The king ordered Haman to do everything he had suggested for Mordecai the Jew, who was sitting at the King's Gate. He was not to leave out a single detail.

How quickly pride can lead to humiliation! Haman had arrived expecting to arrange Mordecai's execution, but instead found himself carrying out the king's command to honor him.

Imagine Haman leading Mordecai through the streets, proclaiming before the people, “This is what is done for the man the king wants to honor.”

Afterward, Mordecai returned to his place at the King's Gate, while Haman hurried home in mourning, his head covered in shame.

When he told his wife Zeresh and his advisers what had happened, their response was very different from the encouragement they had given him the day before. They warned that since Mordecai was Jewish and Haman had already begun to fall before him, his downfall was certain.

Perhaps they recognized something about the history of the Jewish people and their God. Whatever their reasoning, their confidence in Haman's success had disappeared.

Before they could say much more, the king's eunuchs arrived to hurry Haman to the banquet Queen Esther had prepared.

What a remarkable turn of events! Haman's plans had begun to unravel, not through a great battle or a public demonstration of power, but through a sleepless night and the reading of an old record.

I believe this passage reminds us that God does not need extraordinary circumstances to accomplish His purposes. He can work through the smallest details of everyday life, often without our realizing what He is doing.

There may be times when we look back and recognize how an unexpected delay, a conversation, or a change in circumstances affected the direction of our lives. We may not always know whether a particular event was God's direct intervention, but we can trust that nothing escapes His knowledge.

That does not mean we will always be spared suffering or that every difficulty will end as Mordecai's did. Many faithful people have endured great hardships. Yet God's love for His children is not measured by how easily life goes for them.

Jesus reminds us that our Heavenly Father knows even the smallest details about us. If He cares for the sparrows, how much more does He care for those who belong to Him?

And when our earthly journey comes to an end, we have a promise far greater than any honor a king could bestow. Through the sacrifice of Jesus Christ, we have the gift of salvation and the hope of eternal life with our Heavenly Father.
God knows those who are His. Even in our darkest hours, when we cannot see what lies ahead, we can place our trust in His love and care.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Can you recall a time when something small or unexpected made a significant difference in your life? Looking back, can you see how God may have been working through those circumstances?

2. What other examples can you think of, either in Scripture or in your own experience, where God worked through ordinary events to accomplish something important?

3. Do you truly believe that God cares about you personally, even knowing the number of hairs on your head? How does that knowledge help you trust Him during difficult times?
