**DAY 31 – BUILDING OUR FAITH**

**Scripture**

**2 Peter 1:5-11 CSB**

For this very reason, make every effort to supplement your faith with goodness, goodness with knowledge, [6] knowledge with self-control, self-control with endurance, endurance with godliness, [7] godliness with brotherly affection, and brotherly affection with love. [8] For if you possess these qualities in increasing measure, they will keep you from being useless or unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ. [9] The person who lacks these things is blind and shortsighted and has forgotten the cleansing from his past sins. [10] Therefore, brothers and sisters, make every effort to confirm your calling and election, because if you do these things you will never stumble. [11] For in this way, entry into the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ will be richly provided for you.

**Precious Promises**

It is estimated that there are about thirty thousand promises in the Bible. Along with these promises, Scripture also shows us the things that are precious to our Heavenly Father. As we read the letters of Peter, we reflected on how our faith is more precious than gold. We read about the blood of Christ shed at Calvary and how precious His blood and broken body were for our forgiveness. We understood that Jesus is the Living Stone of God, precious in the Father’s sight and the cornerstone of the church. God also taught us that a gentle and quiet spirit is precious in His sight. These are all precious things, and now, in 2 Peter, Peter turns our attention to some of the precious promises of God and what they mean for our faith.

There was a person who had sin in their life who kept shouting to others, “I cannot stop doing this thing.” As this person repeatedly asked others for help in finding a solution, someone finally said, “I know that you cannot stop doing this thing. What you need to understand is that although you do not have the power, God has the power. Will you go to Him in prayer and ask for the strength to overcome? Have you truly given your life to Him?”

The power to live a holy life comes from God, who has called us. His divine power is the source of our ability to live a life that honors Him, and Peter tells us that this comes through knowing Him. The more we get to know the Father through the Son, the more we desire to become like Christ. How precious it is to have faith that God is good and that His Word is true. How wonderful it is to spend time reading the Bible and in prayer, asking for help and direction. How encouraging it is to gather with other believers during the week and on our day of worship, to be strengthened by other Christians and to listen to the Word of God.

We have the promise that God has given us everything we need for life and godliness. Because God has given us what we need, we must be diligent to cultivate this life of faith. Peter tells us to make our calling and election sure. There is to be a real desire on our part for fellowship with the Lord and a life that demonstrates the reality of our faith.

As we read our reflection today, we see that Peter is writing to Christians who already believe, and he is telling them to grow in their faith. We have many examples in the Bible of people who were human just like us. We can read their stories and see examples of faith, obedience, failure, repentance, and perseverance. There is the courage of David, along with his confession of sin and reliance on God for forgiveness. There is Solomon, who sought wisdom and knowledge from the Lord, although his later life also reminds us of the danger of turning away from God. There is the perseverance of Job in distress, and the godliness of Daniel, who refused to take part in the things of the world even when doing so placed his life at risk. There is the brotherly affection of Jonathan, who showed great kindness and loyalty to David.

We are called to learn from those who went before us, to repent of our empty way of life, and to grow in our faith. Jesus is the source of life and forgiveness for the empty way of life we had before we believed. The Father has showered His grace upon us through the Son. We are forgiven, and we experience the goodness of God. That goodness should be reflected in our own actions. But this is just the beginning. We are to add to our faith a thirst for knowledge of the One who saved us. How precious it is to spend time in God’s written Word and with other Christians, learning about God through His Word and worship.

Knowledge of God is more than just an intellectual exercise. It is to have His Word take root in our hearts and influence our actions. From this knowledge, we are to add self-control. We pause and ask God to give us the power to respond as Christ would in our daily walk. If temptation is knocking at our door, we ask God for strength in prayer, listen to the Father through His Word, and allow the Holy Spirit to work in our lives and strengthen us.

We cannot overcome temptation in our own strength. As we continue to grow in self-control, we develop endurance, and through endurance we grow in godliness. From godliness, we show brotherly affection toward one another, and that affection grows into love. These qualities are not a formula for earning salvation. Rather, they are evidence of a growing faith in Christ.

As these qualities increase, our faith becomes fruitful and useful to the Lord. We are able to bear true Christian fruit as a blessing to others. We are able to see more clearly through the light and guidance of the Holy Spirit and are less controlled by our own worldly passions.

We are saved by grace through faith, but our faith should produce a life that increasingly reflects the One in whom we have placed our faith. Let us make every effort to grow in our faith and deepen our love for the Lord and for one another. Let us increase in faith so that, when our earthly journey is finished, we may hear Jesus say, “Well done, good and faithful servant.”

**Reflection Questions**

1. Where is my faith today? Am I showing the courage of David while also being willing to confess my sins and rely on God for forgiveness? Do I seek godly wisdom and knowledge from the Lord as Solomon did? Do I persevere through difficult times as Job did? Do I stand for God, as Daniel did, even when the world around me tells me to compromise my faith?

2. Am I trying to become godly in my own strength, as though I could work my way into God's favor, or have I accepted Jesus as the sacrifice for my sin and have I turned to God in prayer and His Word for the strength to overcome sin in my life?

3. Is my faith in the Lord real? Is Christ truly at work in my life? Is my faith producing the qualities that Peter tells me to pursue?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Please give me the strength and knowledge to grow in my faith. Guide me through knowledge of You and give me a desire to become more like Your Son, Jesus, each day. Help me to truly ask what Jesus would do in a situation and give me the power through Your Spirit to walk as Jesus walked. Do not let me play the academic game of having knowledge about You only in my mind, but let Your Word travel deeply into my heart and affect the way I live. Allow me to grow in faith, goodness, knowledge, self-control, endurance, godliness, and love each day. Let my life give evidence of the calling You have placed upon me. Let me bear fruit for You, and when my days are over, let Jesus say to me, “Well done, good and faithful servant.”

Amen.
