**DAY 88 – TRUST IN JESUS**

**Scripture**

**Matthew 14:29-36 CSB**

He said, "Come." And climbing out of the boat, Peter started walking on the water and came toward Jesus. [30] But when he saw the strength of the wind, he was afraid, and beginning to sink he cried out, "Lord, save me!" [31] Immediately Jesus reached out his hand, caught hold of him, and said to him, "You of little faith, why did you doubt?" [32] When they got into the boat, the wind ceased. [33] Then those in the boat worshiped him and said, "Truly you are the Son of God." [34] When they had crossed over, they came to shore at Gennesaret. [35] When the men of that place recognized him, they alerted the whole vicinity and brought to him all who were sick. [36] They begged him that they might only touch the end of his robe, and as many as touched it were healed.

**Doubt and Fear**

Two of the greatest enemies of our peace and trust in God are doubt and fear. We pray, asking the Lord to help us through a difficult situation, and sometimes we begin to see things change. But then our minds take over. We try to rationalize what is happening. Perhaps things are improving because of our own efforts, or perhaps the problem is simply too great for even God to handle. Before long, doubt begins to creep into our hearts, and the hope we once held so firmly starts to fade.

We see something similar in Peter's experience on the Sea of Galilee. He had just witnessed the miracle of Jesus feeding five thousand men, along with women and children, using only five loaves of bread and two fish. When everyone had eaten, the disciples gathered twelve baskets of leftovers. What an extraordinary demonstration of God's power! Afterward, Jesus sent His disciples across the sea while He went up the mountain alone to pray.

The Sea of Galilee is approximately thirteen miles long and eight miles wide, with depths reaching around 140 feet. Although called a sea, it is actually a freshwater lake surrounded by hills and higher terrain. Winds can rush down through the surrounding valleys, creating sudden and powerful waves. Even experienced fishermen knew these waters could become dangerous.

Now imagine being in that small boat. It is the early morning hours, still dark, and the disciples have been struggling against the wind. Their arms must have grown weary from rowing as waves battered the boat. Then, through the darkness, they see a figure approaching them across the water. Someone is walking toward them where no person should be able to walk! Their minds race, and fear takes hold. They cry out, believing they have seen a ghost.

But it is Jesus, and He immediately reassures them, telling them to take courage and not be afraid.

Peter, perhaps still trying to understand what he is seeing, calls out, asking Jesus to command him to come onto the water if it is truly Him.

Jesus simply tells him to come.

Think about that moment. The wind is still blowing, the waves are still rising, and Peter is standing at the edge of the boat. Yet he trusts the voice of his Lord enough to step out onto the water. And something extraordinary happens. Peter begins walking toward Jesus! For those precious moments, he is doing something no ordinary man could ever accomplish through his own strength.

But then Peter becomes aware of the strength of the wind. Perhaps a wave rises beside him, or the spray of the water strikes his face. Whatever draws his attention, fear begins to overcome his confidence. His trust falters, and he starts to sink.

Peter knows where his help must come from. He cries out, "Lord, save me!"

And what does Jesus do? He does not stand there waiting for Peter to demonstrate stronger faith. He does not turn away because Peter has doubted. Scripture tells us that Jesus immediately reaches out His hand and catches him. Even as He questions Peter's doubt, He holds him securely. When they climb back into the boat, the wind ceases, and the disciples worship Jesus, recognizing Him as the Son of God.

How often do we find ourselves in Peter's place? We begin with confidence in the Lord, but then the troubles of life rise around us. A frightening diagnosis, the loss of someone we love, financial difficulties, or uncertainty about tomorrow can leave us feeling as though we are sinking beneath the waves. We know that God is powerful, yet sometimes our fears seem more real to us than His promises.

And there is something else I find comforting in today's Scripture. After crossing the sea, Jesus arrives at Gennesaret, where people bring their sick to Him. They beg to touch even the edge of His robe, and those who do are healed. On the water, Peter reaches out to Jesus for rescue. On the shore, the sick reach toward Him for healing. In both places, we see people turning to the One who has the power to save.

Our Heavenly Father has never promised that we will travel through life without storms. There will be times when our faith is tested and our strength seems to fail. But Jesus has not abandoned us to face those difficulties alone. We can bring our fears, our doubts, and our deepest troubles before Him in prayer, trusting in His wisdom and love even when the answers are not what we expected.

Peter did not save himself by walking on the water. He was saved by the hand of Jesus reaching out to catch him.

When the storms of life threaten to overwhelm us, may we remember where our help comes from. We can cry out to our Lord, trusting that He hears us and will never forsake those who belong to Him.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Peter stepped out of the boat trusting Jesus, but fear overcame him when he noticed the wind and waves. Have there been times when the troubles around you caused you to lose sight of God's promises?

2. When Peter began to sink, he immediately called upon Jesus for help. When you face difficulties, do you first turn to the Lord in prayer, or do you struggle to solve everything through your own strength?

3. Jesus reached out and caught Peter even though his faith had faltered. How does knowing that Christ is compassionate toward us in our weakness help you face the uncertainties of life?
