**DAY 67 – STORY OF ESTHER**

**Scripture**

**Esther 1:1-9 CSB**

These events took place during the days of Ahasuerus, who ruled 127 provinces from India to Cush. [2] In those days King Ahasuerus reigned from his royal throne in the fortress at Susa. [3] He held a feast in the third year of his reign for all his officials and staff, the army of Persia and Media, the nobles, and the officials from the provinces. [4] He displayed the glorious wealth of his kingdom and the magnificent splendor of his greatness for a total of 180 days. [5] At the end of this time, the king held a week-long banquet in the garden courtyard of the royal palace for all the people, from the greatest to the least, who were present in the fortress of Susa. [6] White and blue linen hangings were fastened with fine white and purple linen cords to silver rods on marble columns. Gold and silver couches were arranged on a mosaic pavement of red feldspar, marble, mother-of-pearl, and precious stones. [7] Drinks were served in an array of gold goblets, each with a different design. Royal wine flowed freely, according to the king's bounty. [8] The drinking was according to royal decree: "There are no restrictions." The king had ordered every wine steward in his household to serve whatever each person wanted. [9] Queen Vashti also gave a feast for the women of King Ahasuerus's palace.

**Wealth and Power**

The book of Esther takes place during the Persian Empire, which ruled over a vast territory that included parts of many modern countries, including Iran. Although the author and exact date of writing are uncertain, the events introduce us to a remarkable story of God's protection and faithfulness.

As we journey through Esther, we will see how God works through ordinary people placed in extraordinary circumstances. Even when His presence is not immediately apparent, He remains aware of what is happening to His people. We will see how Esther is used to protect the Jewish nation from destruction.

Our reflection begins with a mighty ruler named Ahasuerus, generally identified as Xerxes the Great. He ruled over 127 provinces stretching from India to Cush, a vast empire filled with wealth, power, and influence.

In the third year of his reign, Ahasuerus held an enormous celebration. For 180 days, he displayed the wealth and splendor of his kingdom before his officials, military leaders, and nobles. When this period ended, he held another banquet lasting seven days for everyone present in the fortress of Susa, from the greatest to the least.

The reason for these celebrations is not given in Scripture. Perhaps the king wanted to demonstrate his power, strengthen the loyalty of his officials, or prepare for an upcoming military campaign. Whatever the reason, his wealth and authority were on full display.

The banquet itself must have been an extraordinary sight. White and blue linen hangings decorated the courtyard, supported by marble columns and silver rods. Gold and silver couches rested on beautiful mosaic pavement. Drinks were served in golden goblets, each with its own intricate design. Royal wine flowed freely.
According to Esther 1:8, there were no restrictions placed on drinking. Each person could drink according to his own desires. By the seventh day, the king himself was under the influence of wine, and his decisions would soon have consequences.

How often do we see people become consumed by wealth, power, pleasure, and the things of this world? What begins as something enjoyable can become something that controls us. The desire for more can gradually replace gratitude for what we already have.

Alcohol is one example. A celebration among friends can become an occasion for excess. Judgment becomes clouded, passions are stirred, and words are spoken that we wish we could take back. Decisions made under the influence can lead to consequences that remain long after the celebration has ended.

Proverbs 23:31–32 warns us about the deceptive attraction of wine. It may look inviting and go down smoothly, but in the end it can bite like a snake and sting like a viper.

In the verses following Proverbs 23:32, Solomon continues describing the terrible effects of drunkenness. Our eyes begin to see strange things, our tongues speak words we would never say while sober, and our judgment becomes clouded. We stumble and stagger like a sailor trying to keep his footing on the deck of a ship tossed about by a violent storm. The world seems to move beneath our feet, and we struggle to remain upright. We may wake the next morning with bruises we cannot explain, memories of conversations we cannot recall, and regrets over things we said or did. Our heads pound, our bodies ache, and we promise ourselves that we will never drink like that again.

Yet Proverbs describes something even more troubling. Despite the pain, embarrassment, and consequences, the person wakes up wanting another drink. The very thing that caused the suffering becomes the thing they seek again. What began as pleasure has become bondage, and the desire for alcohol has taken control.

What a powerful picture of the bondage that can come from allowing something to control our lives.

As Christians, we are called to live with sober minds and self-control. We are to guard our tongues, consider our actions, and glorify God in all that we do. This does not mean we cannot enjoy the good things God has provided. It means we must never allow those gifts to become more important than the One who gave them to us.

The same principle applies beyond alcohol. Wealth can become an obsession. Power can lead to pride. Entertainment, possessions, and even the desire for recognition can gradually draw our attention away from God.

The king of Persia possessed extraordinary wealth and authority, yet his riches could not guarantee wisdom or good judgment. Surrounded by luxury and celebration, he was still capable of making foolish decisions.

We can become intoxicated by the things of this world and forget who we are in Christ. Overcoming these temptations begins with remembering that God is the Creator and that the gifts of this world must never become more important to us than the One who provides them.

We should enjoy God's blessings with gratitude, but never become slaves to the blessings themselves.

**Reflection Questions**

1. What things in this world can draw us away from God and His purpose for our lives?

2. How can we overcome the temptation to misuse the gifts God has given us and prevent those gifts from controlling us?

3. Are we chasing after the things of this world, or are we seeking the things of God?
