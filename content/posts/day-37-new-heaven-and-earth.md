**DAY 37 – NEW HEAVEN AND EARTH**

**Scripture**

**2 Peter 3:10-13 CSB**

**Redemption**

Sometimes when we read the Bible, we can see only doom and gloom if we do not understand the love of God. A perfect example of this is found in the Garden of Eden.

Adam and Eve lived in the paradise God had created. There was no guilt or shame, and they enjoyed fellowship with their Creator. Sin had not yet entered human life. God had provided abundantly for them and had given them everything they needed.

But the deceiver came—that ancient serpent, the one who twisted the words of God. He made the blessings God had already given seem insufficient. Adam and Eve were tempted by what they did not have. They wanted the knowledge of good and evil and chose their own way rather than trusting the command of their Creator. It was rebellion against God and a desire for self-rule.

God created the world, the garden, and all that it contained. He lavished His gifts upon Adam and Eve, but they chose to disobey Him. God had every right to judge them. Yet He did not immediately destroy them. He clothed them with animal skins and sent them from the garden, away from the tree of life.

The story could have ended there—humanity separated from God because of sin and awaiting judgment. But our loving Creator did not abandon His creation. God would provide the way for us to be reconciled to Him, not through the skins of animals, but through the atoning sacrifice of His Son Jesus. Sin and death entered through Adam, but grace and life have come through Christ. Through Him, we can be brought back into fellowship with God.

Shalom is more than a word of greeting and farewell. It is richer than our English word peace. Its Hebrew root carries the ideas of wholeness, completeness, well-being, soundness, and things being as they ought to be. The Garden of Eden before the Fall gives us a beautiful theological picture of shalom.

Through Jesus Christ, we who believe have peace with God and have been reconciled to Him. Yet we still live in a fallen creation and wait for the fullness of what God has promised. Peter tells us that one day there will be new heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwells. What was broken by sin will not remain broken forever. We will experience the fullness of shalom with God.

Many, however, reject the gift of God, just as Adam and Eve rejected God's command and ate the forbidden fruit.

The Bible warns us about the Day of the Lord. It will come unexpectedly, and the earthly things we have accumulated will pass away. But this is not the end of the story. Just as God preserved Noah and his family through the Flood, those who belong to Christ have an eternal hope. Peter tells us that we are waiting for new heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwells.

After Peter spoke to the Israelites gathered at Pentecost, they were pierced to the heart and asked, “Brothers, what should we do?” The answer pointed them to Jesus. Peter said:

"Repent and be baptized, each of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. For the promise is for you and for your children, and for all who are far off, as many as the Lord our God will call."

Our sin and shame are not something we can cover ourselves. Jesus became the atoning sacrifice for our rebellion against God. Through His blood we receive forgiveness, and through faith in Him we are reconciled to the Father. All who are in Christ have an eternal hope—not in earthly possessions that will pass away, but in the Lord and in the new creation He has promised.

Peter's message should therefore change how we live today. If everything we accumulate in this world will eventually pass away, what kind of people should we be? Peter answers that question for us: people of holy conduct and godliness who are waiting for the Day of God. Our hope is not in preserving everything we have here. Our hope is in the One who is making all things new.

**Reflection Questions**

1.	Is there an earthly desire or possession that I want more than a relationship with God?

2.	How does knowing that the earth and the things I accumulate will pass away change the way I think about hoarding possessions rather than caring for others?

3.	If my life on this earth ended tonight, would I be prepared to meet the Lord?


**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You that You have not left us to the consequences of our rebellion against You. Thank You for providing the way back into fellowship with You through Your saving grace. Through Your Son Jesus, we have peace with You and the hope of the complete restoration that is still to come. Guard our hearts and keep us close to You. Let our eyes be fixed on heavenly things rather than the riches of this world that can so easily corrupt our hearts. Keep us in Your way, show us the path, and make our feet sure. Let people hear the message of salvation as those who heard Peter at Pentecost. May they turn to Your Son Jesus in faith and be saved. In the name of Jesus we pray.

Amen.
