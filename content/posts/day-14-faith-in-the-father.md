**DAY 14 – FAITH IN THE FATHER**

**Scripture**

**James 1:6 (CSB)**

"But let him ask in faith without doubting. For the doubter is like the surging sea, driven and tossed by the wind."

**Matthew 14:29–30 (CSB)**

"He said, ‘Come.’ And climbing out of the boat, Peter started walking on the water and came toward Jesus. [30] But when he saw the strength of the wind, he was afraid, and beginning to sink he cried out, ‘Lord, save me!’"

**Fighting Doubt**

As humans, we are a forgetful people. The Israelites often struggled with this. God brought them out of Egypt, parting the Red Sea so they could walk on dry ground. He destroyed their enemies, fed them in the wilderness, gave them water from the rock, and led them by fire and cloud. Yet even after all these miracles, they doubted. When Pharaoh’s army approached, they feared. In the desert, they longed to return to slavery. Their journey through the wilderness—forty long years—was marked by doubt in God’s provision.

In my own life, faithfulness to the Father seems to ebb and flow like the tide. When I am walking by the Spirit—prayed up, grounded in the Word, and engaged in fellowship with other believers—faith feels natural. But when I become spiritually lazy, when I think, I’ve got this, and walk in my own strength, the world quickly presses in. Doubts arise, distractions creep in, and the things of this world begin to overshadow the things of God.

In Deuteronomy 6:5–8, we are told to love the LORD with all our heart, to talk about His Word constantly, and to bind His commands to our hands and foreheads. Scripture is a powerful tool against doubt—but so is remembering our own lived experience with God. If I would simply write down what the Father has done for me in the everyday moments, I could return to those reminders when doubt tries to take hold.

James reminds us that when we ask, we must ask in faith. The one who doubts is like a wave of the sea—restless and unstable. Peter learned this lesson firsthand. He walked on water toward Jesus, but when fear made him shift his focus, he began to sink. Even then, in that very moment of weakness, Jesus reached out and saved him.

Faith does not mean we never feel afraid—it means we choose to trust God even when we do. We grow stronger in faith by remembering what God has already done for us. One powerful way to do this is through journaling. By writing down the ways God has provided, guided, and answered prayer, we build a record of His faithfulness. Then, when doubt creeps in, we can look back and remember:

God has never failed us yet.

**Reflection Questions**

1. When have I struggled with doubt, and how did God show His faithfulness in that situation?

2. What “storms” in my life cause me to take my eyes off Jesus?

3. How can I make a habit of remembering and recording God’s answers to prayer to strengthen my faith?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for being patient with me when doubt clouds my faith. Like Peter, I sometimes take my eyes off You and begin to sink under fear or worry. Yet You are always near, ready to lift me up and remind me of Your power and love. Strengthen my faith each day, Lord. Help me to trust You completely, to remember Your goodness, and to walk boldly even when the winds rise around me.

Amen.
