**DAY 78 – GALLOWS CONSTRUCTED**

**Scripture**

**Esther 5:11-14 CSB**

Then Haman described for them his glorious wealth and his many sons. He told them all how the king had honored him and promoted him in rank over the other officials and the royal staff. [12] "What's more," Haman added, "Queen Esther invited no one but me to join the king at the banquet she had prepared. I am invited again tomorrow to join her with the king. [13] Still, none of this satisfies me since I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the King's Gate all the time." [14] His wife Zeresh and all his friends told him, "Have them build a gallows seventy-five feet tall. Ask the king in the morning to hang Mordecai on it. Then go to the banquet with the king and enjoy yourself." The advice pleased Haman, so he had the gallows constructed.

**Attempt to Destroy**

We have many examples in the Bible of people who were greatly blessed in life but were not satisfied with what they had. Instead of being thankful for the gifts God had given them, they allowed their desires to grow until those desires brought suffering to others and destruction into their own lives.

Consider King Ahab, who lived in a royal palace surrounded by wealth, power, and luxury. Yet he wanted a vineyard belonging to Naboth, a man who refused to sell the land because it was his family's inheritance. Ahab became so upset that he went home, lay on his bed, and refused to eat. His wife Jezebel then arranged for false accusations to be brought against Naboth, resulting in his death by stoning. Afterward, Ahab went to take possession of the vineyard. What a terrible price was paid because a powerful king could not accept being told no.

We also see this in the life of King David, a man described as being after God's own heart. David had been blessed with a kingdom, wealth, and the favor of God, yet he saw Bathsheba bathing and desired her for himself. When she became pregnant, David attempted to conceal what had happened. Eventually, he arranged for her husband, Uriah, to be placed in the fiercest part of the battle, where he was killed. David's actions brought suffering and consequences that reached far beyond himself.

Then there was King Solomon, blessed by God with extraordinary wisdom, wealth, and splendor. Yet Solomon accumulated hundreds of wives and concubines, along with great wealth, horses, and other possessions. His many foreign wives eventually turned his heart toward other gods. Even the wisest of men was not immune to the dangers of allowing worldly desires to draw him away from God.

These examples remind us that wealth, power, and position cannot guarantee contentment. When we continually desire more, we may lose sight of the blessings already before us.

Now we return to Haman, a man who seemed to have everything he could desire. He possessed great wealth, had many sons, and had been promoted above the other officials of the Persian Empire. He enjoyed the confidence of King Ahasuerus and had recently been invited to a private banquet with the king and Queen Esther.

Haman was so pleased with his position that he gathered his friends and his wife, Zeresh, to tell them about his accomplishments. He spoke of his wealth, his family, and the honors he had received from the king. Then he added another reason for his happiness: Queen Esther had invited him to a second banquet with the royal couple.

Yet with all these blessings and honors, Haman was still not satisfied.

There was one man sitting at the King's Gate who refused to bow before him. Mordecai would not rise or tremble in fear when Haman passed, and this was more than Haman's pride could tolerate. Despite all his wealth and authority, he allowed the actions of one man to overshadow everything else in his life.

Haman wanted Mordecai gone, and his wife Zeresh and his friends offered a terrible solution. They suggested constructing a gallows seventy-five feet tall and asking the king the following morning to have Mordecai executed upon it. Afterward, Haman could attend Esther's banquet and enjoy himself without having to think about the man who refused to honor him.

The suggestion pleased Haman, and he ordered the gallows constructed.

How far pride and resentment can carry a person! Haman was no longer satisfied with having authority over others. He wanted to destroy someone simply because that person refused to give him the honor he believed he deserved.

Jesus teaches us a very different way to live. In Matthew 20:25–28, He reminds His disciples that rulers of the nations exercise authority over others, but it should not be that way among His followers. Whoever wants to become great must become a servant, following the example of Jesus, who came not to be served but to serve.

This is the opposite of Haman's attitude. Instead of demanding recognition and honor, we are called to use whatever position, talents, or blessings God has given us to serve others. Whether we are responsible for a family, a workplace, a church, or a community, our position should never become an excuse to elevate ourselves at the expense of another person.

There may be times when someone receives an opportunity we wanted or possesses something we wish we had. We may even experience unfair treatment or disappointment because of another person's actions. But how we respond to those circumstances reveals much about what is happening in our own hearts.

Do we allow jealousy and resentment to take root, or do we turn to God for the strength to remain humble and forgiving?

We should also remember that God sees what happens in this world. King Ahab, David, and Solomon all faced consequences for their actions, although those consequences were not always immediate or the same. God does not overlook wrongdoing simply because someone holds a position of authority or has received His blessings.

Yet we must also remember that we are all sinners in need of God's forgiveness. David himself came to acknowledge his sin and seek God's mercy. Through Jesus Christ, we have the opportunity to repent, receive forgiveness, and begin walking in a different direction.

One of the best ways to guard against pride and jealousy is to develop a thankful heart. Sometimes we need to sit quietly, look around us, and consider the blessings God has already placed in our lives. They may not include great wealth, an important title, or the recognition of others, but they are blessings nonetheless.

When our personal wants begin to crowd out our peace, gratitude can help us remember what truly matters.

Haman had nearly everything the world could offer, yet he could not find contentment because one man refused to bow before him. As Christians, we have something far greater than worldly recognition. We belong to Jesus Christ, and our worth is not measured by our possessions, accomplishments, or position in society.

Let us be thankful for what God has given us and use those blessings to serve Him and others with humble hearts.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Is there someone in your life who does not give you the recognition or respect you believe you deserve? How can you approach that person with humility and treat them with the love Jesus teaches?

2. Have you ever allowed resentment or thoughts of revenge to take hold of your heart? Did those thoughts bring you peace, or did they make the situation more difficult?

3. What blessings has God placed in your life that you may have overlooked while wishing for something more? How can you show gratitude for those blessings today?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for the many blessings You have given us. Help us not to measure our lives by what others have, but to be thankful for what You have provided. Forgive us when pride, jealousy, or resentment enters our hearts. Teach us to be humble servants and to use our gifts to help others rather than build ourselves up. When we have been hurt, help us to forgive and leave judgment in Your hands. Remind us that our true worth is found in belonging to Your Son, Jesus Christ. In His name we pray.

Amen.
