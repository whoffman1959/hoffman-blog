**DAY 63 – MOTHER'S ADVICE**

**Scripture**

**Ruth 3:1-9 CSB**

Ruth's mother-in-law Naomi said to her, "My daughter, shouldn't I find rest for you, so that you will be taken care of? [2] Now isn't Boaz our relative? Haven't you been working with his female servants? This evening he will be winnowing barley on the threshing floor. [3] Wash, put on perfumed oil, and wear your best clothes. Go down to the threshing floor, but don't let the man know you are there until he has finished eating and drinking. [4] When he lies down, notice the place where he's lying, go in and uncover his feet, and lie down. Then he will explain to you what you should do." [5] So Ruth said to her, "I will do everything you say." [6] She went down to the threshing floor and did everything her mother-in-law had charged her to do. [7] After Boaz ate, drank, and was in good spirits, he went to lie down at the end of the pile of barley, and she came secretly, uncovered his feet, and lay down. [8] At midnight, Boaz was startled, turned over, and there lying at his feet was a woman! [9] So he asked, "Who are you?" "I am Ruth, your servant," she replied. **Ezekiel 16:8 CSB**

"'Then I passed by you and saw you, and you were indeed at the age for love. So I spread the edge of my garment over you and covered your nakedness. I pledged myself to you, entered into a covenant with you—this is the declaration of the Lord GOD—and you became mine."

**Innocent Approach**

In ancient Israelite culture, Naomi's instructions to Ruth were deeply rooted in the customs of family redemption, marriage, and the protection of vulnerable widows. Having both suffered the tragic loss of their husbands before returning to Bethlehem, Naomi and Ruth faced a vulnerable future in a world where women depended heavily on male family for survival and security.

Knowing the law and customs of her people, Naomi instructed Ruth to wash, apply perfumed oil, and put on her finest garments. She was to go down to the threshing floor where Boaz would be sleeping after a long day of winnowing barley. Naomi advised Ruth to remain unseen until Boaz finished his meal and rested. Approaching him privately ensured that if he declined the request, neither of them would suffer public embarrassment or misunderstanding.

When Boaz fell asleep at the end of the barley heap, Ruth quietly approached, uncovered his feet, and lay down. The cool night air on his exposed feet would cause him to awaken naturally, creating a quiet, discreet moment for Ruth to speak. Naomi assured Ruth that once Boaz awoke, he would tell her how to proceed.

At midnight, Boaz startled awake, turned, and was surprised to find a woman lying at his feet. He asked, 'Who are you?'

Ruth answered, 'I am Ruth, your servant. Spread the corner of your garment over me, for you are a family redeemer.'

This was the pivotal moment of the narrative. Ruth was asking Boaz to act as their kinsman-redeemer—a close relative who had the legal privilege and moral responsibility to buy back land, preserve the family name, and take a widow under his care and protection through marriage.

When Ruth asked Boaz to 'spread the corner of your garment' over her, she was using a rich, symbolic idiom of the ancient Near East. Spreading a cloak over someone signified taking them into covenant protection and, in this context, served as a formal proposal of marriage. It is much like someone wrapping a heavy coat around a person shivering in the cold—an intimate, tangible pledge of shelter and security. Notably, earlier in the story, Boaz had blessed Ruth by praying that she would find refuge under the wings of the Lord, the God of Israel. Now, Ruth was boldly asking Boaz to be the very instrument of God’s answer to that prayer.

We see a powerful parallel to this symbolic language in Ezekiel chapter 16. There, God describes His covenant relationship with Israel using the exact same imagery—spreading His garment over her as a solemn vow of love, commitment, and spiritual marriage.

As believers, we see in this account a moving picture of our own redemption. We, too, were vulnerable, spiritually destitute, and unable to secure our own future. But in Jesus Christ, we have a true Kinsman-Redeemer who came near to us, took on human flesh, paid the ultimate price for our debt, and brought us into the family of God.

Just as Ruth approached Boaz in humility and faith seeking his protection, we are invited to draw near to God seeking His grace, mercy, and covering. We do not have to navigate the uncertainties or heartbreaks of life alone. Through Jesus Christ, our Redeemer, we are brought under the shelter of His wings, knowing we belong to Him and can rest securely in His unwavering faithfulness.

**Reflection Questions**

1. How does the covenant image of marriage—and Boaz’s protective care for Ruth—help us better understand the depth of Christ's love and commitment to His Church?

2. Ruth humbly sought care and protection from Boaz, and Naomi supported her in faith. In what areas of your life are you currently called to exercise humility by seeking help or accepting God's protection through others?

3. When you reflect on being brought under the 'garment' of Christ's redemption, how does resting in His covenant protection change how you face anxiety or uncertainty today?
