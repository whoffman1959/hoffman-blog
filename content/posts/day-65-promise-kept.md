**DAY 65 – PROMISE KEPT**

**Scripture**

**Ruth 4:1-10 CSB**

Boaz went to the gate of the town and sat down there. Soon the family redeemer Boaz had spoken about came by. Boaz said, "Come over here and sit down." So he went over and sat down. [2] Then Boaz took ten men of the town's elders and said, "Sit here." And they sat down. [3] He said to the redeemer, "Naomi, who has returned from the territory of Moab, is selling the portion of the field that belonged to our brother Elimelech. [4] I thought I should inform you: Buy it back in the presence of those seated here and in the presence of the elders of my people. If you want to redeem it, do it. But if you do not want to redeem it, tell me so that I will know, because there isn't anyone other than you to redeem it, and I am next after you." "I want to redeem it," he answered. [5] Then Boaz said, "On the day you buy the field from Naomi, you will acquire Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of the deceased man, to perpetuate the man's name on his property." [6] The redeemer replied, "I can't redeem it myself, or I will ruin my own inheritance. Take my right of redemption, because I can't redeem it." [7] At an earlier period in Israel, a man removed his sandal and gave it to the other party in order to make any matter legally binding concerning the right of redemption or the exchange of property. This was the method of legally binding a transaction in Israel. [8] So the redeemer removed his sandal and said to Boaz, "Buy back the property yourself." [9] Boaz said to the elders and all the people, "You are witnesses today that I am buying from Naomi everything that belonged to Elimelech, Chilion, and Mahlon. [10] I have also acquired Ruth the Moabitess, Mahlon's widow, as my wife, to perpetuate the deceased man's name on his property, so that his name will not disappear among his relatives or from the gate of his hometown. You are witnesses today."

**Life Secured**

To get a better grasp of today's reflection, let us imagine ourselves there, watching the events unfold.

Boaz went to the gate and took his seat in the shade of the stone arch. The morning sun was already warming the dusty streets. The man he'd spoken of came walking up the road, his sandals scuffing the dry earth. Boaz lifted a hand. "Come over here and sit down." The man came and sat beside him, wiping his brow. Then Boaz called ten elders from where they stood talking in the square. "Sit here," he told them, and they arranged themselves nearby.

He turned to the redeemer. "Naomi is back from Moab. She is selling the field that belonged to Elimelech." He paused, letting the words settle. "Buy it before these men—before the elders. If you want to redeem it, do so. If not, say so plainly. There is no one else before you—and after you, me."

"I will redeem it," the man said.

Then Boaz explained what else was involved. "Understand—when you buy that field from Naomi, you also acquire Ruth the Moabitess, the dead man's widow, to preserve his name and his inheritance."

The redeemer's face tightened. He shook his head. "Then I cannot do it," he said, pulling at the edge of his robe. "It would jeopardize my own inheritance. You take my right—I cannot redeem it."

In ancient Israel, a family redeemer had the responsibility of helping preserve a relative's family property and protecting the interests of those left behind. In Ruth's situation, redemption also involved marriage and the hope of preserving her deceased husband's name and inheritance. So why did the nearer relative—the man Boaz first approached—refuse when he learned that redeeming the property also involved Ruth? Several interpretations have been offered.

Some suggest the man was already married and feared the complications of taking another wife. Scripture does not tell us whether he was married, though it remains a possibility.

Others point to his own words: "I will ruin my own inheritance." Perhaps he feared that the financial obligations of redeeming the land, combined with providing for Ruth and any children born to her, would diminish the inheritance he intended to leave his own family. After all, the purpose of the arrangement was to preserve the name and inheritance of the deceased, not simply to increase the redeemer's possessions.

Another possibility involves Ruth's Moabite heritage. Relations between Israel and Moab had often been troubled, and the Law contained restrictions concerning Moabites entering the assembly of the Lord. Perhaps the man was reluctant to bring a Moabite woman into his household. Yet Scripture does not tell us that this was his concern.

Whatever his reason, the man decided that the cost was too great. In stepping aside, he yielded the right of redemption to Boaz, who was willing to accept the responsibility.

As custom required, the man removed his sandal and declared to Boaz, "Buy back the property yourself." The elders and those gathered at the gate stood as witnesses. Boaz publicly declared that he was acquiring the property belonging to Elimelech, Chilion, and Mahlon. He would also take Ruth, Mahlon's widow, as his wife, preserving her deceased husband's name through the inheritance he had left behind.

What began as an uncertain future for two widows, Naomi and Ruth, was now moving toward security and the restoration of their family.

Throughout Scripture, we see God's concern for widows, orphans, foreigners, and those who cannot easily provide for themselves. He gave Israel laws intended to protect the vulnerable and prevent families from losing everything when hardship came. Boaz demonstrated the spirit of those laws through his willingness to act, even when another relative declined.

That same responsibility to care for others remains with us today. We may not live under Israel's ancient laws of family redemption, but the call to show mercy, generosity, and compassion has not changed. Boaz's actions remind us that love is more than kind words or good intentions. Sometimes it means accepting a responsibility, making a sacrifice, or helping someone whose circumstances have left them vulnerable.

We may never be called to redeem a family's inheritance, but we can offer a helping hand, a meal, encouragement, or simply our presence to someone who feels forgotten. When we do these things out of love, we reflect the compassion of Jesus Christ, who taught us to care for one another.

God has shown us great mercy through His Son. May we be willing to extend that same mercy to others.

**Reflection Questions**

1. How might we support those in need today, including widows and orphans who are struggling?

2. Why do you think the family member with the first right of redemption decided not to act as Ruth and Naomi's family redeemer?

3. Do I have a family member or someone else in my life who is in need today? How might I offer them help?
