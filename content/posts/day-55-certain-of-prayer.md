**DAY 55 – CERTAIN OF PRAYER**

**Scripture**

**1 John 5:14-17 CSB**

This is the confidence we have before him: If we ask anything according to his will, he hears us. [15] And if we know that he hears whatever we ask, we know that we have what we have asked of him. [16] If anyone sees a fellow believer committing a sin that doesn't lead to death, he should ask, and God will give life to him-to those who commit sin that doesn't lead to death. There is sin that leads to death. I am not saying he should pray about that. [17] All unrighteousness is sin, and there is sin that doesn't lead to death.

**Psalm 91:14-16 CSB**

Because he has his heart set on me, I will deliver him; I will protect him because he knows my name. [15] When he calls out to me, I will answer him; I will be with him in trouble. I will rescue him and give him honor. [16] I will satisfy him with a long life and show him my salvation.

**Kept by God**

No one wants me to be king over their life. There is a joke:

Knock, knock.

Who's there?

Control Freak! Now you say, “Control Freak who?”

It is in the nature of some of us to want to control everything, becoming little gods over our world and the people around us. We begin to think that we have enough wisdom, power, and understanding to direct the lives of others. Our thinking becomes, Everything would be fine if they would just do what I want! We make ourselves the center of everyone else's universe. And what happens? People often rebel. They do not want us to be king over their lives or to surrender control to us. Chaos often follows.

We probably all know people like this, and sometimes we are those people. What is the solution? We begin by realizing that we are not in control. I am not God, and neither are you. There is one God, our Father in Heaven. Instead of trying to build our own little kingdoms, we can surrender the control we think we have to God's sovereign care.

One of the greatest ways we can do this is through prayer. When we enter our quiet sanctuary, we can talk to God. Jesus has opened the way for us to come before the Father. We can glorify God in prayer, pray for others, bring our own needs before Him, and pray unselfishly for the needs of the world. We know that God hears us because He has told us that He does.

But prayer is not another way for us to remain in control. John gives us an important condition: “If we ask anything according to his will, he hears us.” Prayer is not telling God what He must do and then expecting Him to follow our directions. We bring our desires, needs, fears, and concerns before Him while trusting that His wisdom is greater than ours. We can ask with confidence while still surrendering the answer to His will.

John then tells us to pray for a fellow believer whom we see falling into sin. Instead of immediately condemning that person or trying to force them to change, we can bring them before God in prayer. How many parents have continually prayed for a wayward child enslaved by the things of this world? How many people have prayed for a friend or family member who has wandered away? Jesus even tells us to pray for our enemies. We can pray that people will turn to God, come to know the truth, and find life in Jesus Christ.

John also speaks about “sin that leads to death.” This is a difficult passage, and Christians have understood these words in different ways. Some have understood John to be speaking about a settled rejection of Christ and the truth about Him, while others have understood the passage differently. John does not explain everything we might like to know about this statement. What he does make clear is that all unrighteousness is sin and that believers should take the sins of their brothers and sisters seriously enough to pray for them.

This brings us back to our desire for control. There are people we desperately want to change, situations we desperately want to fix, and outcomes we desperately want to control. Prayer allows us to place those people and circumstances into the hands of the One who actually has the wisdom and power that we do not possess. Instead of trying to become lord over another person's life, we can pray for them, love them, serve them, and trust God with the outcome.

Psalm 91 reminds us why we can have this confidence. God does not promise that His people will never experience trouble. He says, “I will be with him in trouble.” We are kept by God even when circumstances do not turn out the way we hoped. He hears us, remains with us, and knows what we need far better than we do.
Chaos comes when we try to establish our own little kingdoms, but we already have a King. He is a King who loves us, cares for us, hears our prayers, and remains with us in trouble. What an opportunity and responsibility we have in prayer. We are speaking to the Creator of the universe, yet we can come before Him as our Father. May we learn to release our imaginary control, enlarge our prayer lives, and trust more completely in His will.

**Reflection Questions**

1. How can I enlarge my prayer life? Is there anything about the way I pray now that needs to change?

2. Do I pray for my family, my enemies, and others in the world, asking that they might come to know Jesus and receive the life that is found in Him?

3. Am I trying to be lord over the lives of others, or am I praying for them, serving them, and trusting them to God's care?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

What a special privilege we have to be able to talk to You, the Creator of all things, the One in whom we have our existence and who loves and cares for us. You hear our prayers and remain with us in times of trouble. Give us this day our daily bread, and help those in our lives and throughout the world to come to know You. Help us release our imaginary control over others into Your hands. Teach us to pray according to Your will and to trust You with the answer. Guide us each day to do Your will, to pray for others, and to serve rather than control them. Keep us in Your care and help us remember that You alone are King. In Jesus’ name we pray.

Amen.
