**DAY 36 – DAY OF THE LORD**

**Scripture**

**2 Peter 3:1-9 CSB**

Dear friends, this is now the second letter I have written to you; in both letters, I want to stir up your sincere understanding by way of reminder, [2] so that you recall the words previously spoken by the holy prophets and the command of our Lord and Savior given through your apostles. [3] Above all, be aware of this: Scoffers will come in the last days scoffing and following their own evil desires, [4] saying, "Where is his 'coming' that he promised? Ever since our ancestors fell asleep, all things continue as they have been since the beginning of creation." [5] They deliberately overlook this: By the word of God the heavens came into being long ago and the earth was brought about from water and through water. [6] Through these the world of that time perished when it was flooded. [7] By the same word, the present heavens and earth are stored up for fire, being kept for the day of judgment and destruction of the ungodly. [8] Dear friends, don't overlook this one fact: With the Lord one day is like a thousand years, and a thousand years like one day. [9] The Lord does not delay his promise, as some understand delay, but is patient with you, not wanting any to perish but all to come to repentance.

**Seeking to Save**

Peter now builds on his warning about false teachers from chapter 2 and turns his attention to scoffers who will come in the last days. Just as with identifying false teachers, Peter encourages his readers to look back at what God has already revealed through His Word.

Peter reminds the believers to remember the words spoken by the holy prophets of old and the command of our Lord and Savior given through the apostles. They could look back through Scripture and see how the words and promises of God had come true. Now Peter tells them to look ahead as well. Scoffers would come, questioning the promised return of Christ. The words given through the prophets and apostles were there to strengthen and warn the believers of Peter's day, and they remain for us today. The scoffers will ask, “Where is His coming that He promised?” Their argument will be that everything has continued just as it always had.

But Peter reminds everyone that this simply is not true. God has intervened in human history before. By His Word the heavens came into being, and by His judgment the ancient world perished in the Flood. Scripture does not tell us everything that happened while Noah built the ark, but we can imagine what such a scene might have been like.

Imagine the scene.

Noah and his sons are building the ark under a dry, unyielding sky—no hint of rain, no sign of flood, only the endless stretch of blue above. The scaffolding groans under their weight as they climb the ramps, hauling planks of gopher wood sealed with pitch. Their wives move steadily below, passing up tools and provisions, faces sheened with sweat and resolve. Bystanders gather at a distance, pointing and murmuring. “What are you doing?” one finally calls out.

Noah pauses, hammer in hand, and warns them of the judgment to come. They laugh and mock what they do not believe. Still, Noah and his family continue the work God has commanded them to do.

Then the day comes. The fountains of the great deep burst open, and the floodgates of the sky open. What had seemed impossible becomes terrifyingly real. The waters rise, but the ark holds firm—a refuge filled with the creatures God had brought to Noah and the family that had trusted and obeyed Him.

Scripture does not give us all the details of what happened while Noah built the ark, but the Flood and God's judgment were real. Peter points his readers back to this event for a reason.

As with the days of Noah, there will be scoffers. Peter warned the Christians of his day, and his warning still speaks to us. People may scoff at the Son of God, His promised return, and the salvation offered through Him, but their unbelief does not change the promises of God. Such scoffing should not surprise us; Peter warned that it would characterize the last days.

Many point to the centuries that have passed since the birth, death, and resurrection of Jesus and say that we do not have to worry about His return during our lifetime. But there is another truth we should remember: whether Christ returns during our earthly lifetime or not, our own lives are short. Each of us will eventually stand before God. Those who continually reject God's offer of eternal life through His Son have refused the refuge He has provided.

The scoffers Peter describes follow their own desires while rejecting the knowledge of God. Instead of bowing before the Creator of the universe who loves us, people can choose other gods to follow, with one of the greatest being the god of self.

What the scoffers fail to understand is the patience of God. Christians have proclaimed the return of Christ for nearly two thousand years, and some use that passage of time as a reason for unbelief. Peter tells us that what appears to us as delay is not delay with God. With the Lord, one day is like a thousand years, and a thousand years like one day. God does not measure time as we do, nor has He forgotten His promise.

The Lord is patient. He does not want people to perish but to come to repentance. The passing of time is therefore not evidence that God's promise has failed. It is another opportunity for people to turn to Him.

Peter also reminds us that the present heavens and earth are being kept for a coming day of judgment. Just as God's judgment in the days of Noah was real, the coming Day of the Lord will also be real. Yet for those who belong to Christ, our hope is not destruction. Our hope is in the Lord who saves us and in His promise of what is still to come.

God's patience should therefore stir us not to fear but to faithfulness. While there is still time, we should live for Christ and tell others about the salvation found in Him.

**Reflection Questions**

1. As I look at the world around me, do I encounter people who scoff at the Lord or His promised return? Am I willing to speak the truth about Christ even when others may ridicule my faith?

2. Where am I in my Christian walk? Am I living with the expectation that Christ will return, or have I become so focused on the desires of this world that I rarely think about eternity?

3. How does knowing that the Lord does not measure time as I do change the way I think about His promised return? Do I see His patience as an opportunity for more people to come to repentance?

**Prayer**

Open our eyes and hearts to Your will. Help us stand strong when the world ridicules us for our faith in You. When everything around us seems peaceful and secure, remind us that Your promises are true. Keep us safely in Your care, and give us the words to tell others that there is forgiveness, eternal life, and salvation in Your Son Jesus. We know that You are patient and do not desire that people perish but that they come to repentance. Help us faithfully tell others about Jesus and live our lives on this earth for You while we wait for His return. Keep our eyes fixed on Christ and our hearts grounded in Your Word.
