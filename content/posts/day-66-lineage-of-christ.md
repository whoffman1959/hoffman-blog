**DAY 66 – LINEAGE OF CHRIST**

**Scripture**

**Ruth 4:11-22 CSB**

All the people who were at the city gate, including the elders, said, "We are witnesses. May the LORD make the woman who is entering your house like Rachel and Leah, who together built the house of Israel. May you be powerful in Ephrathah and your name well known in Bethlehem. [12] May your house become like the house of Perez, the son Tamar bore to Judah, because of the offspring the LORD will give you by this young woman." [13] Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife. He slept with her, and the LORD granted conception to her, and she gave birth to a son. [14] The women said to Naomi, "Blessed be the LORD, who has not left you without a family redeemer today. May his name become well known in Israel. [15] He will renew your life and sustain you in your old age. Indeed, your daughter-in-law, who loves you and is better to you than seven sons, has given birth to him." [16] Naomi took the child, placed him on her lap, and became a mother to him. [17] The neighbor women said, "A son has been born to Naomi," and they named him Obed. He was the father of Jesse, the father of David. [18] Now these are the family records of Perez: Perez fathered Hezron, [19] Hezron fathered Ram, Ram fathered Amminadab, [20] Amminadab fathered Nahshon, Nahshon fathered Salmon, [21] Salmon fathered Boaz, Boaz fathered Obed, [22] Obed fathered Jesse, and Jesse fathered David.

**We are Witnesses**

Genesis 38 tells us the story of Tamar, who was given in marriage to Judah's firstborn son, Er. After Er died, Judah instructed his second son, Onan, to fulfill his family responsibility and provide an heir for his deceased brother. But Onan failed to honor that responsibility, and he too died.

Judah then promised Tamar that his youngest son, Shelah, would become her husband when he was old enough. However, Judah failed to keep his promise.
Tamar was left a childless widow, without the security that a husband or son could provide. When she realized Judah had not given her Shelah as promised, she disguised herself as a prostitute and waited along the road. Judah encountered her, not recognizing who she was, and she became pregnant with his child.

Later, when Judah learned Tamar was pregnant, he ordered that she be brought out and burned. But Tamar produced Judah's seal, cord, and staff, which he had left with her, proving that he was the father.

Judah then acknowledged:

"She is more in the right than I, since I did not give her to my son Shelah." — Genesis 38:26, CSB

Tamar gave birth to twins, Perez and Zerah.

Now, many generations later, the elders at the gate of Bethlehem blessed Boaz and Ruth, asking God to make their family like the family of Perez, the son Tamar bore to Judah. Why would they mention Perez?

Both stories involved widows facing uncertain futures and the importance of preserving a family's name and inheritance. In ancient Israel, having children was considered a blessing from God. Sons often carried on the family name, inherited the land, and helped provide for their parents in their old age. For a widow without children, the future could be especially difficult.

But God had not forgotten Ruth or Naomi.

Boaz took Ruth as his wife, and the Lord blessed them with a son. The women of Bethlehem rejoiced with Naomi, praising God for providing someone who would renew her life and sustain her in her old age. They also reminded her that Ruth, the daughter-in-law who loved her, was worth more to her than seven sons.
Imagine Naomi holding her grandson in her arms. She had returned from Moab believing she had lost everything. Her husband and both sons were gone, and she had little hope for the future. Yet here she was, holding a child who would bring joy and comfort to her remaining years.

They named the child Obed. He would become the father of Jesse and the grandfather of King David.

But the story does not end with David.

When we turn to the first chapter of Matthew, we discover that both Tamar and Ruth are named in the genealogy of Jesus Christ. Tamar, whose circumstances involved Judah's failure to keep his promise, and Ruth, a Moabite woman who left her homeland to follow Naomi, both became part of the family line through which our Savior would come.

Neither woman could have known how God would use her life in the generations to follow. Their stories remind us that God works through ordinary people, even when their lives are filled with hardship, uncertainty, and human failure.

Jesus was born into the nation of Israel, fulfilling God's promises to His people. Yet His coming was never intended to benefit Israel alone. Through Him, salvation would be offered to the whole world, both Jew and Gentile.

What began with Naomi returning to Bethlehem in sorrow ended with a family restored, a child born, and a genealogy that would eventually lead to Jesus Christ.

God's plans often extend far beyond what we can see in our own lifetimes. We may never know how our faithfulness, kindness, or the way we raise our families will affect future generations. But we can trust that God sees the whole picture, even when we can see only a small part.

**Reflection Questions**

1. How did God work in the lives of Naomi and Ruth to bring about good? Is there a blessing or need in your own life that you can bring before Him in prayer?

2. How does knowing that Jesus' family line included both Israelites and those from other nations strengthen our understanding that salvation is offered to everyone

3. How important is the institution of marriage to us today, and how does our understanding of marriage compare with that of Boaz and Ruth's time?
