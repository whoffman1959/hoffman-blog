**DAY 58 – A DAUGHTER'S LOVE**

**Scripture**

**Ruth 1:6-18 CSB**

She and her daughters-in-law set out to return from the territory of Moab, because she had heard in Moab that the LORD had paid attention to his people's need by providing them food. [7] She left the place where she had been living, accompanied by her two daughters-in-law, and traveled along the road leading back to the land of Judah. [8] Naomi said to them, "Each of you go back to your mother's home. May the LORD show kindness to you as you have shown to the dead and to me. [9] May the LORD grant each of you rest in the house of a new husband." She kissed them, and they wept loudly. [10] They said to her, "We insist on returning with you to your people." [11] But Naomi replied, "Return home, my daughters. Why do you want to go with me? Am I able to have any more sons who could become your husbands? [12] Return home, my daughters. Go on, for I am too old to have another husband. Even if I thought there was still hope for me to have a husband tonight and to bear sons, [13] would you be willing to wait for them to grow up? Would you restrain yourselves from remarrying? No, my daughters, my life is much too bitter for you to share, because the LORD's hand has turned against me." [14] Again they wept loudly, and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her. [15] Naomi said, "Look, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods. Follow your sister-in-law." [16] But Ruth replied: Don't plead with me to abandon you or to return and not follow you. For wherever you go, I will go, and wherever you live, I will live; your people will be my people, and your God will be my God. [17] Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the LORD punish me, and do so severely, if anything but death separates you and me. [18] When Naomi saw that Ruth was determined to go with her, she stopped talking to her.

**New Beginnings**

The famine in Israel is over. Naomi has heard that the Lord has provided food for His people, so she sets out to return home. Most people who have served in the military know what it is like to dream of home. It is a longing that can set in at night as they continue their duty or lay their head down on whatever they can find to sleep on. Home—for most of us, it brings memories of comfort, familiar places, and seeing those we love again. Naomi has lost almost everything, and now she is going back to the place where she hopes she might find some comfort. She is going home to Bethlehem.

As Christians, we are also on a journey home. As we walk through this world, we will know joy and we will know sorrow. We have an earthly place that we call home and sometimes yearn for, but our heavenly home will be so much better. It is our place of rest. As we get older, we discover that the places we remember from childhood have changed. New stores are built, new people live in familiar houses, and many of our old friends and loved ones are no longer there. This world changes, but we have a lasting home waiting for us with God.

Our reflection today also gives us a glimpse into the heart of Naomi. She has suffered greatly, yet she is concerned about the welfare of her daughters-in-law. She could think only about her own loss and loneliness, but instead she thinks about their future. She wants Ruth and Orpah to return to their mothers' homes, remarry, and find rest. Even in her sorrow, Naomi is concerned about those she loves.

Naomi was obviously loved by her daughters-in-law, which also tells us something about the relationship they shared. When she told them to return to their mothers' homes, they all wept. At first, both women refused to leave her, saying that they would return with her to her people.

Naomi continued to urge them to go. She believed there was nothing more she could give these young women. She could not provide husbands for them, and she believed her own life had become too bitter for them to share. Naomi even believed that the Lord's hand had turned against her. She could see her losses, but she could not yet see what God was going to do. As we saw yesterday, God had not forgotten Naomi. One of the ways He would care for her was already standing beside her.

Eventually, Orpah accepted Naomi's urging. They wept again, and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law goodbye and returned home. But Ruth clung to Naomi. Then Ruth spoke the words that have been remembered for generations:

“For wherever you go, I will go, and wherever you live, I will live; your people will be my people, and your God will be my God. [17] Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried.”

Ruth's decision was about more than accompanying an older widow on a journey. She was leaving her homeland, her people, and the gods she had grown up around. She was choosing Naomi's people as her people and, most importantly, Naomi's God as her God. Naomi believed Ruth would have a better future if she returned home, but Ruth chose instead to remain faithful to the woman she loved.

They would travel to Bethlehem together. Ruth and Naomi remind us that family is about more than bloodlines. There are people whom God places in our lives who love us, remain beside us in difficult times, and become as close as family. We can find this among fellow Christians as well. We become brothers and sisters in Christ, part of a family that reaches beyond this life and into the life to come.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Is there a Naomi or a Ruth in my life—someone whose welfare I am willing to place above my own?

2. What characteristics do I see in Naomi, Ruth, and Orpah in our reflection today? Do I show those same qualities toward the people God has placed in my life?

3. Are my eyes focused only on my earthly home, or am I also looking toward my heavenly home?

**Prayer**

May we show that same love and concern for others. Protect those we have grown up with and those we cherish from our past. Build around us another family among the fellow believers You have placed in our lives. Give us a glimpse of the blessings we will have in Heaven, our true home. Guard us as we walk through this life, and keep the trials we face from making us bitter. Help us instead to continue walking in love and remaining faithful to those You have placed beside us.
