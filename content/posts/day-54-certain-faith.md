**DAY 54 – CERTAIN FAITH**

**Scripture**

**1 John 5:5-13 CSB**

Who is the one who conquers the world but the one who believes that Jesus is the Son of God? [6] Jesus Christ-he is the one who came by water and blood, not by water only, but by water and by blood. And the Spirit is the one who testifies, because the Spirit is the truth. [7] For there are three that testify: [8] the Spirit, the water, and the blood-and these three are in agreement. [9] If we accept human testimony, God's testimony is greater, because it is God's testimony that he has given about his Son. [10] The one who believes in the Son of God has this testimony within himself. The one who does not believe God has made him a liar, because he has not believed in the testimony God has given about his Son. [11] And this is the testimony: God has given us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. [12] The one who has the Son has life. The one who does not have the Son of God does not have life. [13] I have written these things to you who believe in the name of the Son of God so that you may know that you have eternal life.

**Ephesians 1:13 CSB**

In him you also were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and when you believed.”

**Conquerors**

As John comes to the end of this letter, we see one of his main purposes clearly. He wants these believers to remain in the truth they received about Jesus Christ and to have assurance of their faith. They had not believed in vain or placed their faith in the wrong Savior. John tells them, “I have written these things to you who believe in the name of the Son of God so that you may know that you have eternal life.”

It is terrible when we have come to believe and received every spiritual blessing in Christ, only to have someone tell us, “You are almost there, but there is something more you need to do.” We have been chosen in Christ to have fellowship with God and adopted as His children. We have forgiveness when we fall short of the will of God because of the riches of His grace. Then someone comes along and says that we must also adhere to this or that law, perform some additional work, or possess some special spiritual knowledge. But salvation is not something we earn through our works or through some secret knowledge. Salvation is the work of God, freely given to us through His Son.

John is reminding these believers to remain in the truth they received from the beginning and not be led away by teachers bringing false beliefs about Jesus. Their faith was not lacking something that these teachers could provide. Their life was in the Son of God.

Then John uses words that Christians have discussed for centuries: “Jesus Christ—he is the one who came by water and blood, not by water only, but by water and by blood.”

Some believe that the water and blood refer to the water and blood that flowed from our Savior's side when Jesus was on the cross. Others understand the water as Jesus' baptism in the Jordan and the blood as His death on the cross. Christians have understood these words in different ways over the centuries, but John's central message is clear: the testimony points us to Jesus Christ, the Son of God, in whom we have eternal life.

If we understand the water as the baptism of Jesus and the blood as His death on the cross, we can see how these witnesses point to the same truth about Christ. At the Jordan, where Jesus was baptized and began His public ministry, John the Baptist was there as a witness. John had come in the spirit and power of Elijah, preparing the way for the Lord as the prophets had foretold. There the Father Himself testified about Jesus, declaring, “This is my beloved Son.” The Holy Spirit had already spoken through the Scriptures, prophesying about Jesus and the things He would do.

The testimony continued throughout Jesus' ministry. The works and miracles He performed testified to who He was as He ministered in the power of the Spirit. Then came the blood of the cross, where Jesus willingly gave Himself for our sins. The sky grew dark, the curtain of the sanctuary was torn in two, and even the Roman centurion who witnessed His death declared that He truly was God's Son. That testimony continues. The same Spirit is given to those who believe, sealing us as God's own. From the water of His baptism to the blood of the cross, the testimony points again and again to the same truth: Jesus Christ is the Son of God.

John then brings us to the truth that gives believers assurance: God has given us eternal life, and this life is in His Son. The one who has the Son has life. We do not need another Savior, some secret knowledge, or another work that will finally make Christ's sacrifice sufficient. Our confidence rests in Jesus Christ, the Son of God.

This is the faith that conquers the world. Our assurance does not rest in how much we have accomplished for God, but in the grace that God has given us in His Son. We believe in Jesus Christ, and God has given us eternal life in Him. John wants believers not merely to hope that this is true, but to know that they have eternal life.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Am I assured of my salvation? Do I believe there is something more I need to do to be saved beyond what Jesus has already done for me on the cross?

2. Am I trying to work my way to Heaven, or am I resting in the promise of God that Jesus paid it all?

3. How does the Holy Spirit testify about Jesus in my life and give me assurance that I belong to Him?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for sending Your Son, who truly became one of us, to suffer and be tempted, yet without sin. Thank You, Jesus, for willingly giving Yourself on the cross for our sins. Thank You for giving Yourself so that Your shed blood covers the blemish of sin in our lives and we are complete in You. Help us always to rest in the assurance of our salvation and in the true faith revealed by You. Give us a firm understanding of Your truth so that we can turn away from false teaching. Keep us in Your way and firmly grounded in the salvation that comes only through You. In Your name we pray.

Amen.
