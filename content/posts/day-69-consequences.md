**DAY 68 – CONSEQUENCES**

**Scripture**

**Esther 1:16-22 CSB**

Memucan said in the presence of the king and his officials, "Queen Vashti has wronged not only the king, but all the officials and the peoples who are in every one of King Ahasuerus's provinces. [17] For the queen's action will become public knowledge to all the women and cause them to despise their husbands and say, 'King Ahasuerus ordered Queen Vashti brought before him, but she did not come.' [18] Before this day is over, the noble women of Persia and Media who hear about the queen's act will say the same thing to all the king's officials, resulting in more contempt and fury. [19] "If it meets the king's approval, he should personally issue a royal decree. Let it be recorded in the laws of the Persians and the Medes, so that it cannot be revoked: Vashti is not to enter King Ahasuerus's presence, and her royal position is to be given to another woman who is more worthy than she. [20] The decree the king issues will be heard throughout his vast kingdom, so all women will honor their husbands, from the greatest to the least." [21] The king and his counselors approved the proposal, and he followed Memucan's advice. [22] He sent letters to all the royal provinces, to each province in its own script and to each ethnic group in its own language, that every man should be master of his own house and speak in the language of his own people.

**Matthew 5:21-22 CSB**

"You have heard that it was said to our ancestors, Do not murder, and whoever murders will be subject to judgment. [22] But I tell you, everyone who is angry with his brother or sister will be subject to judgment. Whoever insults his brother or sister, will be subject to the court. Whoever says, 'You fool!' will be subject to hellfire.

**Actions and Change**

As with any royal couple, King Ahasuerus and Queen Vashti were a focus of attention throughout the kingdom. By the king's words, a person's life could end, or great benefits could be given. Their actions also set an example for others to follow.

The men of the royal court suddenly found themselves concerned about losing their authority. Queen Vashti's refusal represented a possible change from what was expected in their society. The king's officials feared that her actions would be repeated by other women throughout the empire. They believed that wives in every province of Persia and Media might begin refusing their husbands' demands and treating them with contempt.

I remember growing up in a small coal-mining community. During the Easter and Christmas holidays, the women would prepare the meals while the men sat together and talked. It was a different time, with expectations about the roles of husbands and wives that were often taken for granted.

I am not ashamed to say that I loved to cook then, and I still love to cook today. Now, one of the blessings of the holidays is spending time in the kitchen with my wife and daughters, preparing meals and enjoying one another's company.

Marriage is a blessing from God, and our spouses and families are precious gifts. Yet throughout history, people have held different expectations about authority and responsibilities within marriage. In the Persian court, we see how deeply concerned the king's advisors were about maintaining the established order.

Memucan, one of the king's trusted advisors, proposed that Queen Vashti should never again be permitted to enter the king's presence. Her royal position would be taken from her and given to another woman. The consequences of her refusal would become known throughout the kingdom.

The king accepted this advice and issued a decree to every province, written in the languages of the people. What began as a disagreement between a husband and wife had now become a matter of law throughout a vast empire.

One moment, one decision had consequences that reached far beyond the royal palace.

It was as if a stone had been thrown into the middle of a still pond. As the stone entered the water, rings began spreading outward, one after another, until the ripples eventually reached the shore. What began at a single point affected the entire surface of the pond.

Queen Vashti was not put to death, but she lost her position as queen and was forbidden from entering the king's presence. Scripture does not tell us what became of her afterward. What we do know is that the decision changed the course of her life and helped set in motion events that would affect the entire kingdom.

Our decisions and actions have consequences. Sometimes those consequences reach people we never intended to hurt. Words spoken in anger, decisions made in haste, and the desire to defend our pride can leave wounds that remain long after the moment has passed.

Yet this is where we also see the love of God. He sent His Son to die for our sins, including those actions and decisions that have caused pain and suffering. Through Christ, we can receive forgiveness and learn to extend that same forgiveness to others.

In Matthew chapter 5, Jesus teaches us how we should respond when anger begins to take hold of our hearts. He reminds His listeners of the commandment given to the Jewish people that they must not murder. This is a good law, one that is also reflected in the laws of nations today.

But Jesus goes beyond the outward action and addresses what is happening within the heart. Anger, hatred, and contempt can begin long before a person commits an act of violence. Even when our actions appear acceptable to others, God knows the thoughts and attitudes we carry within us.

Jesus warns that those who harbor sinful anger against a brother or sister are accountable before God. He speaks of insulting another person and uses the word Raca, an expression of contempt that treats someone as worthless or empty-headed. Such words reveal an attitude of disrespect toward another person.

He also warns against calling someone a fool in a spirit of contempt. This is more than insulting another person's intelligence or disagreeing with their decisions. It is a judgment of their moral worth, an expression of anger that can become so deeply rooted in the heart that we begin wishing they were dead or under the judgment of God. In this sense, hatred becomes a form of murder within the heart, even when no physical act has taken place.

We may never raise a hand against someone, yet our thoughts and words can still cause great harm. When we allow anger to grow into hatred, we begin to lose sight of the fact that the person who hurt us is also someone created by God.

The only way to be free from the bondage of resentment is to forgive from the heart and place judgment into God's hands. This does not mean pretending that we have not been hurt or that the actions of others have no consequences. It means refusing to allow the wrongs committed against us to determine how we live and treat others.

When someone hurts us, the damage does not always end with the original offense. Like the stone thrown into the pond, the ripple effect can continue through our own reactions. Anger can lead to harsh words, broken relationships, and decisions that bring even more pain.

But we have a choice in how we respond. We can allow those ripples to spread, or we can seek God's help to respond with wisdom, love, and forgiveness.

When we call Jesus our Lord and Savior, these are not just words. He calls us to live according to His teachings, even when doing so is difficult. Forgiveness must come from the heart, not merely from the words we speak.

There may be times when we must separate ourselves from someone who continues to hurt us or those we love. We may need to establish boundaries to protect ourselves and others. Yet even when separation is necessary, we can seek to maintain a forgiving heart, leaving judgment and vengeance in God's hands.

This was not the course chosen by King Ahasuerus and his trusted advisors. Rather than seeking reconciliation, they responded to Queen Vashti's refusal with a decree that affected people throughout the empire.

Their decision would have consequences far beyond what they could have imagined. Yet even through human pride and weakness, God was working to prepare the way for Esther, who would eventually play a part in protecting His people in a foreign land.

We cannot always control the actions of others, but we can seek God's guidance in how we respond. Our words and decisions may create ripples that reach farther than we realize. May those ripples reflect the love and forgiveness of Christ rather than the anger and pride of this world.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Is there someone in your life whom you need to forgive from the heart? What might be keeping you from extending that forgiveness?

2. Have you ever been so angry with someone that you withdrew from them or allowed resentment to control your thoughts? What were the consequences?

3. Are there things you need to change in your relationships to show others that you genuinely care about them?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

We come before You in the name of Your Son, Jesus Christ. Thank You for being a God who is raising up a people who demonstrate love and forgiveness from the heart. Forgive us for the times when anger has taken hold of us and we have responded in ways that do not bring honor to You, even when those reactions have remained hidden in our hearts. Protect us from those who would cause us harm, and give us the strength and wisdom to know how to respond with love. Help us forgive those who have wronged us while recognizing when we must protect ourselves and others from further harm. Guard our hearts against resentment and keep our lips from sinful words. May our actions reflect the forgiveness and mercy that You have shown us through Your Son.

Amen.
