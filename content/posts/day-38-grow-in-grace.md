**DAY 38 – GROW IN GRACE**

**Scripture**

**2 Peter 3:14-18 CSB**

Therefore, dear friends, while you wait for these things, make every effort to be found without spot or blemish in his sight, at peace. [15] Also, regard the patience of our Lord as salvation, just as our dear brother Paul has written to you according to the wisdom given to him. [16] He speaks about these things in all his letters. There are some things hard to understand in them. The untaught and unstable will twist them to their own destruction, as they also do with the rest of the Scriptures. [17] Therefore, dear friends, since you know this in advance, be on your guard, so that you are not led away by the error of lawless people and fall from your own stable position. [18] But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. To him be the glory both now and to the day of eternity.

**1 John 2:3-4 CSB**

This is how we know that we know him: if we keep his commands. [4] The one who says, "I have come to know him," and yet doesn't keep his commands, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.

**Standing Firm**

When we see the word therefore, we need to look at what has been written before. Peter has just finished describing the new heavens and the new earth where righteousness dwells. We are called by grace and have the promise of a place in God's eternal Kingdom. This should produce in us a yearning for wholesome, holy living. A desire to follow after the things of God and to live holy lives in His sight.

But with this desire, we must always remember that our holiness does not come from our own ability to make ourselves righteous. We are forgiven and made right with God because of Christ, not because of our own efforts. Yet because we belong to Him, we desire to live lives that please Him.

We look at the world—the hatred, betrayals, and selfishness—and in our own judgment wonder why things are not different. Why does God allow these things to continue? But the Lord is patient with us. God does not delight in the death of the wicked but desires that they turn from their ways and live. We see His patience and mercy throughout the Bible.

Today I read about the prodigal son in Luke chapter 15. The younger son chose to leave his father's house, took his inheritance, and went to a distant country. How many of us have done something similar in life? We look at the things of God and determine that we prefer the things of the world. We run after what the world has to offer.

Many of us eventually discover that the world can be cruel and spiritually dark. We hunger for things to be different. Like the prodigal son, we come to our Father in Heaven humbled by the consequences of putting self and the world first in our lives.

And what does the father do in Jesus' parable? While the son is still a long way off, his father sees him and runs to him, bringing him into his embrace. Jesus tells us that there is joy in heaven over one sinner who repents. The one who was lost has been found; the one who was dead is alive again.

God is patient and forgiving toward those who turn to Him. He desires people to come to the knowledge of the truth. God does not delight in the death of the wicked. He calls people to repentance and salvation.

Peter then turns to the writings of the Apostle Paul and says that Paul also writes about these things. Peter acknowledges that some things in Paul's letters are hard to understand. There are great truths in the Bible that people study for years. It is easy to read a verse without looking at the whole counsel of Scripture or taking the time to understand the historical setting in which the biblical writers, guided by the Holy Spirit, wrote.

A seasoned Bible reader who has studied the Word for years can still stand amazed as truths become clearer from passages read many times before. Some things in the Bible are difficult to understand, but as we continue studying Scripture and honestly seek the Lord, the Holy Spirit helps us grow in our understanding of His truth.

There are foundational truths that Peter and Paul both proclaim: the new birth of the believer; the deity of Christ and His sinless life; His suffering and death on the cross for our sins; His burial and resurrection; the promise of His return; and eternal life for those who belong to Him.

Other truths are harder for our finite minds to fully grasp. How can Jesus be truly God and truly man? How can God be one God yet Father, Son, and Holy Spirit? There are things about our infinite God that our finite minds cannot completely comprehend. We know what God has revealed to us in His Word, while trusting Him with what we cannot fully understand.

Peter's concern, however, is not simply that some things are difficult. His concern is what people do with them. The untaught and unstable twist difficult passages of Scripture to fit their own understanding, sometimes to their own destruction. In doing so, they can introduce false teaching and lead others away from the truth.

There is something else remarkable in Peter's words when he speaks of Paul's writings and then says that people twist them “as they also do with the rest of the Scriptures.” Peter places Paul's writings alongside the Scriptures. Paul's words were not merely his personal opinions; they are part of the inspired teaching God was giving His church.

We as believers must always understand that not everything we hear from someone who claims to know the Bible or the way of salvation is true. There are false teachings and cults in the world that can lead people away from Christ. We must test what is taught against what the Bible teaches.

It can be easy to be swept away by error and lose our spiritual balance if we neglect prayer and the Word of God or isolate ourselves from other believers. The darkness of this world can quietly spread its lies into our hearts when we separate ourselves from God's Word and try to walk through this world alone.

Peter tells us to be on guard so that we are not led away by the errors of lawless people and fall from our stable position.

The Apostle John gives us another test when someone claims to know Christ:

The one who says, "I have come to know him," and yet doesn't keep his commands, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.

Jesus warns us in Matthew chapter 7 about false prophets. They come in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves. Jesus tells us, “You'll recognize them by their fruit.” We should therefore examine not only what teachers say but also what they do and the lives of those who follow them. Does their teaching lead people toward Christ, holiness, love, and obedience to God, or does it produce greed, sexual immorality, pride, and rebellion against God's commands? Over time, the fruit of what is being taught will begin to show.

But Peter does not end his letter by telling Christians merely to watch for error. He tells us what to do instead:

“But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.”

This is our defense and our calling. We continue growing in our knowledge of Christ, grounded in His Word and dependent upon His grace. We do not grow beyond our need for grace; we grow more deeply in it. And while we wait for the day when we will be with the Lord in eternity, we give Him the glory both now and forever.

**Reflection Questions**

1.	How does the Bible teach me to recognize a false prophet or teacher who might try to lead me away from the truth about Jesus?

2.	Do I spend enough time in the Bible throughout the week? Do I surround myself with other believers so that we can share the Word together and grow in our understanding?

3.	What are some practical ways I can grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ?



**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

We know that You look upon us with mercy and do not desire that people perish but that they come to repentance and the saving knowledge of Your Son Jesus. Help us to follow You and to be a light to those who are seeking truth in this world. Fill us with Your Holy Spirit, and let our actions reflect the faith we profess. Help us not to merely listen to the words of Jesus but apply them to our lives, always remembering that we are saved by grace and not by our works. Protect Your church and Your people from ravenous wolves in sheep's clothing, from those who carry a false message and lead people away from the truth. Help us speak the truth in love and with patience, knowing that You desire people to turn to You and be saved. Keep us grounded in Your Word, growing in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. In Jesus' name we pray.

Amen.
