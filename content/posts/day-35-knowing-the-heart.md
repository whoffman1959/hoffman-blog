**DAY 35 – KNOWING THE HEART**

**Scripture**

**2 Peter 2:4-22 CSB**

For if God didn't spare the angels who sinned but cast them into hell and delivered them in chains of utter darkness to be kept for judgment; [5] and if he didn't spare the ancient world, but protected Noah, a preacher of righteousness, and seven others, when he brought the flood on the world of the ungodly; [6] and if he reduced the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah to ashes and condemned them to extinction, making them an example of what is coming to the ungodly; [7] and if he rescued righteous Lot, distressed by the depraved behavior of the immoral [8] (for as that righteous man lived among them day by day, his righteous soul was tormented by the lawless deeds he saw and heard)- [9] then the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from trials and to keep the unrighteous under punishment for the day of judgment…

(Please read the rest of chapter 2 for this reflection.)

**Moving Forward Not Back**

In 1 Samuel 16:7, the prophet shares an insight about how God views people.

"Do not look at his appearance or his stature because I have rejected him. Humans do not see what the LORD sees, for humans see what is visible, but the LORD sees the heart."

God does not judge by outward appearances—how well we are dressed or how attractive we might be. God looks at the heart. He sees what others cannot see.

I remember when I received money from my mother to go to the local mall by myself, buy clothes, and get a haircut. I was so proud of the way I looked when I went to school after the weekend. I am not sure if my classmates even noticed the effort I had taken to look my best, but I thought my appearance was what made me who I was. Later in life, as my body changed and I slowed down, I learned that appearance is fleeting. What is in the heart is what matters.

There will come a time when we will all stand before God. Those who belong to Christ do not depend upon their own goodness to save them. We hold on to Jesus and His sacrifice for us. Our sins are forgiven because of what Christ has done. Yet Scripture also teaches that believers will give an account of how we have lived and served the Lord.

No one likes to talk about judgment. It is easy to say that God forgives everyone and that it does not matter what anyone does in life, but this is not biblical. Our reading today shows both sides of the truth: God knows how to rescue those who are His, and He also judges wickedness.

Peter reminds us of the flood, when Noah and his family were preserved while judgment came upon an ungodly world. He reminds us of Lot, who was rescued when Sodom and Gomorrah were destroyed. Peter even points to the angels who sinned and were kept for judgment. God does judge sin, and Scripture warns us that it is a terrible thing to fall under His judgment. But through Christ, that does not have to be our end.

There is also something important about the people Peter says God rescued. Noah was called a preacher of righteousness. Lot was distressed by the evil he saw around him. As Christians, our hearts should be turned toward our Savior, Jesus Christ, rather than toward the corruption of the world. The things of this world should grow strangely dim as we follow our Lord and Savior. We may suffer because we refuse to follow the same road others are traveling, but God sees and knows.

As we read the rest of the chapter, it is important to remember that Peter has not changed subjects. He is still talking about the false teachers he introduced at the beginning of the chapter. The examples of the angels who sinned, the ancient world, and Sodom and Gomorrah show that God has judged rebellion before. At the same time, Noah and Lot remind us that the Lord knows how to rescue those who are His.

Peter then returns to describing these false teachers in severe terms. He compares them to irrational animals, calls them springs without water and mists driven by a storm, and says that they promise others freedom while they themselves are slaves of corruption. Their words may sound convincing and their promises attractive, but God sees beyond their outward appearance. He sees the heart behind the teaching.

We have a choice when we hear the truth about Jesus. We can follow the empty promises of this world and those who distort the truth, or we can hold firmly to our faith and continue following Christ. False teaching may be wrapped in attractive words and promises of freedom, but God knows what is behind it.

God knows the heart. We cannot fool the Creator who made us.

**Reflection Questions**

1.	Am I careful about who I allow to influence my understanding of God? Do their words point me toward Christ and the truth of Scripture, or do they offer attractive promises that could lead me away from Him?

2.	Are there things in my life that I would be ashamed for Jesus to see? Do I need to confess them and receive His forgiveness? Have I asked God to loosen the hold that the things of this world may have on my heart?

3.	Am I truly trusting in the Lord and in what Jesus has done for the forgiveness of my sins?


**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for sending Your Son to die for my sins. Keep me from running after the empty promises of this world that lead away from Your truth. Turn my heart toward Your ways, and help me to stand apart from the corruption around me as Noah and Lot did. When trials come because I follow You, remind me that You know how to rescue the godly. Keep me sober-minded today, following Your will rather than the voices that would lead me astray. Fill me with Your Spirit, guard my heart from deception, and keep my eyes fixed on Jesus. In Jesus' name.
