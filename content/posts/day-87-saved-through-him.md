**DAY 87 – SAVED THROUGH HIM**

**Scripture**

**Galatians 3:1-6 CSB**

You foolish Galatians! Who has cast a spell on you, before whose eyes Jesus Christ was publicly portrayed as crucified? [2] I only want to learn this from you: Did you receive the Spirit by the works of the law or by believing what you heard? [3] Are you so foolish? After beginning by the Spirit, are you now finishing by the flesh? [4] Did you experience so much for nothing-if in fact it was for nothing? [5] So then, does God give you the Spirit and work miracles among you by your doing the works of the law? Or is it by believing what you heard- [6] just like Abraham who believed God, and it was credited to him for righteousness?

**Gift of God**

Paul certainly did not mince words when he addressed the Galatians. He called them foolish and asked who had cast a spell on them. These were strong words coming from a man who had spent his life preaching the love and grace of Jesus Christ. But Paul was not questioning their intelligence. He was questioning how they could have so quickly turned away from the truth they had received.

The Galatians had heard the gospel. They knew that Jesus Christ had been crucified for their sins, and they had received the Holy Spirit through faith. Yet now they were being persuaded that following the requirements of the Jewish law was necessary to make them right with God. They were beginning to believe that what Jesus had accomplished on the cross was somehow not enough.

Paul wanted them to remember a simple truth. Religious ordinances, traditions, and institutions cannot save our souls. We cannot earn our way into heaven by being good enough or following a list of rules. Salvation is a gift from God, received through faith in His Son, Jesus Christ.

Think about the person whose life has been swallowed up by sin. Perhaps they have chased after the pleasures of this world until there is little left of the person they once were. Their choices have driven away family and friends, and the things they once believed would bring happiness have become chains holding them captive. They have become slaves to the very things they pursued.

Then something wonderful happens. God calls them out of the darkness, and they respond in faith. The Holy Spirit enters their life, and a new beginning unfolds. The chains that once held them begin to loosen, and the things they once loved no longer hold the same attraction. They begin to see the world through different eyes.

Did this happen because they finally became good enough for God? Did they somehow earn His forgiveness by cleaning up their lives? Of course not! God reached into their darkness and offered them the gift of salvation through His Son. The change in their life was not the price of salvation; it was the work of the Holy Spirit within them.

Yet there is a trap that even believers can fall into. Having received salvation by grace, we sometimes begin to believe that we must maintain our standing with God through our own efforts. We turn our relationship with Jesus into a list of things we must do and things we must avoid, as though our obedience could add something to His sacrifice.

This is the foolishness Paul was confronting. How could we begin our new life through the power of the Holy Spirit and then believe we must finish it through our own strength?

Paul reminds the Galatians of Abraham. Long before the law was given to Moses, Abraham believed God, and his faith was credited to him as righteousness. Abraham was not a perfect man, but he trusted the promises of God. The same God who counted Abraham righteous through faith now offers salvation through faith in Jesus Christ.

God has not changed, and neither has His promise. Jesus died once for our sins, and His sacrifice is sufficient. There is nothing we can add to the cross that would make His sacrifice more complete. We are saved through Him, not through our own accomplishments.

Does this mean we are free to continue living in sin without repentance? Certainly not! Grace is not permission to return to the darkness from which God has called us. A person who truly belongs to Christ should desire to leave behind the things that once separated them from God.

That does not mean we will never stumble. We are still human, and there will be times when we fall short. But the Holy Spirit does not abandon His work in us. He convicts us when we sin, leads us toward repentance, and continues changing our hearts to become more like Jesus.

Our good works do not purchase salvation, nor do they complete what Jesus accomplished on the cross. They are the fruit of a life being transformed by the Spirit of God.

What a wonderful gift we have received! We do not have to spend our lives wondering whether we have done enough to earn God's love. We can place our trust in Jesus Christ, knowing that His sacrifice is sufficient and that the God who began His work in us is faithful to continue it.

We were saved through Him, and it is through Him that we are made new.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Paul asked the Galatians why they were trying to finish through their own efforts what God had begun through the Holy Spirit. Have there been times when you felt you needed to earn God's approval rather than simply trusting in His grace?

2. Salvation is a gift that we cannot earn. How has the Holy Spirit changed your desires, attitudes, or priorities since you first placed your faith in Jesus Christ?

3. There is a difference between obeying God out of love and obeying Him out of fear that we have not done enough. How can we learn to live in obedience to Christ while resting in the assurance of His finished work on the cross?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for the wonderful gift of salvation through Your Son, Jesus Christ. We know that nothing we could ever do would be enough to earn the forgiveness and eternal life You have freely offered us. Forgive us when we begin to rely upon our own efforts rather than trusting in the sacrifice Jesus made on the cross. Help us to remember that our salvation rests in Him alone. Fill our hearts with Your Holy Spirit, convict us when we stumble, and guide us away from the things of this world that would draw us from You. May our obedience come from hearts filled with love and gratitude, not from fear or the desire to earn Your favor. Continue Your work within us, making us more like Your Son each day. In Jesus' precious name we pray.

Amen.
