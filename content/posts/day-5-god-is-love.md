**DAY 5 – GOD IS LOVE**

**Scripture**

**John 3:16–17 (CSB)**

"For God loved the world in this way: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life. [17] For God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but to save the world through him."

**Psalm 86:5 (CSB)**

"For you, Lord, are kind and ready to forgive, abounding in faithful love to all who call on you."

**God Is Faithful in Loving Forgiveness**

God’s love begins long before we take our first breath. From the moment we are formed in the womb, His love surrounds us—not because of our wealth, our achievements, or how often we attend church, but simply because of who He is. His love is steady, unconditional, and constant.

There have been seasons in my life when I felt completely abandoned—left to navigate everything on my own. Friends drifted away, trusted people disappeared when I needed them most, and I found myself sitting alone with resentment growing in my heart. It was a hollow place to be. I replayed the hurts, the betrayals, and the disappointments over and over.

But the turning point came when I finally dared to look inward. What part had I played? Where had I been wrong? What wounds had I caused others? Those questions opened the first small window for forgiveness to break through the haze of bitterness.

And then I looked to Jesus.

He knew abandonment far deeper than I ever will. He was betrayed, mocked, spit upon, and humiliated — not because He had done anything wrong, but because He was taking on the cost of my sins. He endured rejection so I could receive acceptance. He stayed on the cross so I could be forgiven.

When I remembered that, something changed in me. The same grace that forgave my failures gave me the strength to forgive others — and to admit where I had been at fault. That was where healing began.

Even though we were created in love, life often shapes us through pain, disappointment, and mistakes. Those experiences can pull us away from God and push us toward hurting others or guarding ourselves. Yet the Father never closes His heart. His forgiveness remains wide open to anyone who turns back to Him.

Just like the father in the parable of the prodigal son, God runs toward us long before we can explain ourselves. His desire is not to shame us but to restore us.

Through Christ, we see the clearest picture of that love. God’s purpose was never condemnation—it was redemption. Jesus came not to punish but to save, to bridge the distance between our brokenness and the Father’s mercy.

Every time we repent, every time we come home, we are reminded:

God’s love is deeper than our sin.
His forgiveness is stronger than our failures.
And His arms are always open.

**Reflection Questions**

1. In what ways have I experienced God’s love even when I felt undeserving of it?

2. What areas of my life still need the healing power of His forgiveness?

3. How can I extend the same kind of patient, forgiving love to others that God has shown me?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for loving me before I ever knew You. Thank You for sending Your Son—not to condemn, but to save. Forgive me for the times I have wandered from Your truth or doubted Your mercy. Teach me to live each day aware of Your faithful love, and help me to forgive others as freely as You have forgiven me. May my heart reflect Your compassion, and may my life point others to the grace found in Christ.

Amen.
