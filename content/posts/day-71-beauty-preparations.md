**DAY 71 – BEAUTY PREPARATIONS**

**Scripture**

**Esther 2:8-14 CSB**

When the king's command and edict became public knowledge and when many young women were gathered at the fortress of Susa under Hegai's supervision, Esther was taken to the palace, into the supervision of Hegai, keeper of the women. [9] The young woman pleased him and gained his favor so that he accelerated the process of the beauty treatments and the special diet that she received. He assigned seven hand-picked female servants to her from the palace and transferred her and her servants to the harem's best quarters. [10] Esther did not reveal her ethnicity or her family background, because Mordecai had ordered her not to make them known. [11] Every day Mordecai took a walk in front of the harem's courtyard to learn how Esther was doing and to see what was happening to her. [12] During the year before each young woman's turn to go to King Ahasuerus, the harem regulation required her to receive beauty treatments with oil of myrrh for six months and then with perfumes and cosmetics for another six months. [13] When the young woman would go to the king, she was given whatever she requested to take with her from the harem to the palace. [14] She would go in the evening, and in the morning she would return to a second harem under the supervision of the king's eunuch Shaashgaz, keeper of the concubines. She never went to the king again, unless he desired her and summoned her by name.

**Hiding Her Origin**

Hegai, the keeper of the king's harem, showed special favor to Esther. He provided her with attendants, beauty preparations, and a special diet. He also accelerated her preparations and gave her and her servants the best quarters in the house of women. Esther had found favor with the man responsible for preparing the young women who would be presented to King Ahasuerus.

We can see something of Esther's humble and obedient nature in the way she listened to Mordecai, the man who had raised her as his own daughter. She did not reveal her Jewish heritage or her family background because Mordecai had instructed her not to do so.

Esther had already experienced hardship in her young life. She was an orphan, having lost both her father and mother, and she belonged to the Jewish people living in a foreign land. We know that prejudice against the Jewish people would become an important part of this story, although Scripture does not tell us whether Esther had personally experienced such treatment at this point.

We are not told whether Mordecai was being guided by God's Spirit when he instructed Esther to keep her background a secret, or whether he understood something about the dangers and intrigues of the Persian court. We also do not know exactly what responsibilities Mordecai held that allowed him to remain near the palace. But we do know that he cared deeply for Esther. Every day, he walked near the courtyard of the harem to learn how she was doing.

There is something touching about this picture of Mordecai. Although Esther was no longer living under his roof, his concern for her had not diminished. He could not control everything happening within the palace, but he could remain close enough to learn about her welfare.

Prejudice and racism are cancers of the world and should never be tolerated within the body of Christ. We are taught in Scripture that God shows no favoritism. Romans 2:11 reminds us of this truth, and Acts 10:34–35 teaches that God does not judge people according to their nationality.

Through Jesus Christ, people from every nation and background are invited to come before God. It is not the color of our skin, our ancestry, or our position in society that makes us worthy of His love. We come to Him through faith in His Son, and a life transformed by His grace should reflect that faith through righteousness, love, and obedience.

I have never been one for beauty treatments, my skin having been toughened by years at sea. But what these young women experienced was certainly more than a day at the spa!

Before a young woman could be presented to King Ahasuerus, she was required to undergo twelve months of beauty preparations. For six months, she received treatments with oil of myrrh, followed by another six months with perfumes and cosmetics. This was the custom of the royal harem, and each young woman was required to complete the process.

When her turn came to go before the king, she could take whatever she requested from the harem to the palace. She would go to him in the evening and return the following morning to a second harem, where the king's concubines lived under the supervision of Shaashgaz. She would not return to the king unless he specifically summoned her by name.

We should remember that these young women had little control over the circumstances in which they found themselves. They had been gathered from throughout the kingdom under the king's command. Their future depended upon the decisions of a powerful ruler, and Scripture gives us no reason to assume that each young woman had willingly chosen this life.

Imagine spending an entire year preparing for a single meeting that could determine the course of your life. The young women could request clothing, adornments, or other items to take with them, hoping perhaps to make an impression upon the king. Yet only one would eventually be chosen as queen.

This is such a reflection of the way the world often operates. People are judged by their outward appearance, their wealth, their social position, or their ability to impress others. We sometimes feel that we must put on a certain face or present ourselves in a particular way to gain the approval of those around us.

But this is not how God measures the worth of a person.

In 1 Samuel 16:7, when Samuel was considering whom God had chosen to become king, the Lord reminded him that people look at outward appearances, but God looks at the heart. What impresses the world is not necessarily what pleases God.

A person may possess great beauty, wealth, and influence, yet have a heart filled with pride and selfishness. Another person may have little that the world considers valuable, yet possess a humble spirit, a generous heart, and a sincere love for God.

Jesus teaches us that the humble and meek are blessed. The kingdom of heaven is not a beauty contest in which we must compete for God's attention or approval. We are invited to come before Him through faith in Jesus Christ, not because of our outward appearance or accomplishments, but because of His grace.

There is another beautiful picture of this truth in Ephesians 5:25–27. Paul describes Christ's love for the church and how He gave Himself for her, preparing her to be presented in splendor, without spot or wrinkle.

What a contrast to the preparations taking place in the Persian palace!

The young women were being prepared with oils, perfumes, and cosmetics in the hope of pleasing an earthly king. But Christ prepares His church through His own sacrifice, cleansing and sanctifying those who belong to Him.

This beauty is not something we can purchase, apply to our skin, or achieve through our own efforts. It is the work of God's grace within us as the Holy Spirit continues to shape our lives into the image of His Son.

Esther's beauty brought her to the attention of those in the royal palace, but as we continue through her story, we will discover that her outward appearance was not what ultimately made her important in God's unfolding plan.

God would use her courage, wisdom, and willingness to act when the lives of His people were threatened.

The world may judge us by what it sees on the outside, but God knows what is within our hearts. May we spend less time seeking the approval of others and more time allowing Him to prepare us for the purposes He has placed before us.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Have you ever felt that you needed to change your appearance, behavior, or personality to gain the approval of others? How does knowing that God looks at the heart change the way you see yourself?

2. Are there times when you have judged someone by their appearance, nationality, background, or position in life? What can you do to show the love of Christ without favoritism?

3. What areas of your heart need God's attention today? Are you allowing the Holy Spirit to shape your character into something that reflects the beauty of Christ?

**Prayer**
