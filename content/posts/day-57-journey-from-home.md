**DAY 57 – JOURNEY FROM HOME**

**Scripture**

**Ruth 1:1-5 CSB**

During the time of the judges, there was a famine in the land. A man left Bethlehem in Judah with his wife and two sons to stay in the territory of Moab for a while. [2] The man's name was Elimelech, and his wife's name was Naomi. The names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites from Bethlehem in Judah. They entered the fields of Moab and settled there. [3] Naomi's husband, Elimelech, died, and she was left with her two sons. [4] Her sons took Moabite women as their wives: one was named Orpah and the second was named Ruth. After they lived in Moab about ten years, [5] both Mahlon and Chilion also died, and the woman was left without her two children and without her husband.

**1 Corinthians 7:39 CSB**

A wife is bound as long as her husband is living. But if her husband dies, she is free to be married to anyone she wants-only in the Lord.

**Family**

The beautiful and interesting story of Ruth begins during the time of the judges. This was a troubled time in the history of Israel, when the people repeatedly wandered away from God and returned to Him. We do not know much about this particular family when the story begins, but we know that their hometown was Bethlehem in Judah and that there was a famine in the land.

We do not know why God allowed the famine to occur, but Elimelech took his wife, Naomi, and their two sons to the land of Moab. Moab was located east of the Dead Sea in what is now Jordan. The Moabites were relatives of Israel, descended from Lot, Abraham's nephew, but the relationship between the two peoples was anything but friendly. During the time of Moses, Balak, king of Moab, hired Balaam to curse Israel. Later, at Baal-peor, Israelite men were drawn into sexual immorality and the worship of false gods.

Yet this is where Elimelech traveled with his family. We are not told whether leaving Bethlehem for Moab was an act of disobedience or simply a desperate decision by a man trying to provide for his family during a famine. We are also not told that Elimelech's death was divine judgment. All we know is that he died in Moab, leaving Naomi with her two sons, Mahlon and Chilion. Naomi, whose name means pleasantness or delight, was now a widow in a foreign land and dependent upon her sons.

God's law repeatedly warned Israel against relationships that would lead them away from Him and into the worship of other gods. Moab had a troubled history with Israel, including the events at Baal-peor. Yet Mahlon and Chilion married two Moabite women, Ruth and Orpah. Ruth does not tell us whether these particular marriages were acts of disobedience, so we should be careful not to say more than Scripture tells us. What becomes important as the story unfolds is the decision Ruth will eventually make concerning Naomi, the people of Israel, and their God.

After about ten years in Moab, Mahlon and Chilion also died. Naomi had now lost her husband and both of her sons. A widow without a husband or sons could be extremely vulnerable in the ancient world. Naomi was far from Bethlehem, surrounded by a foreign people, and much of the security she once had was gone. She was left with her two daughters-in-law, Ruth and Orpah, who were now widows themselves.

There is also a lesson here for us concerning the relationships we choose. Paul tells Christians that a believer who is free to marry may marry anyone they wish, but he adds the important words, “only in the Lord.” Paul does not tell us to separate ourselves completely from unbelievers. If we did that, how could we ever be a witness to the world? But marriage is different. It joins two lives together in one of the closest relationships we can have. When one person desires to follow Christ and the other does not share that faith, they can find themselves being pulled in different directions.

Paul also warns us not to be “yoked together with those who do not believe.” Although this passage is not speaking only about marriage, the principle is important. We need relationships with other believers who will encourage us in our faith rather than continually draw us away from God. At the same time, we are called to love those who do not believe, serve them, and show them the love of Christ.

But God gives more grace, as we will see throughout this story. We cannot say that Elimelech died because he left Bethlehem during the famine, nor can we say that the deaths of Mahlon and Chilion were God's judgment because they married Moabite women. Scripture does not tell us this. What we do know is that Naomi suddenly found herself in a desperate situation. Her husband and sons were gone, and she was living away from her homeland among a foreign people.

From Naomi's point of view, much of what had once given her security was gone. Yet what Naomi could not see was that God was already at work through one of the Moabite women standing beside her.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Do I have Christian friends in my life who encourage me to remain faithful to God, while I also continue to love and be a witness to those who do not believe?

2. If I am considering marriage, do I understand the importance of sharing my faith and commitment to Christ with the person I choose to marry?

3. When circumstances in my life seem to be falling apart, can I trust that God may still be working in ways that I cannot yet see?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Please give me the wisdom and strength to do Your will in this life instead of taking shortcuts to obtain the things I desire. When I am in trouble and distress, let me lift my needs to You in prayer and follow Your Word. Give me Christian friends who love You and whom I can lean on during difficult times. Help me to show that same love to other Christians who may feel abandoned and alone. When circumstances do not turn out as I hoped, help me not to assume that You have abandoned me. Teach me to trust You even when I cannot see what You are doing. Just as Naomi could not yet see what You were doing in her life, help me remember that I do not always know what You are preparing for tomorrow. In Jesus' name I pray.

Amen.
