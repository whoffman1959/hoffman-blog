**DAY 84 – NO FAVORTISM**

**Scripture**

**Job 34:19-21 CSB**

God is not partial to princes and does not favor the rich over the poor, for they are all the work of his hands. [20] They die suddenly in the middle of the night; people shudder, then pass away. Even the mighty are removed without effort. [21] For his eyes watch over a man's ways, and he observes all his steps.

**Ephesians 6:9 CSB**

And masters, treat your slaves the same way, without threatening them, because you know that both their Master and yours is in heaven, and there is no favoritism with him.

**Knows our steps**

The Word of God has a way of reaching deep into our souls, bringing to mind our own shortcomings and reminding us of what truly matters. I sometimes stand in awe when I consider how much effort we put into getting ahead in life. We work long hours, pursue degrees, and strive to achieve success. Yet none of these accomplishments makes us more valuable in the eyes of God. It is not the fine car we drive, the impressive title we hold, or the beautiful person standing beside us that matters most to Him. God looks beyond these things to the condition of our hearts and the way we treat others.

God does not show favoritism. Peter came to understand this when the Lord sent him to the home of Cornelius, a Roman centurion, as recorded in Acts chapter 10. Cornelius was a devout man who feared God, prayed regularly, and generously helped those in need. One day, an angel appeared to him and instructed him to send for Peter.

At that time, Jewish customs generally discouraged close association with Gentiles, particularly entering their homes and sharing meals. But God was about to teach Peter an important lesson. Through a vision of animals being lowered from heaven, Peter was told not to call impure what God had made clean. When the men sent by Cornelius arrived, the Holy Spirit directed Peter to accompany them. Peter began to understand that God was teaching him not to regard any person as unclean or unworthy of hearing the gospel.

When Peter arrived at Cornelius's home, he found a household gathered and eager to hear God's message. Peter proclaimed the good news of Jesus Christ, His death and resurrection, and the forgiveness available to everyone who believes in Him. While Peter was still speaking, the Holy Spirit came upon those who heard the message. God had made it clear that His gift of salvation was not limited by nationality, social standing, or background.

Today’s passage from Job reminds us that God does not favor princes over the poor because all are the work of His hands. The powerful ruler and the person begging for food on a street corner are equally accountable before Him. Our titles do not earn His favor, nor does our education or knowledge of Scripture make us more deserving of His love. We may spend years studying the Bible, learning ancient languages, and understanding its history, but what good is that knowledge if we fail to demonstrate the love of Christ in our daily lives?

Paul makes this point beautifully in 1 Corinthians 13:1–3. He explains that even if we possess extraordinary knowledge, remarkable faith, or the ability to speak with great eloquence, without love we gain nothing. Even great acts of generosity or service are of no spiritual gain to us when they are done without love. What matters is not simply what we know or accomplish, but whether the love of Christ is reflected in the way we live.

One day, each of us will stand before God and give an account of our lives. How precious it is to know that Jesus Christ paid the price for our sins through His sacrifice on the cross. We cannot purchase forgiveness through good works or earn salvation through personal accomplishments. We receive salvation by God's grace through faith in His Son.

Yet this wonderful gift does not excuse us from the responsibility of how we live and treat others. Our actions still have consequences, and we are called to reflect the love and compassion of Christ in our daily lives. Having received such great mercy from our Heavenly Father, how can we withhold that same mercy from others?

When we encounter someone whose circumstances, appearance, or background differ from our own, we should remember that God sees a person created by His hands. Rather than looking down on others or judging them by worldly standards, we are called to be ambassadors for Christ, extending kindness, compassion, and love.

The world may measure people by what they possess or what they have achieved. Our Heavenly Father calls us to something far greater: to love Him and to love one another.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Have you ever judged someone by their appearance, wealth, education, or position in life? How does knowing that God shows no favoritism change the way you see others?

2. Peter learned that God's message of salvation was for Gentiles as well as Jews. Are there people in your life whom you have overlooked or hesitated to approach with the love of Christ?

3. Paul reminds us that even great knowledge and accomplishments mean nothing without love. How can you demonstrate God's love through your words and actions today?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for loving us without regard to our wealth, position, or accomplishments. Forgive us when we judge others by outward appearances or allow pride to influence our hearts. Remind us that every person is created by Your hands and is deserving of kindness and compassion. Thank You for sending Your Son, Jesus Christ, to bring forgiveness and salvation to all who believe in Him. Help us to use the knowledge, abilities, and blessings You have given us to serve others rather than ourselves. Teach us to love without favoritism and to reflect the humility and compassion of Christ in everything we do. In Jesus' precious name we pray.

Amen.
