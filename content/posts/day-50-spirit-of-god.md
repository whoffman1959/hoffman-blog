**DAY 50 – SPIRIT OF GOD**

**Scripture**

**1 John 4:1-6 CSB**

Dear friends, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see if they are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world. [2] This is how you know the Spirit of God: Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God, [3] but every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not from God. This is the spirit of the antichrist, which you have heard is coming; even now it is already in the world. [4] You are from God, little children, and you have conquered them, because the one who is in you is greater than the one who is in the world. [5] They are from the world. Therefore what they say is from the world, and the world listens to them. [6] We are from God. Anyone who knows God listens to us; anyone who is not from God does not listen to us. This is how we know the Spirit of truth and the spirit of deception.

**Testing Truth**

There was an early teaching that later became known as Docetism. The name comes from the Greek word dokein, meaning “to seem” or “to appear.” Those who held this belief taught that Jesus only seemed to have a genuine human body and did not truly suffer and die as a man. This went against the truth that Jesus truly came in the flesh, suffered on the cross, and died for our sins.

Why would anyone deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh? Some early teachings were influenced by the idea that the physical world was inherently evil. If physical matter was evil, they reasoned, then Jesus could not truly have taken on a human body. He must have been a spiritual being who only appeared to be human, making His suffering and death on the cross an illusion—a fake. We do not know whether John’s warning was directed at those who held an early form of Docetism, another developing false teaching, or a group we no longer know about. But his warning is clear: to deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh is to deny a foundational truth of the gospel.

The apostle John walked with Jesus and was taught by Him. He speaks plainly that those who confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh are from God. Those who deny this are teaching the very spirit of the antichrist.
Those who hold on to the truth that Jesus was born as a human infant of the virgin Mary and lived as we live, but without sin, are holding to a foundational truth of the gospel. This is the faith that conquers the world. Peter said it plainly: “You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God.” This baby, born in the flesh and yet by very nature God, faced the temptations of this world that we often fail to overcome, yet He remained without sin. He became the perfect, sinless sacrifice for our sins, offering salvation to the world. That same sacrifice continues to bear fruit today.

John declares that we are from God and have overcome these false teachings because the One who is in us is greater than the one who is in the world. There were false teachers then, and there are false teachers now. John calls us to test what we hear and not simply accept every spiritual claim as truth. The spirit of the antichrist denies the truth about Jesus Christ and that He truly came in the flesh.

John reinforces a truth that is at the heart of Christian belief. Jesus was truly human, but John also teaches elsewhere that He is truly God, the one and only Son of God. How Jesus can be fully God and fully man is a mystery that is beyond our complete understanding. The same is true of the Trinity: one God—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—yet three persons. In the centuries after the apostles, false teachings arose that denied important truths about who Jesus is. The Nicene Creed was developed by the early church to clearly state these foundational beliefs and defend them against false teaching. Today, Christians in many denominations still confess these truths together. Jesus Christ truly came in the flesh, truly suffered for us, and gave His life so that we might be saved.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Do I understand the depth of the love that the Father and Jesus have for us—the Father sending His Son and Jesus willingly enduring the suffering of the cross?

2. Are there teachings today that deny who Jesus is or what He did on the cross for us?

3. Do I test the teachings I hear against the truth about Jesus found in Scripture?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Always guide Your church in the truth. Let the gospel of Christ be kept pure. Do not allow us to explain mysteries by developing untruths from our own imaginations. May we know in our innermost being the depth of Your love for us in giving Your Son for our sins, and the love of Jesus, who willingly gave His life for us. Help us to test what we hear and always stand firm in the truth of the gospel. In Jesus’ name we pray.

Amen.
