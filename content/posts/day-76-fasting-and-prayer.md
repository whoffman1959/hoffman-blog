**DAY 76 – FASTING AND PRAYER**

**Scripture**

**Esther 4:9-17 CSB**

Hathach came and repeated Mordecai's response to Esther. [10] Esther spoke to Hathach and commanded him to tell Mordecai, [11] "All the royal officials and the people of the royal provinces know that one law applies to every man or woman who approaches the king in the inner courtyard and who has not been summoned-the death penalty-unless the king extends the gold scepter, allowing that person to live. I have not been summoned to appear before the king for the last thirty days." [12] Esther's response was reported to Mordecai. [13] Mordecai told the messenger to reply to Esther, "Don't think that you will escape the fate of all the Jews because you are in the king's palace. [14] If you keep silent at this time, relief and deliverance will come to the Jewish people from another place, but you and your father's family will be destroyed. Who knows, perhaps you have come to your royal position for such a time as this." [15] Esther sent this reply to Mordecai: [16] "Go and assemble all the Jews who can be found in Susa and fast for me. Don't eat or drink for three days, night or day. I and my female servants will also fast in the same way. After that, I will go to the king even if it is against the law. If I perish, I perish." [17] So Mordecai went and did everything Esther had commanded him.

**Do not keep Silent**

Sometimes in life, we need to be reminded of who we are and how precious we are in the eyes of God. If we were to calculate the value of the basic chemical elements that make up the human body, some estimates place their raw material cost at only a few dollars. Yet in God's eyes, we are worth far more than the sum of those elements. We are created in His image, and He loves us with a love far greater than we can fully understand.

Before Jesus ascended into heaven, He promised His disciples that they would receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. As believers, we have that same Spirit dwelling within us today. The apostle Paul describes our earthly bodies as jars of clay, yet within these fragile vessels we carry a treasure that comes from God.

We may be weak in our own strength, but we belong to the One who created us, redeemed us, and calls us to live according to His purpose.

The Jewish people had also been set apart by God. He had chosen Israel to be His people, and throughout their history, He had demonstrated His faithfulness to them, even during times of hardship and captivity.

Some have pointed out that the book of Esther never directly mentions God by name. Yet as we read through its pages, we can see His providence unfolding through the circumstances and decisions of those involved. What may appear to be ordinary events can become part of something far greater than anyone could have imagined.

We see this in Mordecai's response to Esther.

Esther had learned of Haman's decree ordering the destruction of the Jewish people. Mordecai had asked her to approach King Ahasuerus and plead for their lives, but she knew the danger involved. Anyone who entered the king's inner courtyard without being summoned faced the death penalty unless the king extended his golden scepter.

Esther had not been called into the king's presence for thirty days. Imagine the uncertainty she must have felt. She was queen, yet even her position did not guarantee her safety. Approaching the king could mean losing her life before she had an opportunity to speak.

Mordecai reminded Esther that remaining silent would not necessarily protect her. She was Jewish, and the decree threatened her people throughout the empire, including those within the palace.

Then Mordecai expressed his confidence that deliverance would come to the Jewish people, even if Esther refused to act. But he also gave her a serious warning. She should not believe that her position in the palace would protect her, for if she remained silent, she and her father's family would perish. Mordecai believed that her position as queen might have a greater purpose than she understood. Perhaps God had placed her there for such a time as this.

What faith Mordecai demonstrated! He could not see the future, and he did not know how deliverance would come. Yet his words reflected a confidence that the Jewish people would not be abandoned to destruction.

Esther now faced a decision that required more than courage. She needed wisdom, strength, and a willingness to place the needs of her people above her own safety.

Her response was remarkable.

She instructed Mordecai to gather all the Jewish people in Susa and have them fast on her behalf. They were not to eat or drink for three days, night or day. Esther and her female servants would also fast. Afterward, she would approach the king, even though doing so was against the law. And if she perished, she perished. Esther understood the danger, but she was willing to act.

Fasting was an important spiritual practice among God's people. It was often associated with times of mourning, repentance, distress, and seeking God's help. By setting aside the ordinary needs of the body, people could devote their attention more fully to the seriousness of their circumstances and their dependence upon God.

There are different examples of fasting throughout Scripture. Some involved abstaining from food for a period of time while continuing to drink water. In Daniel chapter 1, we read how Daniel and his companions refused the king's food and wine because Daniel did not want to defile himself. Later, in Daniel chapter 10, we see Daniel abstaining from rich foods, meat, and wine during three weeks of mourning. Other fasts, such as the one Esther requested, involved abstaining from both food and water for a limited period.

Scripture also teaches about temporarily abstaining from marital relations by mutual agreement for the purpose of prayer, as Paul explains in 1 Corinthians chapter 7. Although this is not the same as fasting from food, it reflects the principle of setting aside something normally enjoyed in order to devote special attention to prayer.

Fasting is not simply going without food or giving something up. Its spiritual purpose is to humble ourselves before God, seek His guidance, and recognize our dependence upon Him.

Fasting is often accompanied by heartfelt prayer, as we humble ourselves before God and seek His guidance, mercy, and help. It is more than simply going without food or drink. It is a time of drawing closer to our heavenly Father, placing our concerns before Him, and trusting in His wisdom and care. Esther's request for the Jewish people to fast reflected the seriousness of their circumstances and their need for help beyond their own strength.

As I look back over my own life, I can remember many times when I faced problems that seemed impossible to overcome. Yet instead of turning to God first, I continued trying to find a way through or around those obstacles by relying upon my own strength and abilities.

Looking back, I can see how foolish that was. Why did I not spend more time with my heavenly Father, lifting my concerns to Him in prayer? Why did I struggle so hard to control circumstances that were beyond my ability to change?

We have been given a wonderful privilege through Jesus Christ. We can come before our heavenly Father in prayer, knowing that His Son intercedes for us and that the Holy Spirit helps us in our weakness.

Prayer should not be something we turn to only after every other effort has failed. It should be a part of our daily walk with God, in times of joy and thanksgiving as well as in times of trouble and uncertainty.

Esther's situation also gives us another beautiful picture of our relationship with God.

Under Persian law, no man or woman could simply enter the king's inner courtyard without being summoned. To do so meant facing death unless the king extended his golden scepter and granted permission to approach. Esther needed the king's favor before she could safely come into his presence.

Now consider our own position before a holy and righteous God.

Scripture teaches that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God. Sin separates us from God, and we cannot earn our way into His presence through our own goodness or accomplishments.

But our heavenly Father has done something wonderful for us. Through His Son, Jesus Christ, He has provided the way for our sins to be forgiven and for us to be reconciled to Him.

Unlike Esther, who had to approach an earthly king without knowing whether she would be received, we have an invitation to approach God's throne of grace with confidence through faith in Jesus Christ.

The writer of Hebrews reminds us that we can draw near to that throne to receive mercy and find grace to help us in our time of need. What an extraordinary privilege! We do not enter God's presence because of our own righteousness. We come through Jesus Christ, whose blood was shed upon the cross for the forgiveness of our sins.

How great is the love of our heavenly Father, who sent His Son so that we might receive eternal life! And how great is the love of Jesus, who willingly gave His life to save us!

In a small but meaningful way, we see a reflection of sacrificial love in Esther.
She was willing to risk her own life for the sake of her people. She could have remained silent and hoped that her position within the palace would protect her. Instead, she chose to stand with those who were facing destruction.

Of course, Esther could not provide the salvation that only Jesus Christ can give. Her sacrifice was a willingness to risk her life, while Jesus knowingly and willingly gave His life to redeem us from sin.

Yet her courage reminds us that love sometimes calls us to place the needs of others above our own comfort and safety.

Esther did not know what would happen when she approached the king. She could only prepare herself, ask her people to fast with her, and move forward with courage.

There will also be times in our lives when we must make difficult decisions without knowing how things will turn out. We may face situations that require courage, patience, sacrifice, or the willingness to speak when remaining silent would be easier.

During those times, we need to remember that we are not alone. We have a heavenly Father who hears our prayers, a Savior who intercedes for us, and the Holy Spirit who helps us in our weakness. We do not need to wait until we have exhausted our own strength before turning to God.

We can come to Him today.

And when the time comes for us to act, we can move forward in faith, trusting that His wisdom is greater than our understanding and that His love will never fail us.

**Reflection Questions**

1. Can you think of someone who has made a great sacrifice for another person or a group of people? How does that sacrifice remind you of the love Jesus demonstrated through His death upon the cross?

2. Is there a situation in your life, or in the life of someone you love, that you need to bring before God through prayer and perhaps a period of fasting?

3. What are some ways you can set aside time for fasting and prayer, drawing closer to God and seeking His guidance? Are you making prayer a daily part of your life, or do you tend to wait until difficulties arise?
