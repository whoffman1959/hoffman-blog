**DAY 34 – SPEAKING THE TRUTH**

**Scripture**

**2 Peter 2:1-3 CSB**

There were indeed false prophets among the people, just as there will be false teachers among you. They will bring in destructive heresies, even denying the Master who bought them, and will bring swift destruction on themselves. [2] Many will follow their depraved ways, and the way of truth will be maligned because of them. [3] They will exploit you in their greed with made-up stories. Their condemnation, pronounced long ago, is not idle, and their destruction does not sleep.

**1 John 2:27  CSB**

As for you, the anointing you received from him remains in you, and you don't need anyone to teach you. Instead, his anointing teaches you about all things and is true and is not a lie; just as it has taught you, remain in him.

**Beware False Prophets**

Peter lays a strong foundation concerning false teaching. He reminds us that there were false prophets among God's people in the past, just as false teachers would arise within the Christian community. These false teachers would bring destructive heresies, denying even the Lord. They would exploit believers through greed and lead others into depraved ways. Later in Peter's letter, we also find scoffers questioning Christ's promised return.

There are other concrete examples given in the New Testament.

In 2 Timothy 2:17–18, we find that Hymenaeus and Philetus taught falsely that the resurrection had already taken place. Through their false teaching, they were ruining the faith of some.

The Nicolaitans appear in Revelation chapter 2. We do not know much about them historically beyond what Scripture tells us, but Jesus clearly condemns their teaching. In this same chapter of Revelation, we are told that some in the church held to the teaching of Balaam. Balaam taught Balak to place a stumbling block before the Israelites, leading them into idolatry and sexual immorality. The account of Balaam and Balak can be found in the book of Numbers, beginning in chapter 22.

This brings us to a woman Jesus condemns in Revelation chapter 2. Jesus calls her Jezebel, and she claimed to be a prophetess. She was misleading believers into sexual immorality and eating food sacrificed to idols. Whether Jezebel was her literal name or a symbolic designation is uncertain. What we do know is that Jesus' eyes are on His church, and He called those involved in this sin to repent before judgment came upon them.

In the same chapter of Revelation, Jesus commends the church at Ephesus for testing people who claimed to be apostles and discovering that they were false. This is particularly instructive for us. The church and the believers within it are not instructed to simply accept everyone who claims to have spiritual authority. What they teach must be tested against the Word of God.

In Acts 20:29–30, Paul warns the Ephesian elders that “savage wolves” would come among them and that men would even arise from among themselves and distort the truth to draw disciples after them. This is remarkably close to what Peter warns about in our reflection today.

When I first came to truly believe and rely on the Lord, it was a wonderful time, but this quickly changed when a person from a Christian cult tried to convince me that I was not saved and that I had to belong to their church to share in the promises of God. I was too young in the faith to recognize what was happening and was isolated from other believers because I was aboard ship much of the time. This person not only had spiritual influence over me but was also someone I worked for.

I spent hours studying the Bible whenever I could. Over the years, as I matured in my faith, I came to understand that this person was part of a cult and that the things he was teaching me were false. Some of the same warning signs can be found in other cults that try to draw Christians away from the truth. This is when I learned how important it is to know God's Word. The Holy Bible contains the truth by which I can test what others teach. I cannot do this in my own wisdom alone. I need the Holy Spirit working in me to teach and guide me.

One warning sign of false teaching can be a denial of who Jesus truly is. Some may acknowledge Jesus as a prophet or a great teacher but deny that He is truly God. Others have taught that Jesus only appeared to be human and therefore did not truly suffer and die on the cross. Scripture teaches us that Jesus is truly God and truly man. He is the Son of God who came in the flesh, died on the cross for our sins, and rose from the dead.

Other warning signs may include leaders who lord their authority over people, lead others into sexual immorality, or exploit those who trust them for financial gain. A false teacher may seek wealth and power while continually demanding more from those caught in their web. Some have even lifted themselves up as though they were Christ, denying the Lord Himself. The list of possible deceptions goes on.

How can we recognize a false teacher or false church? First, we must remain in Christ and rely upon the Holy Spirit, allowing Him to guide us into truth. By spending time in the Word of God and in prayer, daily if possible, we grow in our knowledge of the Father and the Son and become better prepared to weigh what someone teaches against Scripture.

This does not mean that we have no need for Christian teachers. God has given teachers to His church, along with elders and other mature believers who help build up and protect the body of Christ. Rather, it means that no human teacher is above the Word of God. We test what we are taught and hold firmly to what is true.

The early church understood this danger. After the time of the apostles, church leaders met to defend foundational Christian truths when false teaching arose. The creeds that developed from these early councils, including the Nicene Creed, helped clearly state foundational Christian beliefs concerning the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. These statements did not replace Scripture but helped Christians clearly express what Scripture teaches and guard against falsehood.

**Reflection Questions**

1.	Do I spend enough time in Bible study and prayer to rightly determine whether what someone tells me is biblical truth? If I am confused, do I ask God to open my eyes to the truth?

2.	Have there been times in my life when a false teacher or the teachings of the world tried to draw me away from the truth about Jesus and His death on the cross for my sins?

3.	Am I a new believer who needs to be discipled by a mature Christian? Or, as a believer who has been taught and strengthened by the Spirit, am I willing to serve Christians who are young in the faith—not lording over them, but serving them as Jesus teaches in Matthew chapter 20?
