**DAY 10 – BLESSED ARE THE POOR**

**Scripture**

**James 2:5 (CSB)**

"Listen, my dear brothers and sisters: Didn’t God choose the poor in this world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom that he has promised to those who love him?"

**Poor in Spirit**

My wife often reminds me that I enjoy the finer things in life a little too much. I’m not always satisfied with the old car making strange noises, and I remember the days when I drove something shiny with that new-car smell. I wish my waistline looked like it did in my twenties, when I felt strong and confident. I think back to the seasons when I was important in business, when people relied on my expertise. Now I’m retired, older, and cannot work. This itself has been a lesson.

The truth is, no one is better than another. Every one of us is made in the image of God. Every one of us is valuable—not because of what we own, how we look, or what titles we hold, but because of the Father who created us.

James reminds the Church not to show favoritism toward those who seem wealthy or impressive. These things aren’t wrong in themselves, but they become dangerous when they determine someone’s value. In the early church, the poor were often overlooked, treated as less important, and even pushed aside during gatherings. James boldly exposed this as sin—a direct violation of love.

Jesus brings us back to the heart of the matter:
“Blessed are the poor in spirit.”

To be poor in spirit is to recognize our complete dependence on God. It is the humility that says, “Father, without You I have nothing—and without You, I am nothing.”

There is also another kind of “poor”—those who live with real needs: financial, emotional, or physical. Many who lack earthly resources often have a deeper awareness of their need for God. According to Jesus and James, these believers are often the richest—because their faith is uncluttered by pride or self-sufficiency.

It isn’t wrong to have wealth or influence. But it is dangerous to trust in them. True blessing belongs to those who, whether rich or poor, live with open hands—trusting God as their source and treating every person with equal grace and dignity.

**Reflection Questions**

1. What does it mean for me personally to be “poor in spirit”?

2. Are there areas of my life where I depend more on my own strength or status than on God?

3. How can I show Christlike love and equality toward others, regardless of wealth or position?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for teaching me that true riches are found in You alone. Help me to stay humble, dependent, and aware of my constant need for Your grace. Keep my heart free from pride or favoritism, and teach me to see every person as You see them—precious and loved. When I am in need, remind me of Your provision; when I have plenty, remind me to share. Make me rich in faith, gentle in spirit, and full of love for all Your children.

Amen.
