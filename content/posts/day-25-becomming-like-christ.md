**DAY 25 – BECOMING LIKE CHRIST**

**Scripture**

**1 Peter 4:1–6 CSB**

Therefore, since Christ suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves also with the same understanding—because the one who suffers in the flesh is finished with sin—[2] in order to live the remaining time in the flesh no longer for human desires, but for God's will. [3] For there has already been enough time spent in doing what the Gentiles choose to do: carrying on in unrestrained behavior, evil desires, drunkenness, orgies, carousing, and lawless idolatry. [4] They are surprised that you don't join them in the same flood of wild living—and they slander you. [5] They will give an account to the one who stands ready to judge the living and the dead. [6] For this reason the gospel was also preached to those who are now dead, so that, although they might be judged in the flesh according to human standards, they might live in the spirit according to God's standards.

**Not Living for Self**

If we look at our lives, it is not the big moments that define us. Many will not have statues or books written about us when we pass from this plane of existence. What follows us is how we interact with people on a daily basis. It is the small things given out of love.

Our battle in life is not so much on the outside, but instead within. It is the age-old struggle of who is going to win: will we make ourselves god and try to rule our own lives, or will we submit to the kingship of Jesus and be led by the Holy Spirit daily?

This really comes into play when we try to make relationships, our possessions, or our standing in the community the most important part of our lives instead of ways that we might serve God. It is true that sin is in this world, and even the best of us will disappoint others and ourselves almost daily. The job we fought for might not be as glamorous as we first thought, or that once-new car that caught our eye with dreams has seen better days. We fight with those we love over minor things that we often forget about why we fought in a few days. When these things happen, they serve as a stark reminder that we are living for self. This selfishness often wins, and we become less concerned about the welfare of others. What is the best way to get out of self? It is by making a conscious effort to be of humble service to others.

This could mean giving quietly, starting the day with a smile for our spouse, or offering a co-worker praise. Life does not need to be all about us. The Bible and prayer become our reflection, bringing us back to what is important: serving our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. This service does not need to be glamorous; it is found in simple acts that do not draw attention to ourselves. We are reminded to live for others.

Many of us struggle with the thoughts that run through our heads—what is often called stinking thinking. We constantly dwell on the ways we have been wronged or carry the guilt of wronging others. These selfish thoughts drive us farther away from God, but He gives more grace. We are forgiven in His beloved Son if Jesus is truly Lord of our lives. By allowing the Father to work in us through the Holy Spirit, we replace those burdens with a focus on helping others that flowers into action. When we step outside ourselves to help others, the chaotic, selfish mind is replaced by the needs of those around us, and we find peace in the Lord.

**Reflection Questions**

1. What can I do for someone else today without calling attention to myself?
2. How do I envision a life fully surrendered to Christ, and where do my daily habits fall short of that standard?
3. Am I harboring anger, bitterness, or wrath today, and what steps do I need to take to make amends and extend forgiveness?

**Prayer**

Heavenly Father,

Thank you for giving us your word to act as a mirror for our souls. Give us a clear sight of the times when we are selfish. Forgive us when we replace your will with our own desires and demands for recognition. Let our thoughts be on how we can help others in this life and truly reflect your Son in how we live. Give us a desire to spend time with you in prayer to receive the power to live as we should, confessing our sins to you. Guide us through your Holy Spirit to make the proper decisions in life to positively affect others. In all these things we pray in the name of Jesus.

Amen.
